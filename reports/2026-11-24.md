#### 🔮 Separable equations of motion for spinning test bodies in spacetimes with Killing--Yano symmetry

Seenivasan, Ramond, Isoyama, Dolan — [arXiv:2611.23401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23401) — gr-qc

🌟 **Must-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6/10 · 🎯 Relevance 6/10

> 💡 To first order in its spin, a small spinning body moving in any spacetime with the same hidden symmetry as a rotating black hole behaves like a charged, non-spinning particle pushed by an effective force field built from the curvature, and its motion can be written down in closed form.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper takes the worldline-shift trick that Skoupý and Witzany found for Kerr, where moving the reference worldline of a spinning test body turns its linear-in-spin motion into a geodesic with shifted constants, and rebuilds it covariantly from the Killing-Yano tensor, so that only a non-degenerate Killing-Yano tensor is assumed and no field equations. After the shift, the body follows the worldline of a fictitious spinless particle whose charge is set by the spin component along the orbital angular momentum. That particle obeys a Lorentz-force equation with an effective field strength F = f* . Ricci (the dual Killing-Yano tensor contracted with the Ricci tensor), which is shown to be closed and which vanishes in Ricci-flat spacetimes. In the general off-shell canonical metric (the Kerr-NUT-(A)dS-Newman family with arbitrary metric functions), the authors give explicitly separated first-order equations of motion, reproduce the Kerr results, and work through Kerr-de Sitter, where F = Lambda f*.

✅ **Strengths**

- Fully covariant construction: the shift vectors are fixed by the Killing-Yano geometry (f* and f contracted with the Marck tetrad legs) rather than by coordinate-specific guesswork, and the paper states they reduce to the Skoupý-Witzany expressions in Kerr.
- The spin-as-charge picture is a clean structural result: F = f* . Ric is shown to be antisymmetric and closed, so it is a legitimate field strength, and its source current is given explicitly. The paper also correctly notes that this is not the Maxwell field, since in Kerr-Newman it is even in the charge.
- Separated equations are given for arbitrary Delta(r) and Theta(y) in the off-shell canonical metric, and the Lorentz-force equation is checked symbolically for arbitrary metric functions, with a separate covariant proof in an appendix.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Separability itself was already proven by Witzany and Skoupý (2026) through the Hamilton-Jacobi equation; what is new here is the explicit worldline shift and the spin-as-charge picture.
- Applies only to spacetimes with a full Killing-Yano tensor, which excludes most of the beyond-Kerr metrics used in EMRI tests of general relativity.
- Validation is symbolic only: there is no comparison with direct numerical integration and no orbit, flux or waveform computation.
- The Schwarzschild-like limit (zero black-hole spin) lies outside the coordinate domain used and is handled only in a footnote.

📖 **Where to start.** Section 1.3 (summary of results, self-contained); Section 3.1 (how the shift is fixed by the Killing-Yano tensor); Section 4.1 (the effective gauge field F = f* . Ric and its properties); Section 4.3 (Kerr-de Sitter worked example). Skip the long Appendix C unless you want to check the covariant proof of the Lorentz-force equation.

#### ☁️ A Relativistic Newton's Third Law for Binaries in Environments

Dyson, Pound, D'Orazio, Meent et al. — [arXiv:2611.27442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27442) — gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

🌟 **Must-Read** · 🔬 Quality 7/10 · 🎯 Relevance 8/10

> 💡 A first-principles derivation shows how the energy and angular momentum that a small body orbiting a black hole loses to surrounding gas or dark matter can be computed from the force it exerts on that matter, plus a correction from gravitational waves emitted jointly by the body and the matter, which earlier work either guessed at or got wrong.

🔍 **What it does.** Working in the two-parameter (mass ratio times environment density) expansion around Kerr, the authors use conservation of the secondary's stress-energy and retarded/advanced Green's-function reciprocity to rewrite the environment-induced rate of change of the secondary's Killing charges (energy, axial angular momentum) as an integral of the matter stress-energy against the point-particle metric perturbation. The instantaneous form pairs the matter's past history with a time-localised advanced particle field; the orbit-averaged form splits into the retarded-field work done on the matter plus a cross-term gravitational-wave flux (matter x particle) at infinity and the horizon. They then give two usable versions: a leading-order (0PA) version for dynamical environments that can be fed directly from matter simulations, and an order mass-ratio-squared-times-density version for environments that are stationary and axisymmetric at leading order, which needs only the matter response, the vacuum point-particle perturbation, and the environment's leading metric perturbation. No mixed-order gravitational perturbation has to be solved for. The construction is also carried over to comparable-mass binaries in flat-space-at-leading-order settings in an appendix.

✅ **Strengths**

- Addresses a real and widely acknowledged gap: EMRI-environment papers have been closing the loop with assumed balance laws, and this gives a derivation from conservation of the secondary's stress-energy.
- Practical payoff is clear: the forcing functions need only the matter perturbation, the vacuum Kerr point-particle perturbation (available from public self-force codes), and the leading environment metric perturbation. No mixed-order metric perturbation solve is needed.
- Makes the physical point explicit that asymptotic matter fluxes are not equal to the local work on the secondary when the environment can store or dissipate energy (viscosity, cooling, bound states).
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Formal derivation only; no numerical example and no estimate of how big the new correction terms are.
- Energy and axial angular momentum only; Carter constant not covered, so inclined orbits are incomplete.
- Subleading-order result assumes the matter perturbation stays finite at the small body, which excludes accretion and singular wakes.
- The 'corrected' torque prescription includes the lead author's own earlier paper.

**In network:** 🚩 Adam Pound (extended-network collaborator), 🚩 Maarten van de Meent (extended-network collaborator)

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. 2.2 (summary of results, with the key equations in one place); Sec. 4.2 (orbit-averaged Third Law and the mixed-flux identification); Sec. 5.3.1-5.3.3 (bootstrapping to the stationary-axisymmetric subleading order, and the discussion of which earlier prescriptions are wrong and why). Sec. 4.1 only if you care about the instantaneous, causal form; Appendix on post-Minkowskian binaries if you work on gas-embedded massive black hole binaries.

#### ♻️ Post-Newtonian expansion of the energy and angular momentum flux radiated to infinity: Eccentric-equatorial orbits about a Kerr black hole

Castillo, Munna, Nasipak, Brown et al. — [arXiv:2611.30437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30437) — gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

🌟 **Must-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 7/10

> 💡 Closed-form weak-field formulas for the energy and angular momentum that gravitational waves carry away from a small body on an eccentric orbit in the equatorial plane of a spinning black hole are pushed to much higher order than before, and at large separations they match numerical results well enough to stand in for the numerical tables.

🔍 **What it does.** The authors use the Mano-Suzuki-Takasugi analytic solutions of the Teukolsky equation, expanded symbolically in Mathematica, to compute the orbit-averaged energy and angular momentum fluxes to infinity for a point mass on an eccentric-equatorial Kerr geodesic. The results are a double series to 10PN in 1/p and to e^20 in eccentricity, and they are exact in the spin a. Two independent codes (s=+2 and s=-2) agree through 8PN and e^20, and the results reduce correctly to earlier Schwarzschild and circular-equatorial results. They find that the leading-spin (q^n) coefficients resum to closed forms in e up to 6PN in the energy flux and nearly through 10PN in the angular momentum flux. Against pybhpt Teukolsky data at a=0.9M, the 10PN energy flux reaches relative error ~1e-7 (the accuracy of the FastEMRIWaveforms flux grid) for p of about 20 at e=0.1, about 35 at e=0.5 and about 75 at e=0.7. Beyond that point the e^20 truncation, not the PN order, limits the accuracy.

✅ **Strengths**

- Independent cross-check: two separate Mathematica codes built on opposite-spin-weight Teukolsky functions agree exactly through 8PN and e^20, and the results recover the Schwarzschild and circular-equatorial limits.
- The validation is honest and quantitative. It compares against both the interpolated FastEMRIWaveforms grid and higher-accuracy discrete pybhpt data (agreement ~1e-13 at p=200, e=0.1), and it separates PN truncation error from eccentricity truncation error, including an explicit fitted error model at e=0.9.
- Practically useful: it states where in (p,e) the series beats current flux-grid accuracy, and the full expressions are released in public repositories (Black Hole Perturbation Toolkit and others).
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Fluxes to infinity only; horizon fluxes are not included, so these cannot yet replace full Teukolsky fluxes in an inspiral.
- Equatorial orbits only; generic Kerr orbits are already known to 6PN and e^16 from another group.
- Numerically validated essentially only at spin a=0.9M.
- For eccentricity around 0.7 and above, the e^20 truncation, not the PN order, limits accuracy; it is unreliable close to the separatrix.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Introduction (positioning and the two-code cross-check); Sec. III.A (structure and spin decomposition of the flux); Sec. III.C (leading-spin resummations); Sec. #### 🌀 Dynamics of a spinning body with scalar multipoles

Lestingi, Sotiriou, Spiers, Maselli — [arXiv:2611.23426](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23426) — gr-qc, astro-ph.HE, hep-th

🌟 **Must-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6/10 · 🎯 Relevance 7/10

> 💡 For a small spinning body that carries a scalar charge and spirals into a much heavier black hole, the paper derives the complete equations of motion needed for next-to-leading-order waveforms, and finds that only the body's mass, spin, scalar charge and one extra constant matter, while a parity-odd (axion-like) scalar leaves the motion identical to general relativity at that order.

🔍 **What it does.** The authors build a worldline action for a spinning compact body coupled to a massless scalar, extending the Porto-style spinning-particle action by letting the Lagrangian depend on the scalar field and its gradient, and read off the body's scalar monopole and dipole from it. Varying the action gives modified Mathisson-Papapetrou-Dixon equations with a scalar force and a spin torque driven by the scalar gradient, plus the sourced scalar and Einstein equations. They then power-count everything in the mass ratio for extreme-mass-ratio inspirals and keep terms through first post-adiabatic order. For an ordinary (parity-even) scalar, the 1PA dynamics needs only the scalar charge Q0 and a quadratic self-coupling constant; the scalar dipole only enters at 2PA. The only genuinely new 1PA pieces are spin-scalar couplings proportional to Q0, entering the spin precession and the acceleration. For a pseudoscalar (dynamical Chern-Simons-like), symmetry forbids a monopole, the dipole is proportional to spin, the field comes out at order mass ratio squared, and the dynamics matches GR through 1PA.

✅ **Strengths**

- Fills a real gap in the modular scalar-charge EMRI program. The existing 1PA scalar framework (Spiers et al. 2023) was nonspinning, and secondary spin is a 1PA effect, so a consistent spinning extension is needed for 1PA beyond-GR waveforms.
- Clean symmetry argument: building the charge and dipole from contractions of u and the angular velocity Omega (Cayley-Hamilton) shows the parity-even dipole is irrelevant at 1PA, and the pseudoscalar has no monopole and a dipole proportional to spin.
- Sensible limit checks: the GR limit reproduces Mathews et al. 2021, the nonspinning limit reproduces the first- and second-order scalar forces of Spiers et al. 2023, and setting Q=0 with a spin-aligned dipole recovers the dynamical Chern-Simons MPD equations of Loutrel, Tanaka and Yunes 2018.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Formalism only: there are no waveforms, dephasing estimates or numerical results, so we do not know how large the new spin-scalar terms are.
- The modified MPD structure and the spin-scalar coupling are not new in themselves (Obukhov and Puetzfeld 2014, Loutrel et al. 2018, Brax et al. 2021). What is new is the EMRI mass-ratio power counting through 1PA.
- The pseudoscalar 'same as GR at 1PA' conclusion assumes the coupling is set by the secondary's scale; if the coupling is larger, the primary's hair and background corrections would not be negligible.
- Spin-spin terms are neglected; the scalar self-coupling constant is left unmapped to any concrete theory.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

**In network:** 🚩 Thomas Sotiriou (notable author), 🚩 Andrew Spiers (extended-network collaborator)

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. 6 (Scalar vs pseudoscalar: the symmetry-based structure of the charges and dipole); Sec. 7.2 (the final 1PA MPD equations, including the new spin precession term); Sec. 7.4.1 (scalar self-force equations, with the explicit spin-times-scalar acceleration); Sec. 8 (pseudoscalar case, where it reduces to GR through 1PA). Skim the end of the Introduction for how the paper positions itself.

### 🌠 Should-Read

#### 🎵 Convergence of the quasinormal-mode expansion of the Kerr Green function

Ciattaglia, Kuntz, Barausse — [arXiv:2611.23110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23110) — gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.HE

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6/10 · 🎯 Relevance 6/10

> 💡 For a spinning black hole, the sum over ever more rapidly decaying ringing tones only starts to converge after a delay set by light-travel times to a surface near the hole, and that delay depends on whether the source and observer sit near the equator or near the spin axis.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper studies when the sum over quasinormal-mode overtones of the Kerr retarded Green function (Teukolsky, s=-2, fixed (l,m)) converges, by computing how the mode amplitudes grow with damping as a function of source and observer position. A large-damping WKB analysis gives an exponential growth rate per unit damping equal to the optical distance from a 'bounce radius' (about 2.5M at a/M=0.7) to the source plus that to the observer. For the prolate family this is reduced by a(2 - sin theta - sin theta'), because those spheroidal harmonics concentrate near the equator; the oblate family has no angular reduction. The sum therefore converges absolutely for t - t' > max(Gamma_prolate, Gamma_oblate). In practice the oblate family sets the threshold except when both points are close to the hole and near the equator. Growth rates from numerically computed complete amplitudes (overtones up to n=16000 oblate, 6000 prolate) match the prediction to better than 1%.

✅ **Strengths**

- Numerical validation is serious: complete residues, Wronskian derivative included, at overtones up to n=16000 (oblate) and 6000 (prolate), with secant growth rates within 0.65% (prolate) and 0.91% (oblate) of the WKB prediction across an observer-position grid.
- The phases of ratios of neighbouring amplitudes are reproduced without fitting, which tests more than magnitudes.
- The novelty positioning is honest: it cites and acknowledges the concurrent Arnaudo-Withers Kerr paper and states where the results overlap.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- The Kerr bounce radii and radial convergence regions were already published two weeks earlier by Arnaudo and Withers (arXiv:2611.03791); this paper's new content is the angular dependence and the numerical checks.
- All results are for a/M=0.7, (l,m)=(2,2), s=-2 only.
- Sufficient condition for the fixed-(l,m) pole sum only: branch cut, high-frequency arc and multipole sum are not treated.
- No link yet to waveforms at infinity or to ringdown fit start times; the authors explicitly defer this.

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. 2.1 for the convergence criterion setup; Sec. 3 for the radial optical distances and the prolate/oblate angular rates; Sec. 4 for the dominance map, the sufficient condition and the 'converges before causal arrival' remark; Appendix on numerics (growth-rate maps, Fig. with n up to 16000) to judge the validation. Section 5 (bounce surfaces, null fronts) is geometric interpretation and can be skimmed.

#### 💡 Long-Term Evolution of Hills-Captured Stars with TDE Disk Collisions: Links to Repeating Nuclear Transients

Huang, Yao, Lu, Quataert — [arXiv:2611.28220](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28220) — astro-ph.HE

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 7/10

> 💡 Stars captured by a massive black hole after their binary is torn apart can be dragged onto the hours-to-days orbits needed to explain repeating X-ray flares, but mostly because they keep crashing through the gas discs left by earlier star disruptions; gravitational waves and random stellar encounters alone would let most of them be destroyed first, and the stripping of stars in the gas sets a shortest reachable orbital period that grows with black hole mass.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper follows stars captured via the Hills mechanism (a binary broken up near the black hole) onto very eccentric orbits a few tidal radii from a 1e5 to 1e7 solar-mass black hole. It evolves the orbit and stellar mass under drag and ablation from recurrent, viscously spreading tidal-disruption-event discs, Peters gravitational-wave decay and Gaussian angular-momentum diffusion, using a Monte Carlo scheme. Drag plus mass loss together set a period floor that scales roughly linearly with black hole mass, does not depend on disc surface density or TDE rate, and rules out sub-day orbits above about 1e6 solar masses unless gravitational waves dominate. With relaxation switched on, the standard EMRI criterion (gravitational-wave time equals angular-momentum relaxation time) overestimates how many stars survive. TDE-disc drag raises the fraction of stars reaching orbits of 10 days or less from at most about 0.1 to about 0.3 in the fiducial model (t_rel = 10 Gyr, TDE rate 1e-4 per year, H/r = 0.1), and towards unity at higher TDE rates.

✅ **Strengths**

- Tackles a real gap: every orbiter model of quasi-periodic eruptions assumes a star sitting intact on an orbit of tens to hundreds of gravitational radii, and this paper actually tests whether a delivery channel can put it there.
- The period-floor argument is clean and analytic: drag and ablation both scale linearly with disc surface density, so d ln M / d ln a is independent of the TDE rate and disc mass. That gives P_min proportional to M_BH, a falsifiable prediction for the period versus black hole mass distribution.
- Builds on hydrodynamic mass-loss fits (Yao et al. 2025) and a MESA stellar response rather than an ad hoc stripping law.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- The headline survival fractions are set by an assumed uniform two-body relaxation time; going from 1 to 10 Gyr changes them by more than an order of magnitude.
- Sub-day orbits need a thick (H/r ~ 0.1), magnetically supported TDE disc that lasts long after the disruption; for H/r = 0.03 they essentially vanish.
- Disc drag is an orbit-averaged impulse at pericentre and apocentre only, ignoring disc rotation and orbit inclination; mass loss is extrapolated from 2D simulations and tidal heating is omitted.
- The 'EMRI criterion overestimates' result applies to stars starting right next to the loss cone (Hills captures), was partly noted by Kaur 2025, and uses an empirically fitted factor of 3.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Section 3 for the ablation period-floor argument and the P_min proportional to M_BH scaling. Section 5.2.2 for why t_GW = t_L,rel fails near the loss cone and the t_GW = 3 t_TDE replacement. Section 5.3.3 and the first part of Section 6 for the comparison with observed QPEs and repeating partial TDEs, and the admitted tensions. Sections 2.2 and 5.1 only to see how crude the drag and relaxation prescriptions are.

### 🔦 Worth-Skimming

#### ♻️ The Kerr Metric in Canonical Harmonic Coordinates to All Orders

Sadu, Vanhove — [arXiv:2611.27573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27573) — hep-th, gr-qc

🔦 **Worth-Skimming** · 🔬 Quality 6.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 5/10

> 💡 The paper writes down, in closed form, the coordinate change that puts the spinning-black-hole metric into the standard harmonic coordinates used by post-Minkowskian and amplitude calculations, and proves that this is exactly the chart those perturbative calculations produce at every order in Newton's constant and in the spin.

🔍 **What it does.** Starting from the two known complex radial harmonic functions behind the Cook-Scheel harmonic chart of Kerr, the authors take their real, time-reversal-symmetric combination and obtain a harmonic chart in which the azimuth stays the Boyer-Lindquist azimuth. They classify the decaying homogeneous solutions allowed by rotations, parity, time reversal and analyticity in the spin. Beyond linear order, the only one is a pure-gauge spatial mode at third post-Minkowskian order with angular degree p+2 at spin order a^p. Fixing that mode to zero, an induction shows that both the harmonic-gauge Einstein recursion with the Kerr multipole source and the canonical multipolar post-Minkowskian iteration reproduce the new chart to all orders. As a consequence, the canonical iteration never needs finite-part regularisation or a harmonicity correction. Closed-form post-Minkowskian coefficients are given through fifth order. They match Xing-He-Cao and Damgaard et al. at fourth order and are checked in exact arithmetic through G^5 and a^12, with a Landau-Lifshitz iteration carried to G^8.

✅ **Strengths**

- Resolves an open problem stated explicitly in a recent paper (Xing-He-Cao 2026): the closed-form link between the canonical post-Minkowskian chart of Kerr and the known harmonic charts.
- Clear logical structure. A symmetry lemma bounds the angular degree, a theorem confines decaying homogeneous solutions to one third-order spatial sector, and an induction identifies the recursion and the canonical iteration with the exact chart.
- Turns properties that were previously checked or assumed (no finite part, vanishing harmonicity correction, spatial components vanishing at odd orders) into statements valid to all orders.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- The closed-form chart is the real part of the known Cook-Scheel radial function; the new content is the proof that the canonical algorithm selects it.
- Uniqueness holds only after fixing one third-order gauge mode by a boundary prescription; symmetry alone does not pick the chart.
- Absence of logarithms is inherited from the exact Kerr solution, not derived independently.
- No dynamics, waveforms or observables: this is a statement about coordinates on stationary Kerr.

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. 1 (main results and hypotheses); Sec. 5.1-5.2 (harmonic functions and the three charts, i.e. the closed-form transformation); Sec. 4.2-4.3 (Theorem on homogeneous solutions and the uniqueness induction); Sec. 5.6 (identification with canonical multipolar post-Minkowskian coordinates); Sec. 7, Table of checks.

## 🗑️ Papers That Failed Critical Review

*Read in full, then rejected. Scores are the deep reader's, on the same 0-10
scale as the survivors above.*

1. **Orbit-averaged spinning and eccentric binary black holes: Hamiltonian and integrability** ([2611.24492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24492), Colin, Tanay, Alva-Morales, Anand) — 🔬 quality 4.5/10 · 🎯 relevance 5/10

   📣 **Claimed.** An independent derivation of the orbit-averaged Hamiltonian, with an explanation of why orbit-averaging the full Hamiltonian gives the right equation for L. The averaged constants of motion are related to the full 2PN ones. The paper constructs action-angle variables and frequencies, the second application of the fictitious-variable method, and computes the frequencies without differentiating elliptic integrals. It proposes a general criterion for integrability breakdown, det(dC/dJ) -> 0, that recovers the pendulum and Kepler separatrices and the up-down instability, giving the thresholds explicitly for eccentric orbits. It gives a Liouville-type proof that isotropy is preserved when all binaries share the same parameters and radiation reaction does not depend on spin, with numerical confirmation that isotropy can be lost otherwise. It also gives a closed-form generalization of the projected effective spin to binary neutron stars, exact in the quadrupole parameters and valid for eccentric orbits.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The paper's core premise, that the orbit-averaged system is Hamiltonian and integrable with the averaged Hamiltonian, was already established by Correia (Cassini states for black hole binaries, MNRAS Letters 2016). That work also gave the Hamiltonian, identified spin-orbit resonances as Cassini states and analysed the trajectories. The 're-derivation' and the 'confirmation that resonances are not resonances' are therefore confirmations, not results.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The eccentric up-down thresholds are a trivial substitution. The averaged equations depend on the orbit only through L, so circular r_ud± becomes p_ud± = a(1-e^2) via L = mu sqrt(m a(1-e^2)). Keresztes and Gergely (Phys. Rev. D 103, 084025, 2021) had already studied the linear stability of the aligned configurations for eccentric orbits and found the stable-to-unstable transition during inspiral.
