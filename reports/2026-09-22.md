#### 💫 A Systematic Study of Resonance-Driven Flux Modifications in Extreme-Mass-Ratio Inspirals

Levati, Keeble, Cárdenas-Avendaño — [arXiv:2609.25392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25392) — gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

🌟 **Must-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 8.5/10

> 💡 A large new set of black-hole perturbation calculations shows how much the energy and angular momentum lost by a small body spiralling into a spinning black hole changes when its radial and polar motions lock into a simple frequency ratio, and proves a simple counting rule for how that change depends on the relative phase of the two motions.

🔍 **What it does.** The authors implement the Flanagan-Hughes-Ruangsri resonant-flux formalism on top of the public pybhpt frequency-domain Teukolsky solver and compute snapshot energy, axial-angular-momentum and Carter-constant fluxes for geodesics sitting exactly on 3:1, 5:2, 2:1, 3:2, 5:4 and 4:3 radial-polar resonances in Kerr, scanning spin, eccentricity and inclination. Analytically, they show that Kerr's equatorial reflection symmetry kills all interference terms between degenerate harmonics with odd harmonic separation, so the flux modification must complete at least lcm(2, beta_r) cycles as the relative radial-polar phase runs over a full period, and resonances with odd beta_r lose their strongest (nearest-neighbour) interference terms. Numerically they confirm the previously reported strength ordering 3:2 > 2:1 > 5:2 > 3:1 >> 5:4 >> 4:3, give the first Teukolsky-based 5:2 and 5:4 coefficients, and find that the angular-momentum and Carter-constant modifications can be strongly suppressed where the horizon and infinity contributions cancel, locally reversing the 3:2 > 2:1 ordering. Results are validated against fresh runs of the independent gremlin code at the 1e-5 to 1e-2 relative level.

✅ **Strengths**

- The selection rule derivation is short, explicit and checkable: it combines the known Drasco-Hughes equatorial-symmetry relation for the Teukolsky amplitudes with the FHR degenerate-harmonic family structure, and the conclusion (only even harmonic separations survive) is a clean statement that holds mode by mode and therefore for the total flux.
- The odd-beta_r cancellation gives a genuine structural reason for something previously only observed empirically, namely why the 3:2 dominates and why 5:4 beats 4:3, rather than treating the hierarchy as a numerical accident.
- Numerics are taken seriously: adaptive shell-by-shell truncation in j, N and l with a stated tolerance, per-amplitude error flags from pybhpt including catastrophic-cancellation estimates, and geodesic-resolution refinement when flagged amplitudes matter.
- *…and 3 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Snapshot geodesic fluxes only: no inspiral is evolved and no dephasing or parameter-estimation impact is computed.
- Stated numerical tolerances (1e-5) are relative to the total flux, not to the resonant modification; coefficients quoted down to 1e-9 percent for the 4:3 and 5:4 resonances are not demonstrated to be resolved.
- The Carter-constant fluxes omit the known conservative self-force contribution to the on-resonance balance law.
- The analytic selection rule reproduces periodicities already observed empirically for the 3:2 and 2:1 cases; the new content is the general rule and its symmetry explanation, not the observation.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Appendix A (Sec. 6 in the source, 'Phase Dependence of the Resonant Flux Modifications') for the selection-rule derivation - this is the intellectual core and is about two pages. Sec. 4.1 for the statement and the numerical demonstration (2, 2, 6, 4 cycles for 2:1, 3:2, 4:3, 5:4). Sec. 4.6 for the horizon-infinity cancellation, the one genuinely new numerical phenomenon. Appendix B (Sec. 7) if you intend to use the tabulated coefficients, since the error-budget definitions there determine how much of the quoted precision to trust. Sec. 4.2 can be skimmed; its argument is qualitative.

#### 🌀 Black Hole Perturbation Toolkit: Low frequency and post-Newtonian expansions

Neef, Kavanagh, Ottewill — [arXiv:2609.25281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25281) — gr-qc, hep-th

🌟 **Must-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 8/10

> 💡 A free, documented software package now computes weak-field analytic solutions of the equation governing waves around a spinning black hole, automating a step that until now most groups did with private, hand-tuned code.

🔍 **What it does.** This is the companion paper to version 1.2 of the Teukolsky package and version 1.1 of the SpinWeightedSpheroidalHarmonics package in the Black Hole Perturbation Toolkit. It documents new functionality that solves the radial Teukolsky equation analytically in the Mano-Suzuki-Takasugi representation as a low-frequency (post-Minkowskian) or post-Newtonian series: the series coefficients, the renormalised angular momentum, all the standard asymptotic amplitudes, the tidal response ratio, the scattering phase shift, the homogeneous radial functions, and, for circular orbits in Kerr, the inhomogeneous solution and the energy and angular-momentum fluxes at infinity and the horizon. Everything works for generic spin weight and generic harmonic indices as well as for fixed ones, and the authors document the awkward technical part in detail: the places where the three-term recurrence degenerates (beta coefficients vanishing at n = -l, -l-1, -2l-1) and the leading orders of the series coefficients must be determined by an order-balancing argument case by case. Validation is against the toolkit's own numerics, against the stored post-Newtonian self-force data (agreement in Kerr fluxes to 7PN) and against the independent package of Markovic and Ivanov, with quoted timings of roughly four minutes for a 7PN radial function on a laptop and a doubling of cost per additional order.

✅ **Strengths**

- It fills a real gap: high-order analytic MST work has been done for fifteen years with private, largely undocumented Mathematica notebooks, and the barrier to entry has been correspondingly high. Making this public and documented is worth more to the field than another single analytic result.
- Not vapourware. The code in earlier form already underlies eight cited publications (Cunningham, Castillo et al., Bjerrum-Bohr et al., Khalaf et al., Casals et al., Brunello et al.), so it has been exercised on real problems before the paper appeared.
- Section 5 is genuinely useful technical documentation, not filler. The degenerate cases of the MST recurrence (beta_{n,0} = 0 at n = -l, -l-1, -2l-1) and the resulting order-balancing for the leading coefficients are practitioner folklore that is rarely written down anywhere; having it laid out spin by spin, including the ma = 0 subcase, is a service.
- *…and 3 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- A software manual, not a physics result. No new physical content.
- Not the first public analytic MST code: Markovic and Ivanov (arXiv:2511.04765) got there first in print. The real claims are wider scope, full automation and roughly forty times the speed.
- The 9PM scattering phase shift billed as beyond the literature is shown only as a timing, not as coefficients you can use or check.
- 'End-to-end self-force' means circular orbits in Kerr only, and stops short of metric reconstruction; eccentric and inclined orbits are future work.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

**In network:** 🚩 Chris Kavanagh (extended-network collaborator)

📖 **Where to start.** Section 3 ('Features at a glance') to decide in two minutes whether the package does what you need. Section 4.3 for the eta and gamma power-counting conventions, which you must get right before trusting any output and which differ from some conventions in the literature. Section 5.2-5.3 if you care how the degenerate MST recurrences are handled, or if you plan to extend the code. Section 9 for what has and has not actually been validated, and for realistic timings. Skip sections 6-8 unless you are using those specific functions.

#### 🌀 Renormalized Dynamical Friction in Black-Hole Spacetimes

Wilson-Gerow — [arXiv:2609.23192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23192) — gr-qc, astro-ph.HE, hep-th

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6/10 · 🎯 Relevance 8/10

> 💡 When a small compact object orbits a large black hole while ploughing through surrounding gas or dark matter, the drag it feels contains a short-distance infinity, and this paper shows that the infinity is exactly the same as in flat space, gives the recipe for subtracting it mode by mode, and points out that subtraction alone is not enough because one finite number must be supplied from a separate short-distance calculation.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper extends an in-in (closed-time-path) worldline effective field theory of a compact object moving through a medium from flat spacetime to a black-hole background, targeting the leading wake-mediated gravitational self-force at order (environment density) x (mass ratio squared). Using a Riemann-normal-coordinate large-momentum expansion, it argues that the diagram in which the particle's own retarded field polarizes the medium and returns to the worldline is only marginally (logarithmically) ultraviolet divergent, so curvature corrections, suppressed by 1/(k R)^2, cannot alter the coefficient of the logarithm: it is the flat tangent-space coefficient evaluated on the local fluid state. For a circular Schwarzschild orbit the paper then converts this into a Barack-Ori mode-sum statement, F_l = C/L + O(L^-2), supplies an analytic rotationally covariant puncture K_ct(alpha) = (A/2) ln(1 - cos alpha) with closed-form Legendre coefficients, and fixes the leftover finite Wilson coefficient for collisionless dust by matching to an existing near-zone geodesic-scattering result. No black-hole mode is actually computed: the deliverable is a regularization parameter plus a renormalization prescription.

✅ **Strengths**

- The central conceptual claim is genuinely non-obvious and useful to self-force practitioners: in vacuum, mode-sum or puncture subtraction is a complete calculation, whereas here the same subtraction only fixes a renormalization condition and a finite Wilson coefficient survives that must come from an independent short-distance (near-zone) calculation. Anyone planning to bolt environmental effects onto an existing self-force code would otherwise get a scheme-dependent, and hence wrong, answer.
- The locality argument is clean and correctly structured: because the flat diagram is only marginally divergent (measure dk/k), the environmental gradient correction (dk/(k^2 L)) and the curvature correction (dk/(k^3 R^2)) are both ultraviolet convergent, so the logarithm cannot pick up curvature dependence. The paper also states the loophole honestly, that curvature logarithms could appear in diagrams with a higher superficial degree of divergence, and asserts none occur at this order.
- The deliverable is concrete and implementable rather than formal: a closed-form regularization parameter C^a, a closed-form puncture and its Legendre coefficients K_ct,l = -A L/(l(l+1)) for l >= 1, and an explicit scheme-conversion between a hard transverse-momentum cutoff and the hard impact-parameter cutoff used in the matching reference (k_min = 2 e^{-gamma_E}/b_max).
- *…and 3 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- No numerics anywhere: not a single black-hole mode is computed, and the claimed 1/L tail is never checked against an independently calculated F_l.
- The flat-space coefficient, the dissipative operator, and the matched finite remainder all come from the authors' own companion paper of three weeks earlier; the new content here is the argument that curvature does not change the logarithm plus a Legendre rewriting of the counterterm.
- The finite Wilson coefficient rests on a numerical fit performed elsewhere, whose value and uncertainty are not quoted in this paper.
- Two contributions at the same order (the contact terms and the cubic-graviton topology) are dismissed by a two-sentence parity argument with no displayed calculation.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. 3 (Local ultraviolet region) for the power-counting hierarchy in Eq. (3.5) and the definition of the dissipative operator K; Sec. 4 (Mode-sum renormalization) for the F_l = C/L result and the puncture with its Legendre coefficients; Appendix 8.1 for the only actual derivation of the 1/L tail; the Discussion for the explicit list of omitted same-order contributions and the note added on the competing Datta paper. Sec. 5 is short but is where the matching, and hence the paper's weakest empirical link, actually sits.

#### ♻️ A Fully Analytic Expression for the 2PN N-Body Hamiltonian

Heinze, Schäfer, Brügmann — [arXiv:2609.17510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17510) — gr-qc

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 7/10 · 🎯 Relevance 5.5/10

> 💡 The last integral that nobody had been able to evaluate in the second-order relativistic correction to the motion of many gravitating bodies has now been solved in closed form, so the corresponding energy function can be written down explicitly for any number of point masses.

🔍 **What it does.** At second post-Newtonian order the N-body ADM Hamiltonian contains a four-body piece of the transverse-traceless static potential that, since Ohta et al. in 1974, had resisted explicit evaluation; the authors' own earlier work had reduced it to a single unevaluated spatial integral I^ln_{ab;cd}. This paper evaluates that integral analytically, obtaining a compact expression in the six inter-particle distances plus one transcendental piece proportional to the tetrahedron volume times an arctangent of a shape angle. Inserting it gives a fully explicit, quadrature-free conservative 2PN Hamiltonian for an arbitrary number of nonspinning point masses, which the authors also write out in full. The result is validated against direct numerical evaluation of the original three-dimensional integral and of a reduced two-dimensional parameter integral for 1000 random four-particle configurations (generic, hierarchical, coplanar, collinear), with mean relative deviations of order 1e-28 to 1e-26 in quadruple precision. A long-propagated index error in one three-body kinetic term of the published 2PN Hamiltonian is also corrected.

✅ **Strengths**

- The claim is sharp, falsifiable and actually delivered: a single explicit formula for I^ln_{ab;cd} that closes a gap open since Ohta et al. (1974) and explicitly flagged as open by Chu (2009) and by the authors' own 2026 PRD.
- The final expression is remarkably compact and geometrically transparent: everything reduces to the six inter-particle distances, a positive cubic invariant D, the tetrahedron volume Delta, and a single scale-invariant angle Phi = arctan(Delta/D). The only non-rational structure surviving in the whole 2PN Hamiltonian is Delta*Phi.
- Numerical validation is unusually careful for a formula paper: quadruple precision, Gauss-Legendre with automatic order refinement up to order 292, compensated summation, 100-digit spot checks, 1000 configurations split across generic, hierarchical, coplanar and collinear classes, and two independent integral representations (3D Cuhre and the 2D parameter integral).
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- The derivation is only sketched in prose; no intermediate steps are printed, so the formula cannot be re-derived from the paper.
- Validation is numerical and internal -- the closed form is compared to the same group's own integral representation, not to an independent formalism or gauge.
- The step beyond the authors' own 2026 PRD is tractability, not physics: the 2PN N-body dynamics was already computable numerically. No timing benchmark or application is given.
- The derivation was obtained with substantive large-language-model assistance (disclosed in Sec. II); the authors' stated safeguard is independent verification plus the numerical check.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. II (closed form of I^ln_{ab;cd}, the geometric invariants D, Delta, Phi, the compact four-point potential, the full 2PN Hamiltonian, and the corrected index error); Sec. III (numerical validation); Sec. IV (open 3PN problem).

#### ☁️ Environmental Effects in Post-Minkowskian Dynamics: Effective Field Theory, Feynman Rules, and Ward Identities for Compact Objects in Relativistic Fluids

Bern, Degen, Herrmann, Solon et al. — [arXiv:2609.20742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20742) — hep-th, gr-qc, hep-ph

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 7/10 · 🎯 Relevance 5/10

> 💡 A new set of particle-physics-style calculation rules is built for a compact object moving through a relativistic gas, and as its first test it reproduces the known drag force that such an object feels.

🔍 **What it does.** The authors couple the symmetry-based effective theory of a perfect relativistic fluid to weak-field gravity and to a point mass represented by a massive scalar, keeping the fluid's sound modes (phonons) as explicit quantum fields rather than integrating the medium out. They derive the full set of propagators and interaction vertices up to four fields, including the nonlinear graviton-phonon couplings, and verify them with generalized on-shell Ward identities that relate a longitudinally contracted graviton amplitude to the same amplitude with the graviton replaced by a phonon -- a gravitational analogue of Goldstone equivalence in a spontaneously broken gauge theory. They also establish a selection rule that on-shell transverse-traceless gravitons do not mix into phonons, and compute tree-level one- and two-phonon emission amplitudes. As the sole physics application, an energy-weighted unitarity cut in the Kosower-Maybee-O'Connell formalism reproduces the coefficient of the Coulomb logarithm in the known leading-order relativistic dynamical-friction force for supersonic rectilinear motion in an inviscid fluid.

✅ **Strengths**

- The technical deliverable is real and reusable: complete propagators and three- and four-point vertices for the coupled graviton-phonon-perturber system, released as ancillary files, not just a schematic construction.
- The generalized Ward identity is a genuine structural result, not a formality -- it identifies the subluminal longitudinal graviton mode of the fluid background with the phonon, and it provides an independent check on a Lagrangian that contains Levi-Civita structures where errors are easy to make.
- The selection rule that on-shell transverse-traceless gravitons do not mix with phonons kills whole classes of diagrams and materially simplifies the leading-order calculation; combined with the phase-space argument (graviton cuts would need v > 1), it makes the single-phonon cut the unique leading contribution.
- *…and 3 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- No new physics result: the only number computed is the coefficient of the Coulomb logarithm in a dynamical-friction force known since 2007, and the order-one term inside the logarithm is not determined.
- That check barely tests the new vertices -- the leading force turns out to be independent of the sound speed, and the two-phonon amplitude is computed but never used.
- Validity is restricted to a homogeneous, static, inviscid, weakly self-gravitating fluid in flat space with supersonic rectilinear motion; curved backgrounds, bound orbits, viscosity, dissipation and accretion are all future work.
- For the paper's own thin-disk benchmark the effective-field-theory window spans only about three decades, and the orbit-averaged drag is roughly a hundred times smaller than the quoted instantaneous formula.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

**In network:** 🚩 Zvi Bern (notable author)

📖 **Where to start.** Section 2.2.4 (window of scales, thin-disk benchmark, honest regime-of-validity discussion); Section 6 (dynamical friction from a phonon-emission unitarity cut, including the cutoff-independent differential spectrum); Section 5.1 (structure of the generalized Ward identity and the identification of the longitudinal graviton with the phonon); the Note Added at the end of the Introduction for the relation to the concurrent Modrekiladze-Rothstein-Wilson-Gerow papers.

## 🗑️ Papers That Failed Critical Review

*Read in full, then rejected. Scores are the deep reader's, on the same 0-10
scale as the survivors above.*

1. **Correlated Galactic Confusion Foreground for the LISA-Taiji-TianQin Network and Its Impacts on Resolvable-Source Analysis** ([2609.16477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16477), Du, Xu, Luo) — 🔬 quality 6/10 · 🎯 relevance 2.5/10

   📣 **Claimed.** First rigorous construction of the correlated Galactic-foreground covariance matrix (power spectral densities plus cross-detector cross-spectral densities) for the LISA-Taiji-TianQin network from a full realistic catalogue with proper time-delay-interferometry response; an analytic cross-detector coherence model cross-validated against simulation; the first assessment of what neglecting these correlations does to resolvable-source inference (massive black hole binaries and Galactic binaries) in signal-to-noise, Fisher uncertainties and full Bayesian posteriors; and public release of network analysis codes, foreground covariance data and network sensitivity curves.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The central reassuring claim -- that neglecting the correlations produces no parameter bias -- rests on probability-probability tests with 50 massive-black-hole-binary injections and 55 verification binaries. At that sample size the 1-2 sigma bands are wide and a Kolmogorov-Smirnov p-value above 0.05 simply cannot exclude systematic shifts at the few-tenths-of-a-sigma level, which is exactly the regime where a 10-35 percent change in credible-interval width would be expected to show up. Absence of evidence is presented as evidence of absence.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** Both compared noise models use four-year time-averaged (Welch) spectra, while the paper's own appendix shows the LISA-Taiji coherence swinging between roughly 0 and 0.9 with annual modulation. The 'full-covariance' model is therefore itself a misspecification of the true non-stationary foreground, so the comparison bounds the difference between two wrong models rather than the error incurred by ignoring the real correlation. The authors flag time-dependent covariance as future work, but the headline conclusion is stated more broadly than this supports.

2. **Radial Stress and the Innermost Stable Circular Orbit of a Bardeen Black Hole in a Dark Matter Halo: A First-Order Response Criterion** ([2609.16685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16685), Deng, Liu, Guo) — 🔬 quality 4/10 · 🎯 relevance 3/10

   📣 **Claimed.** A general first-order criterion that separates the density and radial-pressure contributions to the halo-induced ISCO shift of a black hole in a dilute static halo, validated across several density profiles and two regular backgrounds; a physically motivated cutoff radius set by the marginally bound circular orbit of the isolated (charged) background rather than by hand; and a demonstration that, at fixed density profile and cutoff, the choice of dark matter radial stress alone changes the accumulated EMRI gravitational-wave phase by several radians.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The headline pressure-induced dephasing is very likely an artifact of normalization rather than a physical signature. Models 2 and 3 differ near the black hole essentially by a constant lapse factor, since d ln(f/g)/dr = 8 pi r (rho + p_r) and the integrated halo contribution is ~2 M_DM/a_0 ~ 5 x 10^-6 for the chosen parameters. Multiplied by the ~1.6 x 10^6 rad total four-year phase this gives ~8 rad, which is exactly the quoted Phi_3 - Phi_2 = -7.93 rad. A constant lapse rescaling is a pure gravitational redshift and is degenerate with the redshifted central mass; the paper never checks this, and the effect may be identically unobservable.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The magnetic-charge dephasings (-47 rad at g_s/M = 0.01 up to -6 x 10^4 rad at g_s/M = 0.5) are an unsurprising consequence of an order-unity deformation of the near-horizon metric. No constraint, bound or measurability statement is extracted, and g_s is never tied to any physical scale or astrophysical expectation.

3. **Massively parallel transdimensional sampling with data assimilation: an application to LISA galactic binaries** ([2609.17674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17674), Demasi, Pozzo) — 🔬 quality 4/10 · 🎯 relevance 3/10

   📣 **Claimed.** A trans-dimensional Sequential Monte Carlo sampler that (i) combines reversible-jump model moves with NUTS within-model mutations, (ii) is massively parallel over particles and GPU-implemented in JAX, (iii) provides automatic adaptive tempering and a by-product evidence estimate, and (iv) can assimilate new data by restarting from a previous posterior rather than re-running from the prior; presented as a proof of concept route toward a scalable LISA global fit.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The central validation claim -- that the sampler 'correctly recovers the number of injected signals' -- is compromised by the experiment design: the prior on k runs over [1,10] and exactly 10 components are injected, so the true value sits on the upper boundary of the prior and the sampler is structurally unable to overestimate it. The LISA run uses 'the same settings' and also injects 10. The heatmaps showing k converging to the injected value are therefore showing convergence to the prior edge.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** Both experiments are noise-free, with a constant power spectral density in the LISA case, and the ten Galactic binaries are placed on an equally spaced frequency grid 'such that the sources are clearly identifiable'. Every ingredient that makes the real problem hard -- noise fluctuations creating and hiding sources, sub-threshold confusion, source overlap, non-stationary and correlated noise -- has been removed.
