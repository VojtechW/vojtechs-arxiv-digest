#### 〰️ Efficient Eccentric and Quasicircular Effective-One-Body Waveforms via Near-Identity Averaging Transformations

Lynch, Buonanno, Gamboa, Meent — [arXiv:2610.29856](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29856) — gr-qc

🌟 **Must-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6/10 · 🎯 Relevance 7/10

> 💡 Averaging away the fast orbital oscillations of an eccentric binary, now with orbital variables that stay well behaved for circular orbits, reproduces a simplified effective-one-body model's waveforms almost exactly while producing them 3 to 100 times faster, though that speed-up is measured against a deliberately slow reference code.

🔍 **What it does.** This is the follow-up to the authors' June 2026 paper (arXiv:2606.30594), which averaged out orbital-timescale oscillations in nonspinning eccentric effective-one-body (EOB) dynamics using near-identity transformations through second post-adiabatic order, but broke down below e = 0.035 and gained only 1.5-8x end to end. Here the eccentricity and relativistic anomaly are replaced by the eccentricity-vector components (alpha, beta) = e(sin w, cos w), which remain regular as e goes to 0. A rotational covariance (spin-weight) argument reduces the apparent 4D tabulation back to the old 3D (nu, p, e) grid. The (2,2) mode is decomposed into harmonics of the relativistic anomaly, tabulated against the averaged variables and assembled sample by sample on the output time grid. Against the full EOB evolution of the same inspiral-only toy model for M = 10-100 solar masses, e0 = 0-0.85 and q up to 96, the 2PA waveforms reach median mismatches of about 1e-8 (maximum 1.1e-5). End-to-end speed-ups are 3-100x on one core and up to 300x with 8-core assembly against a serial reference.

✅ **Strengths**

- The circular-limit problem is solved cleanly. The spin-weight covariance A_kappa(alpha, beta) = exp(i(s - kappa)w) A_kappa(e, w = 0), together with reduced coefficients that are smooth in e^2, makes regularity manifest and keeps the tables 3D (Sec. 3.5).
- A concrete, transferable observation for EMRI waveform builders: expanding the modes in the relativistic anomaly needs at most about 8 harmonics up to e0 = 0.85, against about 50 at e = 0.7 in the mean-anomaly multi-voice approach (Secs. 4.3 and 6.3).
- Validation is dense and systematic: about 1,800 systems over four total masses, with eccentricity and mass ratio swept on grids, a harmonic-truncation convergence study, and an independent osculating-element-only model that attributes the residual error to table interpolation rather than post-adiabatic truncation.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Toy model: nonspinning, inspiral-only, (2,2) mode only, no merger-ringdown.
- The 3-300x speed-ups are against a slow Mathematica reference that evaluates the symbolic mode expression at every step, and the 300x figure is 8 cores against 1. Expect far less against optimized production code.
- The 1e-8 mismatches are against the same toy model's full evolution, not numerical relativity.
- A same-day paper by Gamba (arXiv:2610.30064) does equinoctial-element averaging on a spinning, precessing production EOB model and finds only about 1.5-2.6x median speed-up.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

**In network:** 🚩 Alessandra Buonanno (notable author), 🚩 Maarten van de Meent (extended-network collaborator)

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. 3.5 (rotational covariance and spin-weight reduction to the 3D grid, the most reusable idea); Sec. 4.3 (relativistic- versus mean-anomaly harmonic decomposition and how it differs from FastEMRIWaveforms); Sec. 6.3 (mismatch sweep and the harmonic convergence figure); Sec. 6.4 together with the Discussion for an honest reading of the timing claims and the comparison with Gamba (2026); Appendix A if you care about the circular limit of the osculating equations.

### 🌠 Should-Read

#### 🎵 First-Order Structure of the Linearised Einstein Equations: Constant-Curvature Backgrounds

Krasnov, Shaw — [arXiv:2610.32692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32692) — gr-qc, hep-th

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6/10 · 🎯 Relevance 7/10

> 💡 On de Sitter and anti-de Sitter backgrounds, the standard machinery for decoupling gravitational perturbations and rebuilding the metric from a scalar-like potential can be split into simple first-derivative steps, which yields a new way to rebuild the metric around a source with a purely local correction term instead of the usual long-range one.

🔍 **What it does.** Working in the chiral (Plebanski) formulation of general relativity, the authors show that on four-dimensional constant-curvature backgrounds with nonzero cosmological constant the linearised Einstein operator, the curvature-extraction operator, the decoupled operator and the reconstruction operator of the Teukolsky-Wald framework all factor into first-order maps through the perturbation of the self-dual spin connection; the fourth-order identity SG=OT follows from a single second-order identity d1 d1* - 1 = d2* d2. Conformal flatness is used to choose a weighted reconstruction map so that each component of the rescaled potential obeys the flat wave equation. With sources they propose a 'version II' reconstruction in which the potential equation is sourced by the full self-dual curvature itself, so the corrector becomes the local algebraic term -j (proportional to the stress-energy) rather than the string-supported corrector of Green-Hollands-Zimmerman. The scheme is illustrated for a static point mass in de Sitter space, where the reconstructed metric is real and agrees with linearised Schwarzschild-de Sitter up to an explicitly given diffeomorphism.

✅ **Strengths**

- Clean operator-level statement: the fourth-order identity G S* = T* O* is obtained by composition from a single second-order identity d1 d1* - 1 = d2* d2, the gravitational analogue of the chiral Maxwell factorisation d+* d+.
- The local, algebraic corrector in the sourced scheme addresses a genuinely painful point of radiation-gauge reconstruction (string-supported or shell-supported correctors for point particles), and the mechanism is transparent: the connection-to-metric map is algebraic when Psi + Lambda/3 is invertible.
- Explicit, checkable worked example: static point mass in de Sitter, with the reconstructed metric shown to be real and related to linearised Schwarzschild-de Sitter by an explicitly written diffeomorphism.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Only de Sitter and anti-de Sitter backgrounds; nothing is computed on Kerr or Schwarzschild.
- The local-corrector scheme needs the full linearised Weyl curvature as input, which on Kerr does not obey a decoupled equation.
- Reality of the reconstructed metric is argued for retarded solutions but not proven.
- The connection-to-metric map relies on a nonzero cosmological constant; the flat case needs a separate construction.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Introduction (operator diagram and the identity d1 d1* - 1 = d2* d2); Section 3.4 (proof of the curvature identity); Section 7.1 (a clear review of standard Teukolsky-Wald and GHZ reconstruction in this notation); Section 7.3 (version II, the reality argument and its stated gaps); Section 7.4 (comparison table); Section 7.6 (what changes on Kerr). Section 6 only if you want to check the de Sitter point-mass example.

### 🔦 Worth-Skimming

#### ♻️ PSR J0653+0443: An Eclipsing Millisecond Pulsar in an Extremely Eccentric Double Neutron Star System

Yang, Han, Hou, Chen et al. — [arXiv:2610.29838](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29838) — astro-ph.HE

🔦 **Worth-Skimming** · 🔬 Quality 6/10 · 🎯 Relevance 3.5/10

> 💡 Radio astronomers using the Chinese FAST telescope have found a pair of neutron stars on the most elongated orbit ever measured for any binary star, swinging from far apart to less than a solar radius apart once every 79 days, and briefly hiding one star behind the other.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper reports the FAST discovery and two years of timing of PSR J0653+0443, a 47.5 ms partially recycled pulsar in a 79.08-day double neutron star orbit with eccentricity 0.99238692, the highest measured for any stellar binary. Periastron advance gives a total mass of 2.814 solar masses, and a weak Shapiro-delay constraint splits this into about 1.38 and 1.44 solar masses with inclination 76 (+8/-15) degrees. One periastron observation shows an 85-second eclipse near superior conjunction, which the authors attribute to the companion's magnetosphere. The paper also fits the orbital-phase-dependent part of the relativistic periastron shift (a millisecond-level timing signature) to bound any non-relativistic precession, runs a population synthesis arguing such systems are rare, and computes the burst-like gravitational-wave signal, which LISA cannot detect (signal-to-noise about 0.1) but which proposed larger detectors might.

✅ **Strengths**

- A genuinely new and extreme object: e = 0.99238692 +/- 5e-8 from a dedicated two-hour FAST track through periastron, with a phase-connected solution over more than two years.
- Pragmatic and well-motivated timing reparametrisation (zeta_1, zeta_2, sqrt(1-e^2)) that cuts parameter correlations from |r| about 0.999 to about 0.1-0.3; useful for other very eccentric binaries.
- The total mass from periastron advance is robust (2.814 +0.018/-0.021 solar masses), and the companion mass bound (at least 1.38 solar masses) makes the double-neutron-star identification secure.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- The relativistic timing physics is standard Damour-Deruelle; the novelty is the object, not the method.
- The headline 'non-relativistic periastron advance' is a 1.7-sigma result that is consistent with zero.
- The eclipse is one 85-second event; the timing inclination (76 degrees) sits awkwardly with the 'edge-on confirmed' wording.
- Not detectable by LISA (signal-to-noise about 0.1); detectability claims refer to proposed missions only.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Main-text sections 'Pulsar J0653+0443 and its extraordinary orbit' and 'The relativistic effects' (about 10 minutes, all the key numbers). Methods 'Keplerian parameters and modified timing models' if you care about timing near-parabolic orbits. Methods 'GW emission and future evolution' for the burst waveform and characteristic-strain figure. Skip the population synthesis and read arXiv:2610.31583 instead.

## 🗑️ Papers That Failed Critical Review

*Read in full, then rejected. Scores are the deep reader's, on the same 0-10

1. **Untangling Extreme Mass Ratio Inspirals and Massive Black Hole Binaries in LISA Data** ([2610.27642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27642), Agrawal, Allietta, Burke, Chua) — 🔬 quality 5/10 · 🎯 relevance 4/10

   📣 **Claimed.** The paper claims to be the first parameter-estimation study to look directly at cross-talk between MBHBs and EMRIs in LISA data. It also claims to supply a practical, cheaper-than-joint inference recipe for global-fit pipelines: fit MBHBs first (jointly when their mergers nearly coincide), subtract them, then infer EMRIs one at a time. Finally, it argues that large MBHB parameter shifts caused by missing waveform modes do not necessarily carry over to the EMRI, because what matters is the part of the residual orthogonal to the model manifold.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** Walkers are started at the injected parameters and the priors are centred on them. Search, multimodality and secondary maxima, which are the hard part of EMRI analysis and the most likely place for MBHB residuals to cause trouble, are excluded by construction.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** There are only about ten hand-picked configurations, mostly noiseless, with EMRIs clustered around M ~ 1e6 solar masses and mu ~ 10 solar masses. 'Weak coupling' is shown for these cases, not established in general.

2. **Noisy Pipelines I: An end-to-end pipeline to perform multivariate noise inference for LISA** ([2610.28726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28726), Burke, Bayley, Hoy, Nuttall) — 🔬 quality 5.5/10 · 🎯 relevance 2.5/10

   📣 **Claimed.** The authors claim to be the first to propagate the full data conditioning (band-pass and anti-alias filter response, decimation aliasing, taper) into the LISA noise covariance beyond window normalisation, and to model the drift of the TDI transfer-function zeros within a segment. They pair this with a fast, differentiable multivariate 3x3 noise likelihood built on single-link splines, plus open-source processing and inference codes.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The test is close to an inverse crime: the simulator uses exactly two noise spectra shared by all six links and exact light travel times, which is the model's own structure; there is no clock, tilt-to-length, ranging or laser residual noise.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** Knot positions, ghost knots and prior centres are all set from the true injected spectrum; ghost knots are fixed at true values, and the window-convolution shortcut depends on knowing the true spectral shape.

3. **Lasting mEMRIs: Signatures of Multiple Black Hole Extreme Mass Ratio Inspirals from AGN discs** ([2610.29376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29376), Moncrieff, Grishin, Panther, Trani) — 🔬 quality 4/10 · 🎯 relevance 5/10

   📣 **Claimed.** A mass-ratio and resonance-index criterion for resonances to survive gravitational-wave-dominated migration that unifies earlier numerical results. The first gravitational-wave strain signatures of a co-migrating resonant black-hole pair, including synodic-frequency sidebands. An effective-chirp-mass degeneracy with isolated inspirals, with an observed mass ratio quantised by the resonance order, and a way to break it using amplitude ratios. A rate estimate of about 10 per year at z < 0.2.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The central criterion is a one-line comparison of Newtonian Peters decay timescales at exact resonance, i.e. the standard convergent/divergent migration condition. It ignores the resonant coupling strength, eccentricity and the libration width, and is tested with only two simulations at one k.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The relativistic resonance condition (radial frequencies locked at k:(k-1), azimuthal ratio offset) is essentially the relativistic mean-motion-resonance framework of Yang, Bonga, Peng and Li (2019), re-expressed at 1PN. The paper cites that work only for the precession identity, not for the resonance structure.

4. **Post-adiabatic effective-one-body waveforms for coalescing compact binaries** ([2610.30064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30064), Gamba) — 🔬 quality 6/10 · 🎯 relevance 5/10

   📣 **Claimed.** Application and extension of the Damour-Gopakumar-Iyer / Lynch et al. osculating-orbit averaging to a production effective-one-body model with spins, tides and precession, carried to next-to-next-to-leading order; reformulation in equinoctial elements so the method covers the quasi-circular limit; radiation-reaction-consistent low-eccentricity initial data with residual eccentricity of about 7x10^-8; and frequency-domain eccentric effective-one-body waveforms via a multi-harmonic stationary phase approximation.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** Core method is not new: the averaging scheme comes from Damour-Gopakumar-Iyer 2004, its higher-order near-identity version from Lynch et al. (arXiv:2606.30594), and the regular circular-limit variables were suggested in a footnote of that paper and implemented in the concurrent Lynch et al. (arXiv:2610.29856). What is left is an application to a different, richer model.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** Speedups are modest: median about 1.5 for binary black holes and 2.6 for binary neutron stars against the optimised ODE, and the averaged route is slower than the ODE for a sizeable fraction of black-hole systems above roughly ten solar masses or at moderate eccentricity. The concurrent Lynch et al. paper reports 3 to 100 times speedups by tabulating the averaged rates and waveform harmonics offline.

5. **Price's law for scalar waves on 1+4 dimensional subextremal Myers-Perry spacetimes** ([2610.33812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33812), Peters) — 🔬 quality 5/10 · 🎯 relevance 2/10

   📣 **Claimed.** (i) First proof that the null geodesic flow on 1+4 dimensional Myers-Perry is normally hyperbolically trapped in the full subextremal range (previous wave-equation results on these spacetimes, by Laul-Metcalfe-Tikare-Tohaneanu, needed small spins); (ii) a sharp Price law with explicit leading term phi = c t^{-3} + O(t^{-4+}) for compactly supported data, conditional on real-axis mode stability; (iii) the requisite dynamical and microlocal set-up (radial sets at horizon, convexity at infinity, timelike t_* foliation through the ergoregion) for this family.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The headline Price law is conditional on real-axis mode stability, which is unproven for 5D Myers-Perry. Only upper-half-plane mode stability (Cvetic-Pope-Whiting-Zhang) is known, and the paper calls the missing piece 'mild' without proof.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** Once high-energy estimates are available, the late-time analysis largely transposes Hintz's Kerr framework (and the even-spatial-dimension asymptotics in Hintz's 'Linear waves on asymptotically flat spacetimes I'). The t^{-3} rate is the generic flat-space tail in four space dimensions, coming from the sigma^2 log sigma term, and is not a black-hole-specific effect.
