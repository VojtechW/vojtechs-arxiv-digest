# ⏱ Observations of X-ray Quasi-Periodic Eruptions

**arXiv:** [2607.15359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15359)
**Authors:** T. Wevers, J. Chakraborty, E. Quintin, M. Zajacek, M. Giustini
**Category:** astro-ph.HE
**Submitted:** 2026-07-16

## TL;DR
Invited Handbook chapter reviewing the observational state of X-ray QPEs as of mid-2026: the 13-source population (GSN 069 through eRASSt J2344), spectral-timing phenomenology, host-galaxy demographics, TDE and QPO connections, and observational tests of the two dominant model families (EMRI star-disk collision vs. disk instabilities). Theory chapters are explicitly deferred to sister Handbook contributions. The paper includes one new observational result — MUSE/Magellan integral-field detection of an extended emission-line region (EELR) in eRASSt J2344, plus a population-level EELR fraction f_EELR = 0.69 (+0.31/-0.23).

## Summary
This is an observationally-focused synthesis review, part of a Handbook of X-ray and Gamma-ray Astrophysics volume. Sections cover: (1) discovery history and definitions; (2) X-ray timing/spectral properties source-by-source with a compilation table of 13 QPEs; (3) demographics — rates, selection biases; (4) hosts — nuclear concentration, colours, black-hole and stellar-mass distributions, and the striking result that most QPE hosts show post-starburst signatures and extended emission-line regions consistent with a recent AGN or TDE echo; (5) observational constraints on theoretical scenarios, with side-by-side "strengths and shortcomings" of the EMRI star-disk collision family (Linial & Metzger 2023; Franchini+ 2023; Yao+ 2025; Linial+ 2025) and disk-instability family (Raj & Nixon; Miniutti-family radiation-pressure instabilities; Kaur+); (6) overlap with TDEs and QPOs; (7) forward look — eROSITA cycles, Einstein Probe, ATHENA/NewAthena, and LISA prospects. The QPE-EMRI multi-messenger discussion notes that currently known QPE periods are too long for LISA sensitivity (>1 mHz) and back-propagates from a 2037-2041 LISA operations window to a "sweet spot" ν_QPE = 0.46 +/- 0.22 mHz, i.e., orbital periods of 0.41-1.16 hr.

## Strengths
- Comprehensive, well-balanced coverage of the observational literature and honest about which observations discriminate between models.
- Up-to-date: includes the 2025 additions (Ansky/ZTF19acnskyy, eRASSt J2344, eRO-QPE5, AT2022upj, AT2019qiz-QPEs) and the 2025 Chakraborty super-period papers.
- Introduces a piece of genuinely new data — EELR mapping of eRASSt J2344 and the population EELR fraction — inside a review, which strengthens the TDE-host connection argument.
- Multi-messenger section (§8.4) writes out the LISA sensitivity mismatch explicitly and quantitatively, pointing toward μHz-range detectors (TianQin, Taiji) as the natural coincidence window for current QPE periods.
- Even-handed on models: gives EMRI-collision and disk-instability families equal air time with explicit critique of each.

## Weaknesses
- It is a review; the fraction of genuinely new science is small (one EELR detection, one population statistic). A researcher already tracking QPE literature closely will find few surprises beyond the compilation table.
- No explicit positioning against prior QPE reviews (Nicholl 2024 topical review and Chakraborty et al. 2025 syntheses are not directly contrasted). The Chapter is presented as authoritative synthesis rather than as a delta over previous syntheses.
- The multi-messenger LISA sweet-spot argument is essentially a re-derivation of well-established Peters formula results; QPE-EMRI GW predictions (Kejriwal+ 2024; Lui+ 2025) are cited rather than critically evaluated.
- Some model comparisons flatten important distinctions (e.g., the Yao/Linial "debris-stream" refinement of the collision family gets short shrift compared to the naive direct-collision variant).

And from the closing summary:

> "The discovery of QPEs has revealed a new window of opportunity to study black hole accretion processes and their environments, including the fundamental physics governing the evolution of accretion disks and stellar dynamics in galactic nuclei, and potentially the EM counterparts to low frequency gravitational wave sources. The known sample has grown to 13 systems to date... As a newly identified phenomenon, the properties and behavior of QPEs remain to be charted in full."

Predecessor cross-check: this is a Handbook chapter, so the natural predecessors are Nicholl (2024) topical review of TDE-QPE connection, and the source-catalog compilations spread across Arcodia et al. 2021, 2024, 2025; Chakraborty et al. 2021, 2024, 2025; Quintin et al. 2023; Bykov et al. 2025; Hernández-García et al. 2025 (Ansky); Baldini et al. 2026 (eRASSt J2344). The novelty claim is implicit rather than defended — the chapter positions itself as the observational synthesis to accompany separate theoretical Handbook chapters. The EELR result and f_EELR = 0.69 statistic are genuinely new content.

## Relevance to Witzany
High for topical awareness — Witzany's QPE-via-stellar-transits model needs to be consistent with exactly the observations reviewed here. The 13-source compilation, the O-C timing summary of eRO-QPE2, the AT2019qiz-QPE confirmation as a TDE-connected system, and the LISA-band sweet-spot period range (0.41-1.16 hr) are all worth extracting as reference material. The multi-messenger section is the most likely to yield ideas: the mismatch between current QPE periods and LISA sensitivity is a live problem, and the μHz-detector argument is one Witzany's EMRI work could engage with. This is a Should-Read as a reference to keep next to the desk, not a Must-Read for weekly reading.
