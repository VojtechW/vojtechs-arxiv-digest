## 🎯 Thematic Trends This Week

**EFT-extended perturbation theory is the dominant thread.** Both the Cano group (2606.07070) and the Huan Yang group (2606.09766) attack different angles of higher-derivative gravity on top of the Teukolsky/master-equation backbone: Cano constructs the binary EFT waveform machinery to 6PN on Schwarzschild and finds that all higher-derivative effects funnel through Love numbers at universal (2ℓ+1)PN order, breaking the Heun-class structure of the radial equation along the way; Huan Yang's group plugs an actual EMRI point-particle source into the modified Teukolsky formalism on a cubic-gravity Schwarzschild background and discovers a ~1–2 order-of-magnitude *horizon* flux enhancement. Both stop at Schwarzschild, and both punt on either tidal-Love completion (Yang) or rotating extension (Cano). The clear next paper, from whoever gets there first, is the spinning version with consistent tidal contributions.

**Self-force infrastructure is consolidating around production NR codes.** Vu et al. (2606.04998) port SpECTRE's elliptic DG-AMR initial-data solver to scalar *m*-mode SF on Kerr, with explicit comparison to Macedo et al.'s hyperboloidal approach. Separately, Vretinaris et al. (2606.06487) push a 1+1D lab-frame puncture scheme to avoid the acceleration-dependent worldtube problem; this one is far less mature but the Krishnan group is clearly committing to a NR-style alternative to standard BHPT-SF. Two parallel programmes are visibly competing — and both are still publishing flat-space or scalar/circular toy results, signalling that the gravitational + generic-orbit benchmarks are still months to years away.

**Resonant dynamics is bridging communities.** The Hinderer-coauthored BNS tidal-resonance paper (2606.06376) finally puts proper Bayesian PE on transient phase jumps in inspiral signals — a structural twin of the EMRI transient-resonance problem. The methodology (Bayes-factor with background calibration on no-resonance injections, prior on jump amplitude and frequency) is directly portable. With the Hinderer / Lynch–Witzany / Drummond authorship clusters intersecting, the "transient resonance detectability" problem looks ripe for cross-pollination between the BNS and EMRI communities.
