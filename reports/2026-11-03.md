#### ☁️ Weighing dark matter particles with gravitational waves

Vicente, Duque, Karydas, Bertone — [arXiv:2610.37638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37638) — gr-qc, astro-ph.CO, astro-ph.GA

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 7/10 · 🎯 Relevance 6.5/10

> 💡 A forecast shows that if a small black hole spirals into a lighter massive black hole surrounded by a dense dark matter overdensity, the space detector LISA could measure the mass of a very light dark matter particle to within about a factor of ten, for masses between roughly 10^-14 and 10^-11 electronvolts.

🔍 **What it does.** The authors argue that ultralight scalar dark matter can form a collisionless, particle-like density spike around a massive black hole, because its wavelength is small compared to that black hole, while still being wave-like at the scale of the stellar-mass companion, whose absorption and dynamical-friction cross sections then depend on the particle mass. They add the resulting torque, built from wave-regime cross sections capped at their relativistic particle-limit values, to adiabatic FastEMRIWaveforms circular Schwarzschild inspirals. They also show that the inner spike profile is close to universal, up to one normalisation parameter, for initial cusp slopes 0.25 to 1.5. Full 11-parameter MCMC runs on noiseless LISA injections (M=2e5, m=18 solar masses, SNR 50, last three years before plunge) recover two-sided particle-mass posteriors for 1e-13 and 1e-12 eV. A cheaper estimate based only on the orbital-phase difference, checked against MCMC to within about 30%, then maps where in the space of halo density and particle mass an order-of-magnitude measurement is possible.

✅ **Strengths**

- A clear physical idea. The wave/particle separation of scales (de Broglie wavelength much smaller than the primary's gravitational radius but comparable to the companion's) is set out cleanly. The WKB argument for a collisionless spike, including back through adiabatic growth via action conservation, is short and convincing.
- Full 11-parameter Bayesian inference with the LISA TDI response and FastEMRIWaveforms, not just Fisher matrices. The sampler coordinates follow the curved degeneracy between particle mass and spike density, with convergence diagnostics reported.
- Honest reporting of where sensitivity ends. Below about 1e-14 eV the torque reaches an accretion-dominated plateau that does not depend on the mass, and above about 1e-11 eV the particle limit takes over. In both cases only one-sided bounds remain, and the paper says so.
- *…and 3 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Forecast only: noiseless injections, circular orbits around non-spinning black holes, SNR 50, and the spike assumed to have survived intact.
- The particle-mass sensitivity comes from a hand-made interpolation of wave-regime cross sections, plus a hand-chosen Hill-radius impact-parameter cutoff, applied in the transition region where neither limit holds.
- 'Within an order of magnitude' means 90% intervals that can span close to two decades.
- Needs light (about 1e5 solar mass) primaries and dense or steep host cusps. Heavier systems only work for optimistic halos or eight-year missions.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Methods 'Particles behaving as waves' (the cross-section prescription and the torque-versus-alpha figure; this is where the claim stands or falls), Methods 'Waveform model and data analysis setup' (adding the torque to FastEMRIWaveforms, sampling along the degeneracy, and the phase-difference likelihood shortcut), Results subsection 2 (Fig. 1 posteriors), and Methods 'Universality of the inner radial profiles' if you model spikes yourself. The Discussion is a fair summary of the astrophysical caveats.

#### 🎵 Systematic High Overtone Expansion of Kerr Quasinormal Modes

Hatsuda — [arXiv:2610.38019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38019) — gr-qc, hep-th, math-ph

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 7.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 5/10

> 💡 A new method computes the ringing frequencies of rapidly decaying vibrations of a spinning black hole as a series that can be pushed to any order, matching direct numerical calculations to about twenty-one decimal places.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper builds an all-order asymptotic expansion of Kerr quasinormal frequencies in inverse powers of a WKB integer N (closely related to the overtone number) at fixed spin, restricted to the 'normal' branches of the spheroidal eigenvalue. The large-c spheroidal eigenvalue is first expanded by a Bender-Wu-type polynomial recursion around an equatorial harmonic oscillator. This is inserted into the author's earlier 'dual' radial equation, where the WKB problem lives on a fixed genus-one curve that depends only on spin, and every higher quantum period reduces to two elliptic periods; inverting the quantization condition gives the coefficients d_j(chi) order by order. The truncated series matches direct Leaver-type roots to about 1e-21 at chi=4/5, N=120 for s=0,-1,-2, the series is factorially divergent with optimal truncation scaling with N, it agrees with the author's earlier exact-WKB treatment of extremal Kerr, and it becomes non-uniform as spin goes to zero, holding only for N much larger than chi^(-4/3).

✅ **Strengths**

- The validation is very strong: comparison against independent direct numerical roots (angular eigenvalue computed numerically, not from the series) reaches errors of 1e-21 to 1e-25, and the error first falls and then rises as expected for an asymptotic series.
- The structural result is clean and checkable: one fixed elliptic curve for all s, m, l, and every higher quantum period reduces to two elliptic periods, so the coefficients come out without any numerical fitting.
- The paper states its assumptions openly in the introduction (isospectrality of the dual equation, the all-order quantization condition, normal branches only, N not equal to the overtone number n).
- *…and 3 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Assumes, does not derive, the all-order quantization condition and the isospectrality of the author's dual radial equation.
- Normal angular branches only; m=0 and anomalous branches are excluded; numerical tests only for l=2, m=-2.
- The expansion label N is not the overtone number; the mapping is mode-dependent.
- Breaks down at small spin unless N is much larger than chi^(-4/3); poor accuracy for moderate spin and N around 40.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Introduction (last two paragraphs, for the assumptions); Section 2.2 (the polynomial recursion for the spheroidal eigenvalue at large c); Sections 3.2-3.4 (the fixed elliptic curve and the reduction to two periods); Section 4.1 with Table 1 (numerical validation); Section 3.6 if interested in the small-spin non-uniformity.

#### 🎵 Separation of variables for massive spin-2 fields on the Kerr black hole

Dias, Santos — [arXiv:2610.40780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40780) — gr-qc, hep-th

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 8/10 · 🎯 Relevance 7/10

> 💡 The equations for a massive graviton field around a spinning black hole, previously solvable only as coupled two-dimensional problems, are shown to reduce to ordinary differential equations: two commuting angular eigenvalue problems plus a single radial equation.

🔍 **What it does.** The authors take five projections of a Fierz-Pauli (massive spin-2) perturbation on Kerr, built from the field, its curl and its linearised Weyl tensor, and show they obey a closed third-order radial 'transport identity' whose obstruction term (the analogue of what blocks a Teukolsky equation for massive fields) is removed by differentiating and eliminating an auxiliary quantity. Two explicit commuting 5x5 matrix angular operators, a first-order spin ladder K1 and a coupled spin-weighted spheroidal operator K2, provide the separation; their eigenvalues are tied by a quintic 'spectral curve' F(lambda,nu)=0, which is proven as an operator identity. A quartic polynomial solution of an adjoint equation gives a first integral that removes the spurious massless-asymptotics branch, leaving one second-order radial equation (a massive spin-2 Teukolsky-like operator with apparent singularities at the zeros of the quartic). A reconstruction map back to the metric perturbation is supplied (579 coefficients, ancillary Mathematica file), with angular completeness argued; the method is applied to compute Gregory-Laflamme and superradiant growth rates for massive spin-2 fields on Kerr, equivalently Kaluza-Klein modes of the Kerr black string.

✅ **Strengths**

- Addresses a genuine, long-standing open problem: massive vector fields on Kerr were separated in 2018 (Frolov-Krtous-Kubiznak-Santos), but massive spin-2 had remained a coupled PDE problem treated only in slow rotation, small coupling, or by brute-force 2D numerics.
- The structure is explicit and checkable: K1, K2, the radial operator, the quartic A(r), and the full spectral polynomial F(lambda,nu) are written out in closed form, and the reconstruction operator is provided as an ancillary file.
- The key identities are stated as theorems valid off-shell for any symmetric h, with the Fierz-Pauli case following by setting the source to zero; [K1,K2]=0 and F(K1,K2)=0 are proven as operator identities, not inferred from eigenfunctions.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Separation into a coupled five-component angular problem with a quintic spectral curve, not a single decoupled scalar equation.
- The core identities are proven by large computer-algebra reductions; independent verification needs the ancillary files.
- The radial equation has mode-dependent apparent singularities at the zeros of a quartic polynomial.
- Angular completeness is argued; completeness of quasinormal or quasibound modes in time is not addressed.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Supplemental Material: Sec. 2.1 (explicit K1, K2 and radial operator), Sec. 4.2-4.3 (transport identity and how the Teukolsky-type obstruction is removed), Sec. 5.5 (proof that [K1,K2]=0 and F(K1,K2)=0), Sec. 6 (first integral and radial reduction), Sec. 8 (Schwarzschild limit as a sanity check). The Letter's main text for the overall logic and the growth-rate figure.

#### 💫 Ejected and Rejected: Strong Scatterings in Dense Star Clusters

Distler, Stone — [arXiv:2610.37775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37775) — astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.HE

🔦 **Worth-Skimming** · 🔬 Quality 5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 5/10

> 💡 A closed-form calculation of how often stars near a giant black hole receive single large velocity kicks suggests that a rare close encounter can knock a would-be extreme mass ratio inspiral back off its inspiral track, which would preferentially remove the least eccentric such sources.

🔍 **What it does.** The authors derive an exact two-dimensional rate distribution of velocity kicks (along and across the direction of motion) from two-body encounters in isotropic, spherical star clusters. They give its kinematic boundary and evaluate it for a King-model globular cluster and for power-law cusps around a supermassive black hole. They approximate the marginal distributions and third moments by rescaling the exactly solvable gamma = 3/2 case by density, then convert approximately to energy and angular-momentum kicks. Two applications follow. The first recovers the ejection rates of Teboul et al. and maps regimes where the 'one strong kick per orbit' assumption breaks down. The second is an order-of-magnitude estimate that proto-inspirals just inside the gravitational-wave-dominated region typically suffer one or more 'cancelling' angular-momentum kicks before they reach the LISA band.

✅ **Strengths**

- Clean, general derivation: the master formula reduces the kick-rate distribution for any isotropic distribution function that depends only on energy to a one-dimensional integral. It has a simple kinematic boundary that holds independently of the field-star distribution.
- Sanity checks done: the first and second moments reproduce standard diffusion coefficients to about 1-2 percent, the density-rescaling approximation for the marginals and third moments is checked against direct integration for gamma from 1 to 2, and ejection rates match Teboul et al.
- Analytic third moments for gamma = 3/2 (and a bounded ratio between them) may be useful to people building Fokker-Planck or Langevin codes with skewed kick distributions.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- The EMRI 'cancellation' result is a rate-times-timescale estimate at a single point, not an integration along the inspiral or a simulation.
- The suppression of low-eccentricity EMRIs is a hypothesis; no eccentricity distribution is computed.
- The kicks invoked are the large-kick tail of ordinary relaxation, and they are never compared against cumulative diffusion over the inspiral time.
- Ejection rates reproduce earlier work; the new saturation regimes are only mapped, not quantified.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Section 3.1 (Canceled EMRIs) for the new idea and its scaling argument; Section 2, up to the master formula and the kick boundary, for the formalism; the end of Section 3.2 for the remark on discrepancies in 'cliffhanger' EMRI rates between orbit-averaged and local Monte Carlo methods.

## 🗑️ Papers That Failed Critical Review

*Read in full, then rejected. Scores are the deep reader's, on the same 0-10
scale as the survivors above.*

1. **Resonance correction to time-dependent finite-size effects in Effective Field Theory of binary black hole inspiral** ([2610.38668](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38668), Avitan, Brustein) — 🔬 quality 2.5/10 · 🎯 relevance 4/10

   📣 **Claimed.** A point-particle effective field theory treatment of resonant excitation of the internal electric quadrupole of an ultra-compact object. It claims to give: explicit numerical coefficients for the time-dependent finite-size operators, the result that k2 is negative and that every finite-size term is proportional to k2, a leading 2.5PN resonant term (against 5PN for the static case), and a corrected phase-shift formula that supersedes Avitan, Brustein and Sherf (2023).

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The effective field theory is not set up consistently. The Wilson coefficients depend on the orbital separation: C_E = -(3/2) r^5, and the induced-quadrupole couplings carry 1/r^5 and 1/r^10 factors from evaluating the body's own induced field at distance r. Worldline coefficients should describe the body alone, not the orbit. The resulting oscillator frequency omega_2^2 ∝ beta/r^10 then depends on r unless beta is tuned, and the paper does this tuning silently.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The headline result that k2 is negative comes from a sign convention the authors impose ('in Lorentzian spacetime the potential term has a negative sign'), not from a derivation. The standard dynamical-tide effective action for a stable harmonic-oscillator mode (Steinhoff, Hinderer, Buonanno and Taracchini 2016) gives a positive static Love number equal to the mode-sum overlap divided by omega^2. The paper neither reconciles its result with this nor cites it in the introduction or related-work discussion.

2. **Early-time tails in Kerr black hole ringdown** ([2610.39058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39058), Gupta, Oshita) — 🔬 quality 5/10 · 🎯 relevance 5/10

   📣 **Claimed.** A quantification, as a function of Kerr spin and source frequency, of the early-time branch-cut (tail) contribution relative to the quasinormal-mode contribution at ringdown onset. The onset is defined by a fit-free reconstruction from Green's-function quantities. The paper also reports that the early-time tail waveform before convolution is almost independent of spin, up to a known time shift, so the spin dependence of the tail-to-mode ratio comes mainly from mode excitation.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The headline ratio is evaluated at the earliest time a sum of more than 100 overtones plus branch cut reproduces the waveform. At that time the branch-cut integral is not the familiar power-law tail: the paper admits it is not dominated by omega near 0. It is known (Casals and Ottewill in Schwarzschild) that at early times the large-|omega_I| part of the cut partly compensates the non-convergent high-overtone sum. So '10% tail at onset' measures one piece of a non-unique split of the early response, not a contaminant that a few-mode spectroscopy fit would actually see.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** Nothing is compared at the start times actually used in ringdown analyses (roughly 0 to 20M after peak), and nothing is compared against the fundamental mode, or a few modes, which is what spectroscopy fits. So the stated implication for black hole spectroscopy is not demonstrated.

3. **Complete quasinormal modes of Type-D black holes. II. Extremal Kerr black holes** ([2610.39095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39095), Chen, Zhang, Cao, Jing) — 🔬 quality 4/10 · 🎯 relevance 3/10

   📣 **Claimed.** A double-confluent Heun method for gravitational quasinormal modes of exactly extremal Kerr, carried across frequency branch cuts onto unconventional Riemann sheets. The paper claims to show 'for the first time' that unconventional m<0 modes continued from Schwarzschild form zero-damping sequences approaching M omega = m/2. It also claims that one overtone cutoff cannot separate finite-damping from zero-damping sequences, and that completeness holds in the contour-counting sense within a bounded frequency domain.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** Full text could not be retrieved. The outline, LaTeX source and text-search tools all failed for this id, so this assessment rests only on the abstract, the Paper I abstract and a literature check. None of the numbers, error estimates or approximations could be verified, and under the default-reject standard that alone blocks a keep.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The headline result concerns modes on 'unconventional' Riemann sheets, reached by continuing across frequency branch cuts. These are not poles of the retarded Green's function on the physical sheet, so it is unclear that they contribute to any ringdown signal. Nothing in the abstract argues that they are physically excited or observable.

4. **Quasinormal Mode Excitation in the Horizon Shear of Near-Extremal Kerr Black Holes** ([2610.39729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39729), Black) — 🔬 quality 4.5/10 · 🎯 relevance 4/10

   📣 **Claimed.** Settles an open question left by O'Sullivan and Hughes (2016), who saw high-frequency wiggles in the near-extremal horizon shear, could not tie them to the orbit, and argued they were not quasinormal modes because they were absent from the tidal field. The paper claims they are Kerr quasinormal modes on the damped-mode branch, dominated by (2,1,0) and (3,2,0), and that the wiggles are in fact present in the tidal driving field.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** Novelty is limited. Thornburg, Wardell and van de Meent (2020) had already shown that post-periapsis wiggles in eccentric extreme-mass-ratio orbits are generic Kerr quasinormal-mode excitations, seen in radiation at infinity, in the self-force and in 'field perturbations at fixed spatial positions'. Finding the same thing in a horizon quantity is the expected outcome of applying a standard ringdown-fitting method to a slightly different observable.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The whole study uses one orbit (p = 3M, e = 0.85, equatorial prograde), one viewing angle and five closely spaced spins. Nothing is said about how the excitation depends on orbital parameters, and the stated near-extremal motivation is not used: the identified modes are damped-branch modes whose frequencies barely change between a/M = 0.9999 and 0.99999.
