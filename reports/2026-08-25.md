**💫 Resonance crossings as entire functions of the Koopman operator**
Canizares — [arXiv:2608.21193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21193) — gr-qc, astro-ph.SR, math.DS
A single-author methods paper argues that the long-standing blow-up of averaging schemes when an inspiralling orbit passes through a resonance disappears if you rewrite the averaging step as a well-behaved function of the operator that advances the orbit by one radial cycle, and it checks this on a stirred-up model orbit around a spinning black hole.
The paper recasts orbit averaging for extreme mass-ratio inspirals in the language of the Koopman operator: the waveform is treated as a function on phase space, and evolution over one radial period becomes a linear operator U_Lambda whose eigenvalues are exp(i k.Upsilon Lambda). In this language the standard near-identity transformation is the operator (1 - U_Lambda)^{-1}, which is meromorphic and has a pole exactly where an eigenvalue hits unity, i.e. at a resonance; the proposed alternative applies log U_Lambda, evaluated as a finite-window (Magnus) integral, which is entire and therefore finite at a crossing. The first-order coefficient is chi_k = G_k Lambda exp(i delta Lambda/2) sinc(delta Lambda/2), giving the uniform bound |chi_k| <= |G_k| Lambda, saturated at the crossing. The scheme is demonstrated on a Kerr geodesic (a = 0.9M, e = 0.2) driven through the 3:2 resonance by a toy drag force and by a tidal perturbation, recovering the known stationary-phase jump amplitude 2 sqrt(pi) (measured 3.52 +/- 0.09) and its cosine dependence on the resonant phase, plus a planar post-Newtonian off-resonance benchmark and a second-order Magnus coefficient reduced to a physical-space integral.
Quality 4.5/10 · Relevance 8/10 · **Must-Read**
*Caveats:* The demonstration uses an ad hoc drag force and a tidal perturbation, never a gravitational self-force; the inspiral sweep rate is prescribed by hand.; No inspiral trajectory or waveform phase is computed through a crossing with a quantified error against a resolved reference, so the scheme's accuracy scaling at a resonance is unmeasured.; The comparison with the existing partial-averaging switching scheme is, by the author's own statement, structural rather than numerical; the two 'cannot yet be ranked' and the cost-at-matched-accuracy question is deferred to future work.; The headline '1 part in 1e6' figure is a numerical confirmation that sinc(0) = 1 -- it validates the quadrature, not a physical prediction.; The 'no resonance need be located' claim sits uneasily with the paper's own procedure: crossings are found by bisection, and recovering the resonant jump needs a window of two crossing widths rather than the single radial period the bounded coefficient is defined at.; The off-resonance benchmark is an equal-mass (nu = 1/4) planar post-Newtonian binary with no resonances by symmetry; on a realistic dense-grid waveform estimator the method loses to plain orbit averaging above mass ratio of roughly 2e-6.; The core finite-window result is credited in the paper itself to Blanco (2026) at first order; the new content is the operator language, the second-order extension, and the Kerr toy demonstration.
*Where to start:* Sec. 2.2.1 (operator reframing, the one durable idea), Eq. (2.2.2) and the surrounding text on the exact chi_k / w_k relation, Sec. 3.4 (the crossing-separation scaling argument), Sec. 3.5 (the caveats, which are the honest summary of the paper's standing), and App. 11 (reduction of the second Magnus coefficient). The boundedness figures and Table I can be skimmed.

**🎵 Amplification of metric perturbations by extremal horizons**
Gralla, Salamat — [arXiv:2608.23878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23878) — gr-qc
A calculation shows that if matter outside a maximally spinning black hole circulates exactly in step with the horizon, an observer falling in is stretched far more strongly than normal right as they cross, instead of feeling nothing special.
The paper studies perturbations of extremal (maximally spinning and/or charged) Kerr-Newman black holes that co-rotate with the horizon, i.e. have frequency exactly equal to the azimuthal number times the horizon angular velocity. For such modes the near-horizon radial behaviour becomes a power law in the distance from the horizon, and for the so-called principal modes the exponent has real part -1/2, which the authors show corresponds to a perturbation that grows towards the horizon rather than being screened. Working with nearly co-rotating modes of detuning k to keep the boundary conditions well posed, they show the retarded Green function grows as k^{-1/2}, so the tidal deformation and the energy kick experienced by an infalling observer formally diverge as exact co-rotation is approached. They then compute numerically, using a spectral eigenvalue method for the coupled gravitational-electromagnetic angular equations, the full spectrum of near-horizon scaling exponents of extremal Kerr-Newman as a function of charge, finding principal (complex) exponents for Q < Q_* = 0.9329 M and infinitely many of them below Q = sqrt(3)/2 M, and use the same exponents to predict late-time Aretakis growth and decay rates on and off the horizon.
Quality 7/10 · Relevance 5/10 · **Must-Read**
*Caveats:* The divergence is formal: the paper itself notes the metric perturbation scales like the tidal deformation, so 'infinite deformation' means linear perturbation theory has failed, not that a physical observer is torn apart.; No amplitude is ever computed. The result is a k^{-1/2} enhancement of an unspecified source strength, and the flagship Kerr example needs a sixth-order eccentric harmonic whose intrinsic weakness is never weighed against the enhancement.; Exact extremality is required throughout; there is no near-extremal cutoff estimate, and no radiation reaction, so the co-rotating source must be assumed to persist unchanged forever.; The critical charges 0.9329 M and sqrt(3)/2 M reproduce values already obtained by Dias, Godazgar and Santos (2109.13949); the paper says so, but the abstract reads as if they are new.; The Kerr-Newman Aretakis decay and growth rates are conjectural extrapolations from Kerr, explicitly unverified, and are presented next to computed numbers.; The independent Green-function check for Kerr-Newman covers two parameter points in one spin sector at about three-digit accuracy.
*Where to start:* Section 1 for the framing against the Horowitz et al. singular-extremal-horizon programme; Section 3 for the complete Kerr argument, including Eq. (16) for the overlap-region Green function and the resonant-orbit construction at its end; Section 4.1 (skim the tables, read the Q_* and Q_infinity discussion and Figs. 6-8); Section 5.1 for the honest translation into other people's results; Section 7 for the comparison of driven amplification with the ordinary Aretakis instability, which is the clearest statement of what is new. Appendix A is short and worth reading for the action-angle Vlasov trick. Appendix C can be skipped unless you care about the dynamic-mode-decomposition power extraction.

**🎵 Gravitational Waves from Green's Function Decomposition for a Kerr black hole: I. Equatorial ISCO Plunge**
Su, Khera, Chowdhuri, Casals et al. — [arXiv:2608.17943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17943) — gr-qc
The authors split the response of a spinning black hole into a prompt part, a ringing part and a slowly fading part, and use it to separate the wave emitted by a small body falling into the hole, while finding that the spin introduces a new mathematical obstruction that prevents them from pinning down exactly when one part ends and the next begins.
The paper carries the authors' own Schwarzschild G+/G- Green's-function decomposition over to the Teukolsky equation on Kerr, building the radial Green's function from MST and Jaffe series at high precision and analytically continuing the spin-weighted spheroidal harmonics in the complex frequency plane. They find a genuinely new structural feature absent in Schwarzschild: the spheroidal eigenvalue produces angular branch cuts, so the causal split time for an isolated (l,m) mode is not fixed by the analysis and is instead set operationally to the Schwarzschild value |r_*| + |r'_*|, with an unresolved window of width O(a) around it. Fixed-source kernels built from the direct-part contour plus 42 QNM poles are compared with an independent 2+1 time-domain Teukolsky evolution at a = 0.4M and track it away from the causal fronts. The headline application, the (2,2) waveform of a finite-start equatorial ISCO plunge at a/M = 0.1, 0.4, 0.7, is not actually computed from the contour decomposition: it is a real-frequency inverse Fourier transform that is then time-windowed by causal indicators, yielding a direct-part contribution that terminates exactly at the retarded time at which the particle crosses r'_* = 0.
Quality 6/10 · Relevance 6.5/10 · **Must-Read**
*Caveats:* The abstract says the split times 'are determined by the black hole spin and positions'; the body says the opposite - in Kerr the split time is set by hand to the Schwarzschild value, with an O(a)-wide window where the analysis breaks down.; The plunge waveform is not computed from the decomposed Green's function. It is a real-frequency inverse Fourier transform, cut into pieces by causal indicator functions whose validity for a single (l,m) mode in Kerr is exactly what the paper leaves open.; 'Excellent agreement' with the time-domain code is asserted but never quantified; elsewhere the same comparison is described as qualitative and visual.; The late-time tail is never evaluated, and neither are the angular-cut jump integrals; 'QNM+Tail' means 'whatever is left after the front'.; One mode, one trajectory family, three moderate spins, no high-spin case.; This is a v1 with draft figure placeholders, revision-colour markup and internal co-author comments still in the source; expect the text to change.
**In network:** 🚩 Huan Yang (notable author)
*Where to start:* Sec. 3.2 and Appendix on the angular cuts and the large-frequency split time in Kerr (the actual new structure); the split-time paragraphs of the Introduction, which contain the honest version of the abstract's claim; Sec. 4.3 and Sec. 5, especially Eq. (real-axis-time-split), for what the 'decomposition' of the plunge waveform actually is; Sec. 6.2 for the direct-part termination time. Sec. 3.5 and the appendices on MST/Jaffe implementation are worth skimming for numerical technique.

### 🌠 Should-Read
**∞ Kerr Black Hole Uniqueness**
Han, Khuri, Weinstein, Xiong — [arXiv:2608.23325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23325) — gr-qc, math-ph, math.DG
A new proof shows that two or more rotating black holes can never sit at rest next to each other in empty space, closing a fifty-year-old gap and leaving the single spinning black hole of Kerr as the only possible stationary vacuum black hole.
The paper attacks the long-standing 'balance problem': whether a stationary, axially symmetric vacuum spacetime can hold several black holes in equilibrium without a strut or conical singularity holding them apart. Working in the standard Weyl-Papapetrou reduction, where the field equations become a singular harmonic map into the hyperbolic plane and the remaining metric function alpha is recovered by quadrature, the authors prove two new facts about alpha: a global upper bound coming from a scalar-curvature identity plus a maximum principle, and a strict local lower bound near any bounded axis rod. Combining them forces the logarithmic angle defect to be strictly negative on every finite axis rod, so the interaction force between neighbouring horizons is always attractive and no regular multi-horizon configuration exists. The result holds for any finite number of horizons with any mixture of degenerate and non-degenerate components, provided every degenerate component carries nonzero angular momentum, and it yields the unconditional mass-angular-momentum inequality m >= sqrt(|J|) for multiple black holes as a corollary via the authors' companion paper.
Quality 8.5/10 · Relevance 5/10 · **Should-Read**
*Caveats:* Preprint v1, unrefereed, and it makes a headline claim on a fifty-year-old open problem; treat the result as strong but provisional until it has been checked.; What is proved is the axisymmetric stationary vacuum case. Full Kerr uniqueness for merely stationary spacetimes still requires analyticity plus I^+-regularity to get axisymmetry from Hawking rigidity, and finitely many horizon components; the title does not say this, the introduction does.; Degenerate horizon components are assumed to have nonzero angular momentum. This is automatic for regular vacuum degenerate horizons, but it is a hypothesis of the theorem.; The heavy analytic input (puncture, pole and infinity asymptotics) is imported from the authors' companion papers rather than proved here.; Large parts of the manuscript are disabled inside LaTeX comment blocks, and in Case III of Section 5 this removes the degree-counting justification the argument relies on. The explicit geometric Kerr-uniqueness corollary is also commented out.
*Where to start:* Introduction (rod structure, angle defects, and the proof outline); Section 3.1 Lemma 3.1 (the new identity and differential inequality, one page and the crux of the paper); Section 3.3 (global upper bound via maximum principle on exhaustion domains); Section 6 (short assembly of the main theorem). Section 5 only if verifying the technical core.

The first solution of the gravitational Kerr quasinormal-mode eigenproblem with a purely coordinate-space (non-spectral) physics-informed neural network on the two-dimensional hyperboloidal Teukolsky operator, with no separation ansatz, no separation constant, no Leaver-type asymptotic prefactor, no analytic boundary mask, no Chebyshev basis and no differentiation matrix, offered as a prototype for beyond-Kerr backgrounds whose perturbation equations do not separate. Weakness: No new physics and no new numbers: the entire output is a lower-accuracy reproduction of Kerr frequencies known to ten or more digits since Leaver (1985), and the paper never applies the method to a single non-separable or beyond-Kerr background, which is its sole stated motivation. Weakness: The continuum formulation, including the hyperboloidal radial-fixing operator coefficients reproduced in Appendix A, is taken wholesale from Assaad and Panosso Macedo (2025), who already posed and solved this identical 2D eigenvalue problem with a Chebyshev spectral method at far higher precision; the contribution reduces to swapping the discretisation. 2. **Quasi-periodic Eruptions from Recurrent Satellite Black Hole Transits through Magnetized Galactic Nucleus Accretion Disks** ([2608.19796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19796), Wang, Xing, Liu, Li et al.). FAIL — did not survive the deep read. A two-channel model in which a single satellite-black-hole disk transit powers both the soft X-ray eruption (via drag-heated, optically thick ejecta) and a physically distinct, delayed ultraviolet counterpart (via in-disk magnetic reconnection followed by photon diffusion), in contrast to single-ejecta shock-cooling models where both bands come from the same material; the paper claims this naturally accommodates both the Ansky ultraviolet lag and the non-detection of ultraviolet counterparts elsewhere. Weakness: The paper's headline target quantity, the ~1-day ultraviolet lag, is not reproduced. Their own Eq. (10) gives t_diff ~ 6.6x10^5 s, i.e. about 7.6 days, roughly eight times the measured 0.96(+0.38/-0.39) days. This is rescued only by asserting that the true deposition site is shallower or that shocks carve a low-density channel, with no calculation of how much shallower or how much column is removed. The central claim is therefore accommodated, not derived. Weakness: Serious internal inconsistency in the Ansky application. To stretch the X-ray duration to ~10^5 s the authors need a 'sufficiently low orbital inclination' (t_ej scales as sin(i/2)^-5/2, requiring roughly i ~ 4 degrees down from the fiducial 18 degrees). But the reconnection luminosities scale as sin(i/2)^4 (poloidal) and sin(i/2)^2 (toroidal), so at that inclination L_th,pol collapses by more than two orders of magnitude. The quoted ultraviolet powers are evaluated at the fiducial inclination, not at the inclination required for Ansky's X-ray duration. The two channels are never evaluated at a consistent parameter point for the one source the paper claims to explain. (Quality 3.5/10, relevance 4/10.)
3. **Bayesian P-spline recovery of stochastic gravitational-wave backgrounds in LISA** ([2608.20629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20629), Aimen, Maturana-Russel, Vajpeyi, Christensen et al.). FAIL — did not survive the deep read. Extension of the authors' earlier noise-only Bayesian P-spline spectral estimator to joint noise-plus-signal inference for LISA; a fixed-dimension, roughness-penalised spline framework that avoids reversible-jump MCMC while remaining flexible; a systematic four-way comparison of parametric versus flexible signal models crossed with wide versus informed test-mass noise priors, mapped as Bayes-factor detection boundaries over 180 injections; and a demonstration on a non-power-law (Gaussian bump) injection that a flexible signal model prevents the unmodelled feature from being absorbed by the flexible noise model. Weakness: The two headline detectability conclusions -- that the noise prior matters more than the signal-model choice, and that a flexible noise model with separate test-mass and optical-metrology transfer functions degrades low-frequency detectability -- were already obtained by Karnesis, Baghi, Bayle and Galanis (arXiv:2601.19741) seven months earlier, with more realistic instrument simulations. The paper says so itself. What remains genuinely new is a change of spline machinery (fixed-knot penalised B-splines instead of trans-dimensional Akima splines) plus one demonstration figure. Weakness: The one genuinely new demonstration, the Gaussian bump, is run at signal-to-noise ratio 853.6. That is an enormously loud injection; showing that a flexible model tracks a feature that dominates the periodogram near its peak is not a stress test. No bump result is reported at the marginal signal-to-noise ratios (around 10-30) where the power-law study lives, and no Bayes factors are quoted for the bump at all, so the claim that flexibility 'prevents bias' is not quantified anywhere. (Quality 5.5/10, relevance 3/10.)
4. **Extreme mass-ratio inspirals around rotating accelerating black holes** ([2608.22249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22249), Du, Zhou, Xiong, Zi et al.). FAIL — did not survive the deep read. The first computation of gravitational radiation and adiabatic inspiral evolution for an extreme mass-ratio system around a rotating accelerating (spinning C-metric) black hole: a small-acceleration Teukolsky framework with a derived point-particle source, a regular angular solution, Sasaki-Nakamura radial solutions, an intermediate-wave-zone flux prescription that circumvents the absence of asymptotic flatness, a near-equatorial circular-orbit family with a coupled radial-polar stability analysis, and the resulting order-radian secular dephasing. Weakness: The headline effect is largely a comparison convention. By their own Eq. Weakness: Waveforms are compared at fixed initial ORBITAL RADIUS, not fixed initial frequency. This puts a constant fractional frequency offset 2AM at t = 0, which integrates linearly.
