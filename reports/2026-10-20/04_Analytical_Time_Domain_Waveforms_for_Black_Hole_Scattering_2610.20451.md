# 🌀 Analytical Time-Domain Waveforms for Black-Hole Scattering in the Self-Force Expansion

🌟 **Must-Read** · 🔬 Quality 7/10 · 🎯 Relevance 6/10

📎 **Citation:** Chris Kavanagh, Julio Parra-Martinez, Davide Usseglio, *Analytical Time-Domain Waveforms for Black-Hole Scattering in the Self-Force Expansion*, arXiv:[2610.20451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20451) [gr-qc, hep-th], submitted 14 Oct 2026.

> 💡 A regularisation trick and a single family of hypergeometric integrals let the authors write down the gravitational-wave signal in time from a small body flying past a black hole to far higher accuracy than before, together with the energy and angular momentum it radiates.

## 🔍 Executive Summary

The paper takes the gravitational waveform at first order in the mass ratio for a small body on an unbound (hyperbolic) geodesic past a non-spinning black hole, expanded jointly in weak field (post-Minkowskian) and low velocity (post-Newtonian), and converts it from the frequency domain to the time domain. Order by order, the inverse Fourier transform diverges because unbound orbits have a continuous spectrum. The authors fix this with an analytic regulator in the spirit of dimensional regularisation and show that every term then reduces to one integral family generated by the Lauricella function F_D^(3). They handle that family with Feynman-integral methods: integration-by-parts reduction to four master integrals, then a first-order differential equation in canonical form with letters {0, +i, -i}, whose solution is harmonic polylogarithms of argument -iy. As a demonstration they produce the quadrupole waveform to O(G^10 v^42), meaning 10th post-Minkowskian and 20th post-Newtonian order, and the full l=2..22 waveform to 10PM-10PN, and from these the radiated energy, the radiated angular momentum (with the static memory contribution restored) and the dissipative scattering angle.

## 📣 Claimed Contribution

An all-orders structural description of the 1SF time-domain scattering waveform: a dimensional-regularisation-inspired prescription that makes the order-by-order inverse Fourier transform finite everywhere, and the result that the whole post-Minkowskian/post-Newtonian waveform comes from one Lauricella F_D^(3) integral family. That family is solved by integration-by-parts reduction and differential equations, which pushes analytic scattering waveforms 'multiple orders beyond the state of the art' in both expansions, along with fluxes and the dissipative scattering angle.

## ✅ Strengths

- Turns what had been a growing collection of special Fourier integrals into a closed algebraic problem. The four-master basis, the explicit residue matrices and the path-ordered solution in harmonic polylogarithms (Supplemental Material) are concrete, checkable and reusable.
- Clear jump in reach. The closest prior analytic 1SF scattering waveforms (Geralico 2026) reach 5PM-7PN in the time domain and 8PM-6PN in the frequency domain; this paper gives 10PM-10PN for all multipoles up to l=22 and 10PM-20PN for the quadrupole.
- Handles the angular-momentum loss carefully: it restores the constant incoming strain and argues that the resulting shear-memory boundary term is the full static loss at leading self-force order. Leaving this out is a common way to get the dissipative angle wrong.
- Imports Feynman-integral technology into black-hole perturbation theory in a way that plausibly carries over to conservative 1SF scattering, as the authors say they plan.
- The acknowledgements mention numerical comparisons with Niels Warburton, so there is apparently an independent check against numerical self-force data. I could not see its details (see weaknesses).

## ⚠️ Weaknesses

- The retrieval tools exposed only the conclusions and the two supplemental appendices of this letter, not the main text. The regulator's definition, the main formula (Eq. GF_main), the validation and the comparisons with prior results were not available to me. This assessment rests on the abstract, the conclusions, the appendices and the literature check.
- The regulator that makes the inverse Fourier transform finite is only 'inspired' by dimensional regularisation. The authors defer its derivation from the D=4-2epsilon perturbation equations to a forthcoming paper, so for now the central step is a prescription justified by its results, not derived.
- The physics is a geodesic source in a non-spinning background at first order in the mass ratio. There is no conservative self-force, no second order and (as far as the accessible text shows) no Kerr. The new content is reach and structure, not new physical effects.
- Results are double expansions truncated at 10PM. The letter gives no statement of how fast the series converge, or what they buy, for realistic strong-field encounters, where numerical self-force scattering already works.
- The 'beyond the state of the art' framing rests largely on the same 1SF frequency-domain input that Geralico has been extending month by month. The conceptual step is the time-domain integral family, not the waveform data as such.
- Letter format: important details are pushed into supplemental material and an announced follow-up, so reproducing the results from this paper alone is likely hard.

## 🤔 Skeptic's Cross-Examination

The divergences being 'cured' come from expanding a smooth time-domain signal in PN/PM order by order, and the cure is a regulator the authors have not yet derived. Without the promised D-dimensional derivation, how do we know the regulated finite parts are the physical waveform rather than a scheme-dependent artefact differing by local-in-time or memory-like terms? Agreement with the lower-order results and the numerical comparison is the only safeguard, and the letter, as far as I could access it, does not spell out how strong that test is at the new orders.

## 🆕 Novelty in Context

The closest prior work is Andrea Geralico's 2026 series on the same problem with the same 1SF Teukolsky/MST-type input: the frequency-domain waveform to 5PM-6PN (arXiv:2603.11774, PRL), extended to 8PM-6PN (2607.26614), and the time-domain waveform to 5PM-7PN (2609.30118, posted three weeks before this paper). Geralico reports that from O(G^5) the Fourier master integrals proliferate into new families that obey inhomogeneous Bessel equations. This paper's real contribution is to replace that case-by-case growth with one regulated Lauricella F_D^(3) family solved by integration-by-parts reduction and differential equations, which lets it reach 10PM-10PN, and 10PM-20PN for the quadrupole. The claim of going 'multiple orders beyond the state of the art' is therefore accurate in PM/PN reach for 1SF, but it is an advance in reach and technique within an established 1SF scattering programme, not a first time-domain 1SF scattering waveform; the time-domain waveform itself already exists at lower order. Using integration-by-parts and differential-equation methods for waveform Fourier transforms also has precedent in the amplitudes literature (Brunello, De Angelis, Kosower: 'Fourier calculus from intersection theory' and the analytic one-loop waveform, arXiv:2511.05412), though there in the post-Minkowskian setting at arbitrary mass ratio and low PM order. The memory/static angular-momentum treatment builds directly on Georgoudis, Goncalves, Heissenberg and Parra-Martinez (2506.20733) and on Riva et al.

## 🎯 Relevance to Your Research

Moderately high for a self-force and perturbation-theory reader. It is the scattering (unbound) counterpart of the 1SF waveform programme, and the method, which recasts black-hole perturbation integrals as a Feynman-integral family solved by integration-by-parts reduction and canonical differential equations, could be borrowed for other analytic black-hole perturbation calculations, including post-Newtonian-expanded bound-orbit fluxes. It does not touch Kerr, spin, extreme-mass-ratio inspiral data analysis or integrability directly. Read the Supplemental Material on the Lauricella expansion for the method, and the flux/dissipative-angle appendix for the memory-corrected angular-momentum loss.

📖 **Where to start:** Supplemental Material, 'Expansion of Lauricella Function around integer arguments' (the core technique: integration-by-parts reduction to four masters, residue matrices, harmonic-polylogarithm solution), and 'Asymptotic fluxes and dissipative scattering angle' (the static angular-momentum loss via memory). In the main text, the part that defines the regulator and Eq. GF_main, and any comparison with Geralico's 5PM-7PN time-domain results and with numerical self-force data.

## 🚧 Caveats

- First order in the mass ratio on a geodesic source only; no conservative self-force.
- The regulator that makes the Fourier transforms finite is a prescription here; its derivation is promised in a future paper.
- Non-spinning black hole only, as far as the accessible text shows.
- Truncated weak-field/low-velocity series; no strong-field accuracy statement.
- The main text of the letter could not be retrieved for this review; the assessment rests on the appendices, conclusions and literature check.
