# 🎵 Dynamical quasinormal mode excitation II: propagation and convergence in Schwarzschild

**arXiv:** [2605.16492](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16492)
**Authors:** Marina De Amicis, Enrico Cannizzaro, Gregorio Carullo, Adrien Kuntz, Laura Sberna
**Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th, math-ph
**Collaborator flags:** Sberna (T2)

## Summary

The authors study the dynamical excitation of quasinormal modes during the plunge of a particle into a Schwarzschild black hole, building on their Paper I framework. By investigating the high-frequency behaviour of Leaver's QNM solutions, they obtain a more accurate prescription for QNM propagation, confirming the existence of a "bounce radius" $r_* = 0$ as a characteristic radius for QNM excitation. To the right of this radius the QNM signal scatters off the bounce point before reaching the observer; to the left it propagates directly on the light cone. Applied to inspiralling particles, the formalism reproduces the oscillatory waveform component after the bounce crossing to high accuracy, providing an essentially complete first-principles description of the ringdown waveform from shortly after the signal peak.

## Key Results

- Refined prescription for QNM-mode propagation using high-frequency behaviour of Leaver's solutions.
- Confirmation of the bounce radius $r_*=0$ as a characteristic QNM excitation point; division of the spatial region into pre-bounce (light-cone propagation) and post-bounce (scattering) regimes.
- Plunge waveform reproduced from a first-principles QNM-sum after the signal peak — claimed essentially complete.
- Discovery of a regime transition during plunge: from "quasi-resonant" (overtones in counter-phase, destructive interference) to "free-oscillator" (overtones in phase, fast convergence of the QNM sum). The bounce-radius crossing triggers this transition.

## Strengths

- Genuine sequel that adds substantial new content over Paper I, not a re-packaging: the high-frequency propagation prescription, the bounce-radius characterization, and the convergence transition are all new.
- The convergence-transition result (quasi-resonant → free-oscillator) is a real structural insight about why overtone sums behave the way they do during plunge — it explains, rather than merely describes, the empirical observation that QNM sums converge slowly near the peak and rapidly later.
- First-principles plunge waveform reconstruction from QNM sums is a non-trivial benchmark.
- The bounce-radius picture is consistent with the recent McMaken-Hamilton and Panosso Macedo "hyperboloidal" perspectives on BH perturbation theory.

## Weaknesses / Caveats

- Restricted to Schwarzschild; the natural physical case (Kerr plunge) is left for future work.
- Particle on a plunging geodesic, no self-force / no back-reaction; this is a Teukolsky-equation source problem, not a full second-order treatment.
- The "essentially complete first-principles description" claim is for the post-peak waveform; the pre-peak / merger-peak portion is still not first-principles.
- Connection to the algebraically-special-frequency anomalies (cf. 2605.17840 in the same week) is not discussed but is potentially relevant.
- "Paper II" format means the paper depends heavily on Paper I; standalone readability is limited.

## Novelty Assessment

This is structurally interesting work. The "bounce radius" $r_*=0$ as a characteristic radius for QNM excitation is not new in the abstract (recent literature on hyperboloidal slicing and on McMaken-Hamilton matched expansions has identified this) but the authors connect it concretely to QNM-sum convergence behaviour during a real plunge waveform — and the quasi-resonant → free-oscillator transition is, I believe, new. The application to plunging-particle waveforms produces a quantitatively accurate reconstruction, which raises the paper above the level of formal analysis. This is genuine first-principles ringdown work rather than yet another QNM-on-exotic-background computation.

The paper is well-aligned with the recent trend in the ringdown community (Carullo, Sberna, De Amicis, Cannizzaro, Panosso Macedo, Macedo et al.) toward putting QNM excitation on a rigorous footing, and it advances that program meaningfully. Connection to the dynamical resonant-excitation picture in 2605.17840 (Kubota-Motohashi) is suggestive — both papers point toward QNM dynamics being controlled by analytic-structure features of the Green's function.
