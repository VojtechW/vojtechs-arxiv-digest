# 🎯 Evidence for Multiple Orbiting Hotspots in the 340 GHz Variability of Sgr A*

**arXiv:** [2605.11698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11698)
**Authors:** Kazuki Yanagisawa, Tomoharu Oka, Tatsuya Kotani, Ryo Ariyama, Kazuki Yanagihara, Yuhei Iwata
**Categories:** astro-ph.HE
**Collaborator flags:** none

## Summary

The authors analyze 11 epochs of archival ALMA 340 GHz data of Sgr A*. One epoch shows light-curve components with characteristic timescales of $\sim30$ min and $\sim50$ min — comparable to the ISCO orbital period for Sgr A*. The periodogram peaks are highly significant against white noise but drop below $3\sigma$ once red noise is properly included, so they explicitly do not claim a periodic detection. They argue instead that the variability is well described by a model of multiple orbiting hotspots with decaying emission, which can produce both apparent quasi-periodicity and the broader non-periodic variability, providing a unified phenomenological framework for Sgr A* millimeter variability.

## Key Results

- Identification of $\sim30$ min and $\sim50$ min variability components in a single epoch — at the ISCO orbital scale for Sgr A*.
- Red-noise corrected significance is below $3\sigma$; the authors do NOT claim a periodic detection (this is an unusual and welcome admission).
- Multiple-hotspot-with-decaying-emission model reproduces both the phase-dependent structure and amplitude evolution of the variability.
- Proposed unified framework where periodic and non-periodic Sgr A* mm variability share an orbiting-hotspot origin.

## Strengths

- Explicit, honest treatment of red-noise statistics — the field is plagued by claims of significant periodicities that vanish under proper noise modeling, and this paper steers clear of that trap.
- The unifying interpretation (periodic and non-periodic variability from the same orbiting-hotspot population) is parsimonious and physically motivated.
- Multi-epoch ALMA data at 340 GHz is the right tool for this question.

## Weaknesses / Caveats

- The "interesting" timescales come from a single epoch; the result is therefore an existence proof of consistency with hotspot models, not a robust population-level statement.
- The hotspot model is phenomenological; no GRMHD comparison, no actual orbit fitting to extract spin or inclination.
- No quantitative comparison to the existing literature on Sgr A* hotspots (GRAVITY infrared flares, EHT, MAD/SANE simulations). The "unified framework" claim is qualitative.
- No new physical insight into accretion-flow dynamics; the paper is observational fitting, not modelling.

## Novelty Assessment

The novelty is observational: a particular ALMA epoch shows variability at ISCO timescales consistent with multiple orbiting hotspots, and the authors are explicit that this is not a periodicity detection. The honesty about red-noise statistics is a real virtue in a field that has produced many false QPO claims for Sgr A*. However, the orbiting-hotspot picture itself is well-established (Broderick-Loeb, GRAVITY collaboration, EHTC), and the unified periodic+non-periodic framework is more of a re-statement than a new result. As an observational data point for the hotspot model in mm wavelengths, it is useful; as a theoretical/structural advance, it is modest.

## Relevance to Vojtěch

Tangentially relevant. Vojtěch's interest in orbiting hotspots is mostly through the EMRI / QPE / stellar-transit lens, where the hotspot is treated as a luminous body orbiting a SMBH. This paper is the inverse use case: hotspots as features of the accretion flow itself. The observational signature shape (decaying emission from multiple orbiting features at ISCO scales) does inform what kind of accretion-flow background a real EMRI counterpart would have to compete against in Sgr A*. Worth knowing as part of the broader Galactic Centre variability landscape, but not a paper Vojtěch needs to read closely.
