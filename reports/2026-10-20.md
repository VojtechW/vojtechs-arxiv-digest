#### ☁️ Modeling extreme mass-ratio inspirals in astrophysical and dark-matter environments

Li, Kuan, Weller, Yang et al. — [arXiv:2610.22480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22480) — gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

🌟 **Must-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 8/10

> 💡 For a small body spiralling into a spinning black hole surrounded by a light dark-matter field cloud, the cloud's effect on the gravitational waves and on the orbit itself shifts the phase about ten times more than the previously computed leakage of energy into the field alone.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper uses the modified Teukolsky formalism (perturbation equations for the Weyl scalars, sourced by the deformed background, the matter stress-energy and the particle) to compute order zeta^2 epsilon corrections to the gravitational-wave fluxes for circular, equatorial inspirals into Kerr. The black hole is surrounded by a synchronized dipolar complex scalar cloud with mu M = 0.3 and a/M about 0.877. The background metric deformation comes from a numerically constructed Einstein-Klein-Gordon hairy black hole, linearised in the cloud amplitude. The particle's metric perturbation is a Lorenz-gauge reconstruction, and the scalar response is taken from the authors' earlier work. For a cloud of 1e-4 of the black-hole mass, with M = 1e6 solar masses and mass ratio 1e-5, the combined adiabatic dephasing after two years is about -57.3 rad, against -6.14 rad when only the scalar flux is included. Most of the effect comes from the background-deformation terms, with the gravitational-flux and conservative pieces partially cancelling.

✅ **Strengths**

- Real technical step: this is the first computation of the gravitational-sector environmental corrections (Psi_0 and Psi_4 at order zeta^2 epsilon) around a rotating primary, with a fully numerical, non-perturbative-in-spin hairy black-hole background. Previous Kerr cloud work kept only the scalar channel.
- The physics result is useful and robust in kind: including only the matter response gives the wrong leading environmental imprint, because terms from the background deformation of the metric dominate.
- The horizon flux formula is properly re-derived for a non-Kerr, non-vacuum background through horizon area change and optical scalars, instead of the Kerr formula being reused.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Single fiducial configuration: one dipolar cloud, mu M = 0.3, a/M about 0.877, cloud mass 1e-4 M.
- The breakdown into 'flux' and 'conservative' contributions is taken at fixed coordinate radius, so it is gauge-dependent. Only the total dephasing is physical.
- The -57 rad dephasing is never tested for degeneracy with the black-hole mass or other binary parameters, and there is no mismatch or bias study.
- Circular, equatorial, adiabatic only. Environmental flux corrections are truncated at l <= 5, with up to about 5% sensitivity to input-mode cutoffs.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Section 2 (modified Teukolsky equations for environments); Section 4 (fluxes, especially the modified horizon flux formula, Eq. flux_schematic); Section 5 and Methods 8.4 (orbital evolution and frequency matching); Supplemental 9.4 (hairy black-hole background and perturbativity checks) and 9.7 (breakdown by source sector and convergence).

#### 🌀 Analytical Time-Domain Waveforms for Black-Hole Scattering in the Self-Force Expansion

Kavanagh, Parra-Martinez, Usseglio — [arXiv:2610.20451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20451) — gr-qc, hep-th

🌟 **Must-Read** · 🔬 Quality 7/10 · 🎯 Relevance 6/10

> 💡 A regularisation trick and a single family of hypergeometric integrals let the authors write down the gravitational-wave signal in time from a small body flying past a black hole to far higher accuracy than before, together with the energy and angular momentum it radiates.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper takes the gravitational waveform at first order in the mass ratio for a small body on an unbound (hyperbolic) geodesic past a non-spinning black hole, expanded jointly in weak field (post-Minkowskian) and low velocity (post-Newtonian), and converts it from the frequency domain to the time domain. Order by order, the inverse Fourier transform diverges because unbound orbits have a continuous spectrum. The authors fix this with an analytic regulator in the spirit of dimensional regularisation and show that every term then reduces to one integral family generated by the Lauricella function F_D^(3). They handle that family with Feynman-integral methods: integration-by-parts reduction to four master integrals, then a first-order differential equation in canonical form with letters {0, +i, -i}, whose solution is harmonic polylogarithms of argument -iy. As a demonstration they produce the quadrupole waveform to O(G^10 v^42), meaning 10th post-Minkowskian and 20th post-Newtonian order, and the full l=2..22 waveform to 10PM-10PN, and from these the radiated energy, the radiated angular momentum (with the static memory contribution restored) and the dissipative scattering angle.

✅ **Strengths**

- Turns what had been a growing collection of special Fourier integrals into a closed algebraic problem. The four-master basis, the explicit residue matrices and the path-ordered solution in harmonic polylogarithms (Supplemental Material) are concrete, checkable and reusable.
- Clear jump in reach. The closest prior analytic 1SF scattering waveforms (Geralico 2026) reach 5PM-7PN in the time domain and 8PM-6PN in the frequency domain; this paper gives 10PM-10PN for all multipoles up to l=22 and 10PM-20PN for the quadrupole.
- Handles the angular-momentum loss carefully: it restores the constant incoming strain and argues that the resulting shear-memory boundary term is the full static loss at leading self-force order. Leaving this out is a common way to get the dissipative angle wrong.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- First order in the mass ratio on a geodesic source only; no conservative self-force.
- The regulator that makes the Fourier transforms finite is a prescription here; its derivation is promised in a future paper.
- Non-spinning black hole only, as far as the accessible text shows.
- Truncated weak-field/low-velocity series; no strong-field accuracy statement.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

**In network:** 🚩 Chris Kavanagh (extended-network collaborator), 🚩 Julio Parra-Martinez (notable author)

📖 **Where to start.** Supplemental Material, 'Expansion of Lauricella Function around integer arguments' (the core technique: integration-by-parts reduction to four masters, residue matrices, harmonic-polylogarithm solution), and 'Asymptotic fluxes and dissipative scattering angle' (the static angular-momentum loss via memory). In the main text, the part that defines the regulator and Eq. GF_main, and any comparison with Geralico's 5PM-7PN time-domain results and with numerical self-force data.

#### 💫 EMRIs in Kerr Spacetime: The Role of Inclination Diffusion

Alush, Stone, Hughes — [arXiv:2610.18964](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18964) — astro-ph.GA, astro-ph.HE, gr-qc

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 5/10

> 💡 Simulations that let random stellar encounters keep tilting a compact object's orbit around a spinning giant black hole find that rapid spin roughly doubles the rate of slow gravitational-wave inspirals relative to direct plunges, and that these inspirals end up strongly biased toward orbits that co-rotate with the black hole.

🔍 **What it does.** The authors write diffusion coefficients for energy, the axial angular momentum and the Carter constant from two-body scattering. They then average them over Kerr geodesics and run orbit-averaged Langevin Monte Carlo simulations, with gravitational-wave losses taken from the Gair-Glampedakis numerical kludge. They compute the fraction of captures that become inspirals rather than plunges, plus differential rates and inclination and eccentricity distributions, for 10^4 and 10^6 solar-mass black holes and 1.4, 10 and 50 solar-mass compact objects. For a 10^6 solar-mass black hole and 10 solar-mass objects, the integrated rate goes from 7.2e-9 per year (Keplerian orbits) to 1.3e-8 (Schwarzschild geodesics) to 2.8e-8 (spin 0.9), and saturates near spin 0.99. Over all semimajor axes, the inclination distribution is close to the earlier fixed-inclination prediction. Letting inclination evolve mainly suppresses retrograde inspirals that form at large semimajor axes (about a factor of 5) and erases an initially anisotropic distribution of orbit orientations.

✅ **Strengths**

- To my knowledge, the first loss-cone Monte Carlo for inspirals around spinning black holes in which inclination evolves under scattering instead of being frozen. That makes it a real, if incremental, step past Amaro-Seoane et al. 2013 and the 2026 fixed-inclination follow-ups.
- Honest check of the gravitational-wave flux model: Gair-Glampedakis kludge fluxes are compared with Teukolsky fluxes from the Hughes code. Errors are under 10% at pericentre at or beyond 10 gravitational radii, about 50% at 3, and about a factor of 2 at 2. The paper gives power-law correction fits.
- Sensible internal checks: the 3D code at spin 0.001 reproduces the 2D geodesic results, and the diffusion coefficients reduce to the standard 2D ones in the non-spinning limit.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- The diffusion coefficients are Newtonian; 'Kerr' enters only through the plunge boundary and the averaging along geodesics, which the authors say barely matters.
- The live-versus-fixed-inclination comparison uses an analytic rescaling, not the same code with inclination frozen; summed over all orbits, the inclination distribution matches the old fixed-inclination prediction.
- The spin enhancement in the paper's own table is about 2 and vanishes for 50 solar-mass objects or 10^4 solar-mass black holes; 'about 2.5' is generous.
- The gravitational-wave fluxes come from a kludge that underestimates them by up to a factor of 2 in the strong-field prograde regime that drives the result; the paper's correction fits are not applied.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

**In network:** 🚩 Scott Hughes (extended-network collaborator)

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. 3 (kludge versus Teukolsky flux errors, Fig. 1); Sec. 5, Table 1 and Figs. 8-10 (integrated rates and inclination-eccentricity distributions); Sec. 6.2 (summary and honest list of approximations). Skip Sec. 2.2 unless you need the coefficients: they are Newtonian.

#### 💡 Secular Evolution of Quasiperiodic Eruptions: eRO-QPE2

Zhang, Zhou, Pan — [arXiv:2610.21844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21844) — astro-ph.HE

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 8/10

> 💡 Four years of X-ray monitoring of the repeating flares from the galaxy nucleus eRO-QPE2 show no hidden faint flares between the bright ones and a slowly shrinking flare period, which the authors read as a small body on a nearly circular orbit hitting a tilting gas disk twice per orbit.

🔍 **What it does.** The authors reanalyse all 11 XMM-Newton observations of eRO-QPE2 (August 2020 to December 2024). They find that the eruption peak count rate stays roughly constant while the quiescent flux falls, with deviations from a linear decline that they attribute to disk precession. Stacking all eruptions, they find no secondary flare between consecutive eruptions, with a 95% upper limit of 0.56% on its counts relative to a normal eruption. Combined with a Roche-lobe argument and published star-disk collision simulations, they use this to argue against the one-eruption-per-orbit interpretation. They then fit the flare start times with their existing Kerr EMRI plus precessing-disk timing model, using an osculating (forced) trajectory with a constant period derivative, fitting epochs 1-4 and 4-11 separately. The results are e < 0.026 and e < 0.033 (95%), an orbital period of about 17.4 ks decreasing at dT_obt/dt of about -1.1e-5, and essentially unconstrained black-hole mass, spin and disk parameters.

✅ **Strengths**

- The secondary-flare upper limit (0.56% of the normal eruption counts, from stacking all 11 epochs) is a concrete new empirical number that bears directly on the one versus two eruptions per orbit debate for this source.
- Uses the full public XMM baseline (4.33 yr). The timing likelihood marginalises over unknown cycle counts across observing gaps, and an explicit systematic-timing term absorbs flare-shape variability (measured intrinsic scatter of t_peak - t_start is 171 s).
- Section 3 gives a clear analytic catalogue of which mechanisms produce in-phase versus anti-phase odd/even recurrence-time modulation (apsidal precession, disk precession, light-travel geometry, Lense-Thirring coupled to viewing angle, companion black hole), with order-of-magnitude amplitudes. This is useful intuition for anyone fitting QPE timing.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Mostly an update of the same group's earlier eRO-QPE2 fits (2411.18046, 2605.29382) to all 11 XMM epochs; the stacked secondary-flare limit is the main new result.
- The 0.56% secondary-flare limit is in 0.2-2 keV counts, while the simulation asymmetries it is compared with are bolometric.
- The 'oscillation' in quiescent flux is a deviation of a few points from a linear trend, not a detected period.
- Eccentricity limit e < 0.033 depends on the model attributing most of the early long-short timing contrast to light-travel geometry; mass, spin and disk parameters are unconstrained.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Section 2.2 (secondary-flare stacking and the one-versus-two-eruption argument; the main new content), Section 3 (analytic in-phase versus anti-phase modulation taxonomy, a useful reference), and Section 4.3 (timing posteriors, especially the split into a geometric versus eccentric long-short contrast). Sections 2.1 and 4.2 can be skimmed.

#### 🎵 Towards ultrarelativistic scattering near the light ring: asymptotic divergence of gravitational-wave fluxes

Trestini, Gonzo, Leather — [arXiv:2610.20708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20708) — gr-qc, hep-th

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 5.5/10

> 💡 A semi-analytic calculation pins down exactly how the gravitational-wave power from a small body circling a non-rotating black hole blows up as its orbit approaches the photon sphere, including the next correction beyond the leading logarithmic growth, and checks it against high-precision numerics.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper solves the Regge-Wheeler equation for a massive particle on an unstable circular orbit at radius r0 = 3M + delta M using a parabolic-cylinder WKB matching around the potential peak, keeping the full dependence on m*delta and the complex phases of the in/up solutions. Plugging these into the Teukolsky flux formula (via a Chandrasekhar transformation) gives closed-form large-m mode fluxes, and the infinite harmonic sum is then handled with a Mellin transform, yielding F = [alpha ln(1/delta) + beta]/delta with alpha = 0.0212177 in closed form (same for horizon and infinity) and beta_inf = 0.0009117(6), beta_H = -0.0352830(5) obtained by gluing numerical light-ring modes (m up to 2000) to the analytic tail. A new arbitrary-precision hyperboloidal spectral dataset of total fluxes down to delta = 1.6e-3 (l up to ~16000) agrees with the asymptotic formula at the 0.2-0.7% level, with no fitting to delta > 0 data. A Kerr extension is conjectured from the 1974 Chrzanowski-Misner spin prefactor and checked numerically mode-by-mode to 0.2%.

✅ **Strengths**

- The asymptotic formula is tested out of sample: alpha is analytic, beta comes only from delta = 0 data, and the comparison is against independent delta > 0 total fluxes, so agreement is a genuine check rather than a fit.
- Numerics are unusually strong: multidomain spectral hyperboloidal solver in arbitrary precision, light-ring modes up to m ~ 10^6 with quoted relative errors below 1e-22, total fluxes summed over ~3e5 modes per radius with errors below 1e-16; data released as ancillary files.
- The Mellin-transform treatment of the harmonic sum is clean and makes the origin of the logarithm (the m*delta -> 0 region of the large-m tail) transparent, with an explicit, rapidly converging sum over k and an error bound on truncation.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- The leading logarithmic coefficient was obtained independently a week earlier by Barausse (arXiv:2610.12082); the extra content here is the subleading coefficients, phases, and numerics.
- The subleading coefficients beta are numerical extrapolations, not closed forms.
- The Kerr result is a conjecture checked on a subset of modes, not a derivation.
- Unstable circular orbits at the light ring are a theoretical benchmark; the link to actual scattering energy losses has an uncontrolled remainder.

**In network:** 🚩 David Trestini (extended-network collaborator), 🚩 Benjamin Leather (extended-network collaborator)

📖 **Where to start.** Section 6 (Mellin-transform summation, Table I, comparison with numerics and Fig. 3) is the core; Section 4 for the closed-form large-m mode fluxes and the comparison with the 1970s literature; the Note before the Acknowledgments for the overlap with Barausse; Section 8 for the proposed uses of large-l tails in second-order self-force and near-separatrix mode sums. Section 7 (Kerr) only if interested in spin dependence.

#### 🎵 Tidal Excitation of Relativistic Stars

Miao, Su, Khera, Yang — [arXiv:2610.18475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18475) — gr-qc, astro-ph.HE

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6/10 · 🎯 Relevance 5.5/10

> 💡 The authors split the gravitational-wave response of a non-rotating neutron star hit by an incoming wave into four parts: a prompt echo identical to a black hole's, a reflection off the stellar surface, the star's ringing modes, and a slowly decaying late-time tail, and they check the split against direct numerical simulations.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper treats tidal driving of a non-rotating perfect-fluid star as a scattering problem: a wave comes in from past null infinity and the star scatters it out to future null infinity. It builds the retarded Green function of the matched interior-Schwarzschild exterior and deforms the frequency contour differently in four causal regions. This separates a prompt piece identical to that of a black hole of the same mass, a wave reflected at the stellar surface, a sum over quasinormal modes, and a late-time tail from a branch cut. A complex bilinear form on the full metric-plus-fluid Hamiltonian phase space then projects an exterior source onto each mode and onto the continuum of branch-cut states, and every amplitude obeys a first-order driven equation. Numerical checks cover mode orthogonality (off-diagonal terms at the 1e-11 to 1e-17 level), the match between the bilinear-form norm and the Green-function residue, and agreement with a time-domain evolution for a delta-like source and for a chirp that sweeps through the f-mode. The post-resonance f-mode amplitude agrees with the authors' earlier stationary-phase result.

✅ **Strengths**

- Concrete, well-defined analytic structure: four causal regions with explicit arrival times (direct, surface-reflected, centre-return) and branch-cut and pole contributions written out in closed form.
- Strong numerical consistency checks: bilinear-form orthogonality across f, p1-p3 and w0-w3 modes at 1e-11 or better, and agreement between the bilinear norm and the Green-function residue to machine precision (Table II).
- Independent time-domain validation in all four causal regions with a full interior-plus-exterior metric-fluid evolution in a different set of variables, not just the same equations solved twice.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- The split into direct, mode and tail pieces during driving depends on an arbitrary continuation of the incoming wave; only the total waveform is unique.
- Mode amplitudes are observer-dependent coefficients, not intrinsic stellar oscillator coordinates.
- No orbit, no backreaction, no tidal phase: the only physical output (post-resonance f-mode amplitude) reproduces the authors' 2025 result.
- Single toy polytrope, l = 2, non-rotating, no g-modes.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

**In network:** 🚩 Huan Yang (notable author)

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. II.B.1-2 (stellar reflection coefficient split into prompt and centre-return parts, and the four-region contour strategy); Sec. II.C (bilinear form on the metric-fluid phase space, Table I orthogonality); Sec. III.A.2 (source projections and the residue-norm identity, Table II); Sec. III.B.1 last paragraph (dependence on the choice of continuation, p). Appendix J (Schwarzschild black-hole version) if you care about sourced ringdown. #### 🎵 Cubic Quasinormal Modes in the Black-Hole Ringdown

Khera, Yang — [arXiv:2610.20547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20547) — gr-qc

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6/10 · 🎯 Relevance 6/10

> 💡 The first calculation from Einstein's equations of how three ringing modes of a spinning black hole combine into new third-order tones; when the predictions are added to a simulated merger signal, they appear to carry a large share of the signal shortly after the peak.

🔍 **What it does.** The authors extend black-hole perturbation theory around a rotating (Kerr) black hole to third order. They reconstruct the first- and second-order metric perturbations with Newman-Penrose transport equations in split light-cone gauges, then solve the sourced third-order Teukolsky equation in the frequency domain on a hyperboloidal, compactified grid along complex radial contours. The output is a table of complex coupling coefficients that fix each cubic mode amplitude as a product of three linear parent amplitudes. Applied to one numerical-relativity waveform (SXS:BBH:0305, remnant spin 0.692), the six cubic products of the (2,2) fundamental mode, its first overtone and their conjugates reduce the fit mismatch over some start times without adding free parameters, and they shift the fitted overtone amplitudes. At 3M after the peak, the summed cubic contribution is quoted as about 38% of h22.

✅ **Strengths**

- A real technical step beyond the authors' own second-order Kerr work (Khera, Ma and Yang, Phys. Rev. Lett. 134, 211404). Third order needs the second-order metric reconstructed from the curvature, which is hard. The supplement sets this out explicitly: a hierarchy of Newman-Penrose transport equations, with homogeneous solutions fixed by asymptotic flatness, residual gauge freedom, and boundary data at infinity derived from Psi_4.
- Careful numerics for imposing outgoing boundary conditions. Pairs of reflected complex radial contours keep the conjugated fields on a shared contour, the outgoing condition is imposed in the correct Stokes sector, and the two contour solutions are averaged for purely imaginary frequencies (the m=0 cubic modes).
- The cubic amplitudes have no free parameters. They are fixed by the fitted parent amplitudes and the computed couplings, so the linear-plus-cubic model has the same number of fitted amplitudes as the linear-only model.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- One simulation (SXS:BBH:0305) and 'selected' fit windows; no survey over mass ratio or spin.
- The dominant cubic products sit almost on top of the linear first and second overtones in frequency, so cubic modes and overtones are hard to tell apart in fits.
- The 38% cubic share at 3M comes from cubic terms that partly cancel each other, the same overfitting signature seen in overtone fits; it also means perturbation theory is being pushed to its limit.
- h42 agreement can get worse at early start times when cubic terms are added.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

**In network:** 🚩 Huan Yang (notable author)

📖 **Where to start.** Supplement section 'Newman-Penrose transport equations', for the third-order metric reconstruction scheme and boundary data; 'Complex radial contours and outgoing boundary conditions', for the Stokes-sector treatment; the coupling-coefficient table; and 'Waveform components and cancellation', to judge the 38% claim. In the main Letter, the waveform-fit figures that show amplitude stability against fit start time.

## 🗑️ Papers That Failed Critical Review

*Read in full, then rejected. Scores are the deep reader's, on the same 0-10
scale as the survivors above.*

1. **Nonlinear Perturbations of Relativistic Stars** ([2610.18952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18952), Kwon, Hegade, Venumadhav, Yu et al.) — 🔬 quality 5/10 · 🎯 relevance 3/10

   📣 **Claimed.** A fully time-dependent nonlinear Lagrangian perturbation theory for relativistic perfect-fluid stars. It includes an all-orders formula relating Lagrangian and Eulerian perturbations, second-order sourced master equations in Regge-Wheeler gauge, and, combined with the authors' relativistic overlap-integral formalism, the first relativistic mode-coupling coefficients and nonlinear mode-amplitude equations. A static quadratic Love number computed from the f-mode reproduces the direct calculation to about 4 percent.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The only numerical validation is incomplete. The 4 percent agreement leaves out the constraint-fixing term, which is zero only at Newtonian order. For a 1.4 solar-mass SLy4 star (compactness about 0.17), first post-Newtonian corrections could easily be larger than 4 percent, so the agreement may be partly accidental and does not yet validate the relativistic coupling coefficients.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The test covers only the static limit, one equation of state and two masses, with a single mode. The time-dependent capability, which is the paper's headline, is not exercised at all and is deferred to two future papers.
