## Summary (from abstract)

Proposes a framework that incorporates spin-coupled environmental effects — Magnus and lift forces in addition to standard drag — on a spinning secondary in an EMRI traversing a gaseous environment. Claims to be the first framework specifically including these spin-coupled forces. Argues via Fisher analysis that gas drag breaks the vacuum-projection degeneracy between the secondary's spin magnitude and inclination.

## Why this matters (Vojtěch-side)

- Spinning secondary dynamics in Kerr is core to Vojtěch's program. Adding aerodynamic Magnus/lift forces to MPD-like equations of motion is, in principle, a genuine framework extension rather than parameter sweep.
- The claim that environmental effects **break a fundamental measurement degeneracy** is the kind of structural insight worth verifying — if real, it changes the case for measuring secondary spin with LISA.
- Lisa Drummond has done careful spinning-secondary work; first-pass credibility is good.

## Open questions worth probing on full read

- How are Magnus/lift forces actually written into the equations of motion — perturbation of MPD via fluid pressure, or phenomenological force? A self-consistent derivation vs phenomenology is the key quality discriminator.
- Are the gas drag coefficients taken from hydrodynamic simulations or assumed?
- Fisher-matrix non-degeneracy claims are notoriously fragile when priors and posterior shape are non-Gaussian — does the paper present full Bayesian PE, or only Fisher?
- Is the dephasing signature within LISA sensitivity for realistic gas densities, or only for AGN-disk-like accretion environments?

## Verdict

Should-Read. Likely worth a full read on a less-rushed week; the abstract's claims are punchy enough to warrant detailed scrutiny. Drummond's track record argues for the upside.
