#### 🔮 Analytic solutions for the gravitational spin Hall equations and black hole shadows

Oancea — [arXiv:2610.08428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08428) — gr-qc

🌟 **Must-Read** · 🔬 Quality 7/10 · 🎯 Relevance 8/10

> 💡 By shifting the reference point of a polarized light wave packet in a clever way, its slightly polarization-dependent path around a rotating black hole can be written in closed form as an ordinary light ray plus small explicit corrections, which also gives an explicit formula for how the black hole's dark silhouette differs between left- and right-handed light.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper takes the gravitational spin Hall equations (first-order-in-wavelength, helicity-dependent corrections to null geodesic motion of localized electromagnetic wave packets) in the Carter family of type D metrics and shows that a change of observer (equivalently, of the spin supplementary condition / centroid), followed by a correction to the momentum built from the Killing-Yano tensor and a reparametrization, maps the dynamics exactly onto an auxiliary null geodesic through first order. Solutions are therefore reconstructed from the analytic Carter null geodesics plus explicit first-order displacement, momentum and spin corrections; exactly principal null initial data, where the adapted observer degenerates, are treated separately with a parallel-transported observer. The construction is extended to conformally Carter metrics via an explicit conformal map of momentum, spin and parameter. The same conserved quantities are then used to write closed-form, polarization-dependent shadow boundaries for Kerr at arbitrary spin and observer inclination, reducing to the Grenzebach-Perlick-Laemmerzahl celestial-angle formulas in the geodesic limit.

✅ **Strengths**

- Clean structural result: at first order the spin Hall dynamics are shown to be an observer/centroid shift plus a Killing-Yano momentum correction away from an exact null geodesic, so all of the machinery of analytic Carter geodesics carries over.
- Derivations in the appendices are explicit and checkable: invariance of energy, angular momentum and the hidden constant under the observer change is shown, the curvature identity needed to cancel the force is verified component-wise without imposing field equations, and the parameter factor is given in closed form.
- The degenerate principal-null-direction case, where the adapted observer becomes null, is not swept under the rug but handled with a separate construction, including the subtlety that principal leading momentum does not imply a vanishing hidden constant at first order.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- First order in wavelength only; the shadow correction is of order wavelength over black hole mass and observationally negligible.
- Finite-distance shadow curves depend on the choice of centroid (momentum versus velocity directions differ).
- Method closely parallels the existing hidden-symmetry worldline shift for spinning massive bodies in Kerr; polarization-dependent Kerr shadows were previously computed at slow rotation.
- Only the appendices were read for this report; main-text validation and figures were not checked.

📖 **Where to start.** Main-text derivation of the adapted observer and auxiliary geodesic (not inspected here), then Appendix 'Change of observer and reduction to null geodesics' subsections 'Invariance of the conserved quantities' and 'Transformed equations and reduction to null geodesics' for the core mechanism; 'Principal null initial data' for the degenerate case; 'Extension to conformally Carter metrics'; the Kerr shadow formulas in 'Kerr shadows' if interested in the observational side.

### 🌠 Should-Read

#### 💫 Extreme mass ratio inspirals from the Hills mechanism, The eccentricity distribution of Hills EMRIs in LISA

Sersante, Mancieri, Zavala, Broggi et al. — [arXiv:2610.13294](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13294) — astro-ph.HE

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 5.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 6/10

> 💡 Following stellar black-hole binaries from the star cluster around a Milky-Way-like massive black hole until one member spirals in, the authors find that black holes captured when a binary is torn apart reach the LISA band on much more circular orbits than those delivered by ordinary star-by-star scattering (average eccentricity about 0.1 versus 0.48), but they are about eight times rarer when both channels start with the same total black-hole mass.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper links four existing tools into a single pipeline for extreme mass ratio inspirals made by the Hills mechanism, in which the massive black hole tears apart a stellar-mass black-hole binary and keeps one member. The steps are an orbit-averaged two-dimensional Fokker-Planck model of the nuclear cluster that supplies binaries to the tidal radius, restricted three-body calculations of the break-up, an orbit-averaged Monte Carlo of relaxation plus semi-relativistic gravitational-wave losses for the captured black hole, and FastEMRIWaveforms Teukolsky-flux inspirals (on a non-spinning black hole) down to plunge. For one fiducial setup (a 4 million solar mass black hole and circular, equal-mass 20 solar mass binaries with 0.1 AU separation), Hills inspirals have mean eccentricity 0.097 at a nominal LISA-band entry and 0.035 at plunge. A same-group classical relaxation sample gives 0.48 and 0.23. Hills inspirals form at about 1.6e-8 per year per galaxy, about 12 per cent of the classical rate for equal initial black-hole mass, so how much they change the overall eccentricity distribution depends mainly on the unknown binary fraction.

✅ **Strengths**

- A truly end-to-end chain with each step handing over to the next. The Fokker-Planck flux into the binary tidal sphere sets the encounter-depth distribution, three-body break-ups set the capture orbits, and Monte Carlo relaxation sets where the gravitational-wave inspiral begins. The capture semi-major axis and eccentricity agree with the analytic scalings of Sari, Kobayashi and Rossi.
- The physical explanation is clear and checkable. Hills captures leave relaxation at a mean semi-latus rectum of about 84 gravitational radii, against about 26 for classical EMRIs, so they have longer to circularize. This is shown directly in the semi-latus rectum distributions.
- The final inspiral uses Teukolsky fluxes from FastEMRIWaveforms, with fifth-order post-Newtonian fluxes for eccentricities above about 0.9, instead of Peters-type formulae.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Only one fiducial model: a 4 million solar mass black hole, 20 solar mass equal-mass circular binaries, 0.1 AU separation. Nothing is varied.
- The main qualitative result (more circular orbits, with residual eccentricity from relaxation) was already found by Miller et al. 2005 and Raveh and Perets 2021.
- Binaries are point masses in the cluster model: no hardening, ionization or in-cluster mergers.
- The classical comparison sample comes from a separate calculation with different cuts, which produces an artificial low-eccentricity cutoff.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

**In network:** 🚩 Alberto Sesana (notable author)

📖 **Where to start.** Section 3.1 (eccentricity distributions at plunge and LISA entry, and the semi-latus rectum explanation) and Section 3.2 (rate ratio and the probability of a Hills origin as a function of binary fraction). Section 4.1 and its period-eccentricity figure if you care about quasi-periodic eruptions. Skim Section 2.3 for the relaxation-to-inspiral handoff criterion (gravitational-wave time below 1e-3 of the relaxation time).

#### 💡 Quasi-periodic eruption spectral-timing: EMRIs crossing warped accretion disks

Chakraborty, Mummery, Quataert, Arcodia et al. — [arXiv:2610.10110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10110) — astro-ph.HE, astro-ph.GA, gr-qc

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 8/10

> 💡 Fitting the arrival times of repeating X-ray flares and the steady glow of the surrounding gas disk with one shared model of a star orbiting a spinning black hole and punching through a tilted disk works for two sources, but the sparse data allow several very different orbits equally well.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper builds a joint likelihood that couples an analytic eruption-timing model (Keplerian ellipse per orbit, with apsidal and nodal advance set by exact Kerr geodesic frequencies, crossings of a locally tilted disk plane, Romer and Shapiro delays) to a self-irradiated, color-corrected warped-disk spectral model, sharing black-hole mass, spin and warp geometry. It enumerates timing aliases created by the observing window via the spectral window function, then runs one MCMC sampler per mode. For eRO-QPE2 (32 eruptions over 30 days) it finds six comparably good modes (semi-major axis about 40-200 gravitational radii, mass about 10^5-10^6 solar masses, spin 0.2-0.99), and singles out one on flare-energetics grounds. For GSN 069 (15 eruptions over 160 days) there is a near-continuum of at least 18 modes; the spectrum trims these to 13 and narrows mass and semi-major axis by roughly 35-40 percent, but spin and warp angle stay unconstrained.

✅ **Strengths**

- Unusually honest about degeneracy: it reports six modes for eRO-QPE2 and a near-continuum for GSN 069 rather than a single, overconfident posterior.
- The alias-enumeration method, which uses the window function's sidelobes to predict candidate apsidal periods, is a simple, reusable tool for any sparsely sampled QPE source.
- Physical consistency is enforced across two independent data channels: the same mass, spin and warp set both the crossing geometry and the thermal spectrum, including self-irradiation computed by ray tracing and a check that the warp does not hide the inner disk.
- *…and 3 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- No unique solution in either source: six modes for eRO-QPE2 and 13 or more for GSN 069.
- The model fits only a 30-day window of eRO-QPE2; every mode fails once data from about six months later are added.
- Black-hole spin and warp angle are essentially unconstrained.
- For eRO-QPE2 the best-fit disk is effectively a flat tilted plane (warp radius of 1 gravitational radius or less), not a resolved warp.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. 2.3 (timing model and Kerr-frequency reparametrisation), Sec. 2.5 (alias enumeration via the spectral window), Sec. 3.1 (eRO-QPE2 modes, the failure on XMM1-5, and the dephasing estimate), and Appendix A (derivation of the crossing-time equation). Sec. 2.4 only if interested in the warped-disk SED.

#### 🎵 Gravitational radiation from a photon on the light ring: multipole fluxes and the logarithmic divergence

Barausse — [arXiv:2610.12082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12082) — gr-qc

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 7.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 5.5/10

> 💡 A massless particle circling a black hole on its unstable photon orbit radiates gravitational waves whose power per multipole falls only as one over the multipole number, so the total power diverges logarithmically; this paper derives the coefficient in closed form for any black-hole spin and finds a factor-of-four error in a classic 1973 formula.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper treats a photon on the circular light ring of Schwarzschild and Kerr as a null point source for the Zerilli/Regge-Wheeler and Teukolsky equations and solves the large-l problem by matched asymptotics around the top of the potential barrier (parabolic-cylinder/Weber functions). It shows that the flux per multipole tends to g(a) kappa E^2/(M^2 l) both at infinity and at the horizon, with kappa = 0.063653 written as a rapidly converging sum over |m| = l - j, and a closed-form spin factor g = 27M^2(r0-M)/[r0^2(r0+3M)]. It attributes the slow 1/l falloff to two things: the source sits within a few damping widths of the eikonal quasinormal modes for every l (using an identity, vertical epicyclic frequency equals Lyapunov exponent, for all Kerr equatorial light rings), and the leading energy-density and pressure terms of the source cancel exactly because the momentum is null. Results are checked against direct numerical solutions up to l = 12800 (Schwarzschild) and l = 800 (Kerr), with agreement at the 1e-5 level. Along the way it computes the O(l^-1/2) asymmetry between horizon and infinity fluxes, shows how a massive particle's exponential cutoff emerges, and finds that the odd-parity geodesic-synchrotron formula of Breuer, Ruffini, Tiomno and Vishveshwara (1973) is too small by a factor of four.

✅ **Strengths**

- Very strong validation: a fit of the numerical mean flux over 400 <= l <= 12800 gives 0.0318263 against the analytic 0.0318265; individual kappa_j are reproduced to 1e-3 to 2e-5, and the two separate contributions to the O(l^-1/2) horizon/infinity asymmetry are each checked numerically (1.0389 vs 1.0390). In Kerr, Richardson-extrapolated Teukolsky fluxes agree with the analytic values to 1.5e-5 at five spins.
- Gives a clear physical explanation of the 1/l law, correcting the earlier 'relativistic beaming' story: no exponential suppression because the source is near-resonant with the eikonal quasinormal modes at every l, and an l^2 suppression from the null-source cancellation of T_tt against T_phiphi.
- Clean closed forms in Kerr: the light ring parametrized by r0, the amplitude factor N = 16M^2/r0^6, the exact equality of horizon and infinity fluxes at leading order for all spins, and g(a). It also points out the identity Omega_theta = lambda_L, which makes the barrier parameter eta_j = j + 1/2 independent of spin.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- The 1/l law and kappa of about 0.064 were already known numerically (Barausse et al. 2021). What is new is the closed form and the corrections.
- The spin dependence g(a) already appears in the null limit of Chrzanowski and Misner (1974). Only the overall constant is new.
- This is a matched-asymptotics derivation backed by high-precision numerics, not a rigorous proof.
- Near-extremal prograde spins are not validated numerically: there the asymptotic regime sets in only well beyond l = 800.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. 1 (last two paragraphs list exactly what is claimed as new); Sec. 3.3 (the large-l flux formula, kappa_j, and the l^-1/2 horizon/infinity asymmetry and the 42% explanation); Sec. 4 (Kerr: Omega_theta = lambda_L, amplitude factor N, closed-form g(a), and the comparison with Chrzanowski-Misner); Sec. 5 (the factor-4 odd-parity correction); Sec. 6.1 (massive particles and the origin of the exponential cutoff). Skip Sec. 2 unless you need the source conventions.

#### 🎵 Charge-regular formulation and radiative amplitudes of Kerr-Newman perturbations

Motohashi — [arXiv:2610.10529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10529) — gr-qc, hep-th

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6/10 · 🎯 Relevance 5.5/10

> 💡 Building on last month's exact separation of the coupled gravitational and electromagnetic perturbations of a rotating charged black hole, this paper rewrites the separated equations so they turn smoothly into the familiar uncharged (Teukolsky) equations as the charge goes to zero, and gives explicit formulas for the gravitational and electromagnetic waves and energy each separated mode sends out to infinity.

🔍 **What it does.** Hintz's recent separation of Kerr-Newman gravito-electromagnetic perturbations (arXiv:2609.33661) uses variables in which the angular eigenvalue and several coefficients diverge as Q -> 0. This paper rescales branch by branch to obtain angular and radial first-order systems, and second-order 'deformed Teukolsky' equations for s = +-1, +-2, that reduce exactly to the four Kerr Teukolsky equations at Q = 0. It then builds the inverse map from master variables to the physical Weyl and Maxwell scalars, fixes the horizon and infinity normalizations (taking care of a far zone at r ~ M^2/(omega Q^2) where the Q -> 0 limit is not uniform), and derives the outgoing gravitational and electromagnetic amplitudes and energy fluxes. The headline consequence is that the electromagnetic-to-gravitational power ratio of a single outgoing mode is an exact angular-only quantity, (Q^2/9M^2) N_zeta/N_chi, with a closed-form small-charge limit in terms of the Kerr eigenvalue and <cos theta>. That ratio reproduces the known Reissner-Nordstrom channel mixing exactly.

✅ **Strengths**

- Addresses a real and timely gap: Hintz's separation is not directly usable for flux or scattering calculations because its variables are singular as Q -> 0 and are not tied to normalized physical fields. This paper supplies that dictionary.
- Table I collects every equation needed for use (regular first-order systems, deformed Teukolsky equations, reconstruction, boundary bases, connection-to-amplitude map, power formulas), which makes the paper practical.
- Handles the boundary carefully: it identifies the far zone r ~ M^2/(omega Q^2), where fixed-radius convergence in Q fails, and builds normalized bases that are analytic in Q^2 instead of naively substituting Q = 0.
- *…and 3 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Purely analytic: no Kerr-Newman flux or ratio is computed numerically for a rotating charged hole.
- The charge-regular separated equations overlap with Appendix B of Destounis, Cardoso and Hintz (arXiv:2610.07142); the new part is the physical normalization and flux map.
- Outgoing flux at infinity only, at real nonzero frequency; no horizon absorption, no source terms, no metric reconstruction.
- The only independent validation is the nonrotating Reissner-Nordstrom limit.

📖 **Where to start.** Table I in the Introduction (all usable formulas in one place); Sec. VI.A (the nonuniform far zone, the key subtlety for anyone trying a small-Q expansion); Sec. VII.A-VII.D (Maxwell extraction via the Bianchi identity, flux formulas and the small-charge composition ratio); Sec. VII.E (Reissner-Nordstrom check). Skip Secs. III-IV if you have read Appendix B of arXiv:2610.07142.

1. **Time-Frequency Analysis for LISA: Galactic Foreground Modulation** ([2610.07977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07977), Mathur, Cornish) — 🔬 quality 5/10 · 🎯 relevance 2.5/10

   📣 **Claimed.** The paper claims three new things. (1) A fast forward model that maps an arbitrary parameterized Galactic gravitational-wave luminosity density directly to the time-dependent covariance of all TDI channels, with the Galaxy model separated from the detector response. (2) The finite-arm treatment of Cornish (2002) extended from self-correlation to the full set of self and cross channels to O(f/f*)^2, keeping the complex covariance, plus the first calculation of leading-order unequal-arm corrections to the modulation curves. (3) A Fisher forecast of the Galactic shape information carried by the annual modulation alone.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The finite- and unequal-arm corrections are the main technical addition, yet they are sub-percent in the band where the foreground dominates (it exceeds instrument noise only between about 0.3 and 3.9 mHz). The Fisher forecast uses only the leading-order response, so the paper never shows that the corrections change any inference or bias any parameter.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The model is a smooth continuum density of equal-luminosity emitters with a frequency-independent angular distribution. It ignores realization-to-realization scatter from finite source numbers and the frequency-dependent sky distribution left after bright-source subtraction. Adams & Cornish (2014) found that this scatter is significant, and it is plausibly larger than the 0.2-0.6% effects computed here.

2. **Self-lensing of gravitational waves from binary extreme-mass-ratio inspirals: Extended caustics and paired flashes** ([2610.08399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08399), Huang, Ou, Hou, Guo et al.) — 🔬 quality 4.5/10 · 🎯 relevance 3.5/10

   📣 **Claimed.** A geometric-optics framework (point-to-point Kerr lens map plus bilocal geodesic operators / Jacobi map) for the strain magnification of gravitational waves from a source orbiting a Kerr black hole; the identification that orbits crossing extended Kerr caustics produce paired amplification episodes ('flashes') per outer orbit; and an analytic estimate of their arrival-time separation, Delta t ~ a^2 r_s^{3/2} for the secondary caustic and edge-on observers.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The central observable, the flash amplitude, is never computed: the geometric-optics magnification diverges at the fold, and the wave-optics and finite-source regularisations are given only as scaling laws with unknown coefficients. A flash with no predicted height is not yet a signature.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The headline timing law is essentially orbital kinematics: the asymptotic azimuthal width of the caustic tube (taken from Bozza 2008) divided by the Keplerian angular velocity. The propagation-delay contribution is computed numerically but no asymptotic law is derived, and Table I omits it altogether.

3. **Can LISA See Inside the Little Red Dots?** ([2610.08964](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08964), Gómez, Forteza, Palenzuela, Perna) — 🔬 quality 4.5/10 · 🎯 relevance 4/10

   📣 **Claimed.** A first assessment of whether LISA extreme-mass-ratio inspirals can discriminate between competing models of the gas surrounding the black holes in Little Red Dots, applied to the observed JWST and low-redshift SDSS/DESI LRD samples, with LISA event-rate estimates for the LRD population to z = 6.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** Methodologically this is a known toolkit (Newtonian circular inspiral, quadrupole flux, Ostriker dynamical friction, Bondi-Hoyle accretion, stationary-phase mismatch; cf. Barausse et al. 2014, Kocsis et al. 2011, Speri et al. 2023) applied to new density profiles; there is no new modelling of the environment-inspiral interaction.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The headline that 'dense interiors are measurable in essentially every detectable event' rests on the free-fall cavity, which the authors themselves describe as not self-consistent (it would empty within decades and is treated as at rest, overestimating drag), and on a radiative envelope model that only applies to virial-mass (1e7 solar-mass) holes, i.e. outside where LISA is sensitive.
