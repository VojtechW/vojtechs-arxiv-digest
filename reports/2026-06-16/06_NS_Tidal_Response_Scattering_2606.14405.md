# 〰️ Dynamical Tidal Response of NS via Scattering Amplitudes

**arXiv:** [2606.14405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14405)
**Title:** *Dynamical tidal response of neutron stars via scattering amplitudes*
**Authors:** M.V.S. Saketh, Suprovo Ghosh, Nils Andersson
**Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc
**Verdict:** Should-Read — Quality 7.5/10, Relevance 8/10

---

## One-line summary

Defines the frequency-dependent neutron-star tidal response F(ω) (equivalently k₂(ω)) *gauge-invariantly* by matching the gravitational Raman/Compton scattering amplitude in worldline EFT to MST-based stellar perturbation theory, capturing f- and g-mode resonances including gravitational radiation damping.

## Strengths

- **Cleanly addresses worldline-EFT/UV matching.** Bypasses the high-loop "far-zone" diagrams that plague Ivanov–Li–Parra-Martínez–Zhou by subtracting the BH scattering phase and isolating ΔNS − Re ΔBH, with a controlled unitary resummation in M ω (Eqs. 49, 50, 55, 56).
- **Resulting Eq. (60) is fully analytic in F(ω)** and connects directly to the worldline action (Eq. 25). Drop-in usable for PN Hamiltonian construction à la Mandal–Mastrolia–Silva–Patil–Steinhoff.
- **Genuine improvement over near-zone matching.** Low-frequency static limit recovered with negligible systematic; f-mode imaginary part (GW damping) reproduced to ≤ 0.002% (Table 1), vs ~10% in Lindblom–Mendell–Ipser-style near-zone schemes.
- **g-modes via np-eμ frozen-composition sound speed (β-equilibrium)** — standard NS-physics best practice. Andersson's expertise is visible in the careful Detweiler–Lindblom interior treatment and the Krüger et al. low-frequency variable switch.

## Weaknesses

- **The "linear in M" diagram chain (Fig. 5) is *guessed* by unitarity rather than computed:** "we leave a proper analysis of this for the future and instead 'guess' a form." Pragmatic but not rigorous.
- **Goes to (Mω)² ≈ 3PN beyond leading tidal — but no further.** Does not push to (Mω)² loop/log renormalization. Falls short of the 4PN-equivalent tidal program of Jakobsen–Mogull–Plefka–Sauer or Caron-Huot–Correia–Isabella–Solon O(G⁷).
- **Dissipation handled only through GW radiation damping.** Bulk viscosity and Urca dissipation in the interior are explicitly deferred — yet that is the physics most relevant for distinguishing exotic-matter signatures. Andersson/Pnigouras worldline-EFT setups with explicit dissipative modes are not superseded here.
- **No spin (acknowledged); no waveform-level validation.** Authors note no PN Hamiltonian or waveform comparison has been done.
- **Numerical proof-of-concept on a single equation of state (BSk22), three stellar models** — narrow.
- Minor sloppiness ("MSS" vs Mano–Sasaki–Suzuki, footnotes about typos in cited references, a "BETA" tag at the end of the HTML).

## Relevance to Vojtěch's research

- **Strong relevance to scattering-amplitudes-for-binary-dynamics agenda.** Provides the cleanest current bridge between Bern/Ivanov/Parra-Martínez/Zhou Raman-amplitude technology and *matter* compact objects. The trick of subtracting Re ΔBH to dodge multi-loop far-zone divergences (Eqs. 41–45) is a transferable technical maneuver.
- **High relevance for EFT-of-compact-objects.** Shows how a frequency-dependent worldline response function can be defined as an *observable* (S-matrix element), not a coordinate-dependent ratio of perturbation modes — a definition that ports into binary Hamiltonians cleanly.
- **Lower direct relevance for dissipative dynamics.** Viscous channels are explicitly deferred; only GW back-reaction damping is captured.
- **Not yet a plug-in 3PN/4PN waveform input** ready for EOB.
