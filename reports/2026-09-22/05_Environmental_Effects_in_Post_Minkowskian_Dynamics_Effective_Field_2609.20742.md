# ☁️ Environmental Effects in Post-Minkowskian Dynamics: Effective Field Theory, Feynman Rules, and Ward Identities for Compact Objects in Relativistic Fluids

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 7/10 · 🎯 Relevance 5/10

📎 **Citation:** Zvi Bern, Samuel Degen, Enrico Herrmann, Mikhail P. Solon, Anna M. Wolz, *Environmental Effects in Post-Minkowskian Dynamics: Effective Field Theory, Feynman Rules, and Ward Identities for Compact Objects in Relativistic Fluids*, arXiv:[2609.20742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20742) [hep-th, gr-qc, hep-ph], submitted 17 Sep 2026.

> 💡 A new set of particle-physics-style calculation rules is built for a compact object moving through a relativistic gas, and as its first test it reproduces the known drag force that such an object feels.

## 🔍 Executive Summary

The authors couple the symmetry-based effective theory of a perfect relativistic fluid to weak-field gravity and to a point mass represented by a massive scalar, keeping the fluid's sound modes (phonons) as explicit quantum fields rather than integrating the medium out. They derive the full set of propagators and interaction vertices up to four fields, including the nonlinear graviton-phonon couplings, and verify them with generalized on-shell Ward identities that relate a longitudinally contracted graviton amplitude to the same amplitude with the graviton replaced by a phonon -- a gravitational analogue of Goldstone equivalence in a spontaneously broken gauge theory. They also establish a selection rule that on-shell transverse-traceless gravitons do not mix into phonons, and compute tree-level one- and two-phonon emission amplitudes. As the sole physics application, an energy-weighted unitarity cut in the Kosower-Maybee-O'Connell formalism reproduces the coefficient of the Coulomb logarithm in the known leading-order relativistic dynamical-friction force for supersonic rectilinear motion in an inviscid fluid.

## 📣 Claimed Contribution

The first formulation in which gravitons, phonons and the compact object all appear as fields with local vertices and standard propagators, so that the nonlinear response of the medium is generated by Feynman diagrams and higher orders can be built with generalized unitarity and multiloop integration -- concretely, Feynman rules through four points, generalized on-shell Ward identities verified at three and four points, a transverse-traceless selection rule, one- and two-phonon emission amplitudes, and recovery of relativistic dynamical friction from a tree amplitude.

## ✅ Strengths

- The technical deliverable is real and reusable: complete propagators and three- and four-point vertices for the coupled graviton-phonon-perturber system, released as ancillary files, not just a schematic construction.
- The generalized Ward identity is a genuine structural result, not a formality -- it identifies the subluminal longitudinal graviton mode of the fluid background with the phonon, and it provides an independent check on a Lagrangian that contains Levi-Civita structures where errors are easy to make.
- The selection rule that on-shell transverse-traceless gravitons do not mix with phonons kills whole classes of diagrams and materially simplifies the leading-order calculation; combined with the phase-space argument (graviton cuts would need v > 1), it makes the single-phonon cut the unique leading contribution.
- Unusually honest scoping: Section 2.2.4 builds an explicit thin-disk extreme-mass-ratio benchmark, states that the validity window spans only about three decades, that the Coulomb logarithm is therefore about 8, and that for a 60-degree inclined orbit the orbit-averaged force is roughly a hundred times smaller than the quoted instantaneous formula. Most formalism papers would have omitted all of this.
- The point that the differential force spectrum dF/dK is cutoff-independent because the nonanalytic 1/K cannot come from any local operator is a nice, genuinely effective-field-theoretic observation, and it gives a way to extract the physical coefficient without short-distance matching.
- The Note Added is candid about two concurrent papers and states plainly what those do that this one does not (dissipation, short-distance matching).

## ⚠️ Weaknesses

- There is no new physics result. The single physical output is the coefficient of the logarithm in Barausse's 2007 leading-order relativistic dynamical friction -- and only that coefficient; the order-one term inside the logarithm is left unfixed. A large formal apparatus is erected and then used to re-derive a nineteen-year-old answer.
- That one check is nearly blind to the new machinery. The authors note that the leading force does not depend on the sound speed at all beyond requiring supersonic motion, even though the phonon-graviton mixing vertex used to derive it does depend on it. So the check probes very little of the fluid-specific vertex structure; the four-point Ward identities are internal consistency, not physical validation.
- The advertised route to higher post-Minkowskian orders is blocked at the very next step by physics the paper does not supply: higher orders are power-law divergent and need matching functions that, as the conclusions concede, exist only for pressureless collisionless matter, not for a fluid.
- The applicable regime is narrow and far from an actual extreme-mass-ratio inspiral: homogeneous, static, weakly self-gravitating, inviscid, non-dissipative fluid in flat space, rectilinear supersonic motion, with amplitudes tabulated under the eikonal assumption p.k_i = 0. Generic orbital motion is admitted to be nontrivial because of long-time effects; curved and rotating backgrounds are future work. The physically interesting target -- a body on a bound orbit in a differentially rotating disk around a Kerr black hole -- is not reached.
- The field became crowded in the same month. Two companion papers by Modrekiladze, Rothstein and Wilson-Gerow, plus a further Wilson-Gerow paper on renormalized dynamical friction in black-hole spacetimes, cover much overlapping ground and go further on dissipation, renormalization and matching. The differentiator here is a basis choice (explicit phonon field) rather than new physics, and the paper's own conclusions lean on the competing work for the renormalization it cannot do itself.
- The perfect-fluid idealization sits uncomfortably with the astrophysical benchmark used to motivate it: the disk is radiation-pressure dominated and turbulent, and the turbulence is dismissed in a footnote as an order-one uncertainty in the infrared cutoff. That is probably defensible at leading logarithmic accuracy but will not survive the precision the framework is being built to deliver.

## 🤔 Skeptic's Cross-Examination

The strongest objection is that the paper validates its machinery on the one observable that cannot test it. The leading-order dynamical friction is independent of the sound speed, is fixed by the perturber's mass and velocity and the fluid enthalpy, and was obtained by Barausse in 2007 from linear perturbation theory in a few pages. If the point of Feynman rules, unitarity cuts and Ward identities is to capture the medium's nonlinear response, then the first result to publish should have been a nonlinear one -- a two-phonon contribution, or a second-order-in-G correction. The two-phonon amplitude is in fact computed and then not used for anything. The conclusions explain why: the next order is power-law divergent and needs fluid matching functions that do not exist. So the paper is a promise whose first instalment is a known answer, and whose second instalment depends on an unsolved problem in a different paper.

## 🆕 Novelty in Context

The novelty claim is carefully worded and, as worded, survives: prior field-theoretic treatments of gravitational drag in a medium either work through the medium's stress-tensor correlator with the medium integrated out (Katz et al. 2019; Modrekiladze 2609.05639), or treat phonons in a nonrelativistic superfluid dark-matter context (Berezhiani et al.), and none provides relativistic graviton-phonon-perturber Feynman rules through four points with verified Ward identities. The generalized Ward identity of Goldstone-equivalence type in a fluid background does appear to be new in this gravitational setting. But the claim is smaller than the framing suggests. It is a claim about calculational infrastructure, not about the physics of environmental effects: every physical number in the paper was already known, and the paper says so. It is also not the only such infrastructure to appear this month -- 2609.01644 (Modrekiladze, Rothstein, Wilson-Gerow) and 2609.05639 (Modrekiladze) predate this posting by weeks and handle dissipative and stochastic channels plus the renormalization-group running of the drag coefficient, which this paper explicitly does not attempt. The authors acknowledge this in a Note Added rather than burying it, which is to their credit, but a reader should understand the contribution as one entry in a simultaneous multi-group effort to put environmental effects into amplitude language, distinguished by keeping the phonon explicit.

## 🎯 Relevance to Your Research

Relevant as background rather than as a tool. Environmental effects on long-lived extreme-mass-ratio inspirals are squarely in your area, and this is the amplitudes community formally staking a claim on that territory, which is worth knowing about for how the environmental-effects literature will be framed over the next few years. But nothing here is usable for a body orbiting a Kerr black hole: the construction expands about a homogeneous fluid in flat space with a weak-field point mass in rectilinear motion, and the authors say that curved backgrounds and generic orbits are open. If you read it, read Section 2.2.4 (the window of scales and the thin-disk benchmark) and Section 6 (the dynamical-friction extraction) -- those are where the physics assumptions are exposed and where you can judge how far from an actual inspiral the framework currently sits. Section 5.1 is worth skimming for the graviton-phonon Ward identity, which is conceptually pretty and is the part most likely to outlive the specific application.

📖 **Where to start:** Section 2.2.4 (window of scales, thin-disk benchmark, honest regime-of-validity discussion); Section 6 (dynamical friction from a phonon-emission unitarity cut, including the cutoff-independent differential spectrum); Section 5.1 (structure of the generalized Ward identity and the identification of the longitudinal graviton with the phonon); the Note Added at the end of the Introduction for the relation to the concurrent Modrekiladze-Rothstein-Wilson-Gerow papers.

## 🚧 Caveats

- No new physics result: the only number computed is the coefficient of the Coulomb logarithm in a dynamical-friction force known since 2007, and the order-one term inside the logarithm is not determined.
- That check barely tests the new vertices -- the leading force turns out to be independent of the sound speed, and the two-phonon amplitude is computed but never used.
- Validity is restricted to a homogeneous, static, inviscid, weakly self-gravitating fluid in flat space with supersonic rectilinear motion; curved backgrounds, bound orbits, viscosity, dissipation and accretion are all future work.
- For the paper's own thin-disk benchmark the effective-field-theory window spans only about three decades, and the orbit-averaged drag is roughly a hundred times smaller than the quoted instantaneous formula.
- Higher orders are power-law divergent and need fluid matching functions that do not currently exist, so the route to post-Minkowskian corrections is not yet open.
- Two closely related papers on effective field theory for environmental effects appeared within weeks of this one and go further on dissipation and renormalization; the distinguishing feature here is keeping the phonon as an explicit field.
