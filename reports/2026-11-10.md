#### 🌀 Kerr metric perturbations in Lorenz gauge via numerical relativity methods

Nishimura, Vu, Gispert-Fernández, Kelly et al. — [arXiv:2611.05259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05259) — gr-qc

🌟 **Must-Read** · 🔬 Quality 7/10 · 🎯 Relevance 8/10

> 💡 Borrowing the equation solver that numerical relativists use to set up black-hole merger simulations, the authors compute how a small body on a circular orbit perturbs the spacetime of a rotating black hole, up to nearly maximal spin, and match existing results to about one part in a billion as a step toward the higher-accuracy calculations needed for space-based gravitational-wave detectors.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper writes the first-order Lorenz-gauge linearized Einstein equations for a point mass on a circular equatorial Kerr geodesic as ten coupled two-dimensional elliptic equations in (r, theta) for each azimuthal m-mode in the frequency domain. It uses horizon-penetrating coordinates, a piecewise hyperboloidal 'vtu' slicing and compactification to future null infinity, so that every outer boundary condition reduces to regularity, and it handles the particle with an effective-source/worldtube puncture scheme. The system is solved with the SpECTRE elliptic solver: a spectral discontinuous Galerkin discretization, hp adaptive mesh refinement, and GMRES with a multigrid-Schwarz preconditioner. Validation covers exponential decay of the gauge-constraint violation, agreement to about 1e-7 with an independent Teukolsky-reconstruction Lorenz-gauge field (including the residual field inside the worldtube), agreement of the redshift invariant with reference values to below 1e-8 for |a|/M up to 0.998 at r0 = 8M, and agreement of the energy flux at infinity to below 1e-10 at r0 = 10M (below 2e-9 across r0 up to 50M).

✅ **Strengths**

- Validation is unusually thorough and quantitative: gauge-constraint monitoring, a pointwise field comparison with an independent Lorenz-gauge code that also tests the residual field at the particle (relative difference at most 1e-7), and two gauge-invariant benchmarks over the full spin range.
- Near-extremal spins (a/M = +/-0.998) and orbits inside the innermost stable circular orbit are covered, with redshift agreement at the 1e-9 level, which is better than the stated reference uncertainty.
- Formulation choices (horizon-penetrating coordinates, hyperboloidal slicing, factored-out asymptotics) are made with the second-order problem in mind, so all boundary conditions become regularity conditions. The paper says this fixes the near-horizon problems of the finite-difference Schwarzschild version.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Methods paper: reproduces known first-order redshift and flux values, with no new physics and no second-order results yet.
- Lorenz-gauge Kerr metric perturbations for circular equatorial orbits were already computed by Dolan et al. (2023/2024); what is new is the solver, not the quantity.
- Only circular equatorial orbits; eccentric and inclined orbits are deferred.
- High-m convergence depends on hand-tuned domain layout and preconditioner shift, with no systematic prescription yet.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

**In network:** 🚩 Benjamin Leather (extended-network collaborator), 🚩 Barry Wardell (extended-network collaborator)

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. 1 (positioning against Teukolsky and (l,m)-mode routes); Sec. 2.4-2.7 (vtu hyperboloidal slicing, compactification, boundary conditions); Sec. 5.2 (field comparison with the independent Lorenz-gauge calculation); Sec. 5.3 (redshift, including the Berndtson mass/spin correction and the m^-4 tail fit); end of Sec. 5.4 (domain-layout and preconditioner-shift sensitivity); Sec. 6.

#### 🌀 Metric reconstruction in a Bondi-like gauge

Casals, Hollands, Toomani, Zimmerman — [arXiv:2611.06367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06367) — gr-qc

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6/10 · 🎯 Relevance 7/10

> 💡 A practical recipe, with open-source code, turns a recent existence proof into an algorithm that rebuilds the metric disturbance caused by a small body orbiting a spinning black hole in a form that falls off properly far from the source, so radiated energy, momentum and wave content can be read off directly.

🔍 **What it does.** Metric perturbations reconstructed from a Hertz potential in ingoing radiation gauge grow with radius at future null infinity. Hollands and Toomani proved that a residual gauge transformation plus a specific choice of the Hertz potential's undetermined radial integration 'constants' removes this growth; this paper shows the whole completion is generated by one scalar on null infinity (the 'Held Master Scalar'), obtained from the leading 1/r^5 coefficient of the spin+2 Weyl scalar by an angular Teukolsky-Starobinsky inversion, and gives explicit formulas for the four Hertz-potential constants and the gauge vector. In Schwarzschild everything is algebraic and diagonal in (l,m) modes; in Kerr it is tridiagonal in a spherical-harmonic basis, or done by spectral collocation in theta in an m-mode scheme. A proof-of-concept for one circular equatorial orbit (r0=10M, a=0 and a=0.667M) in a first-order puncture scheme shows the growing pieces cancelling to give 1/r fall-off, reproduces the known Detweiler redshift, and shows example Bondi mass, news and angular-momentum-aspect data in Kerr.

✅ **Strengths**

- Clean structural result: the entire completion (four Hertz-potential integration constants plus three residual-gauge-vector components) follows from one spin-weight-2 scalar on null infinity by explicit differential operators; in modes, its coefficients are just the Teukolsky-Starobinsky inversion of the asymptotic Weyl-scalar amplitudes.
- Fully closed-form mode formulas in Schwarzschild (diagonal in l,m); in Kerr the operators are banded/tridiagonal, so the extra cost over a standard radiation-gauge reconstruction is small and needs only the asymptotic Teukolsky amplitudes that production codes already output.
- Independent numerical cross-checks: the m-mode spectral implementation agrees with the closed-form (l,m) reference to about 1e-6 to 1e-7, fitted large-radius slopes are -1.000 to -1.003, and the redshift agrees with a standard radiation-gauge reconstruction (CCK) to within 4.7e-8.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- This is an implementation of the 2024 Hollands-Toomani theorem, not a new existence result.
- Proof of concept only: one circular orbit at r0=10M, one Kerr spin (a=0.667M), l up to 20.
- The redshift check cannot see the new gauge piece; the real test is the large-radius fall-off plots.
- The Kerr Bondi news and aspects are not checked against known fluxes or balance laws.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Sec. 3 (the four-step algorithm and the Held Master Scalar definition, Eqs. for the completion pieces and the gauge vector) and Sec. 4 (closed-form Schwarzschild mode formulas) carry the content. Sec. 5.6 has all the numerical evidence. Sec. 5.7 covers the Kerr (l,m) tridiagonal scheme if you plan to implement. The appendices on generic-orbit punctures can be skipped.

#### ☁️ Negative Dynamical Friction in Dark Matter Spikes around Rotating Black Holes

Xiong, Li, Chen — [arXiv:2611.11380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11380) — gr-qc

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 5.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 7/10

> 💡 Close to a spinning giant black hole, a dense dark matter cloud can push a small orbiting black hole forward and add to its orbital energy, instead of slowing it down as usual.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper extends the relativistic local momentum-exchange model for a small black hole moving through collisionless dark matter (Vicente, Karydas and Bertone 2025) from a non-spinning to a spinning (Kerr) central black hole. The dark matter spike is built by adiabatically growing a Kerr black hole inside a Hernquist (or isochrone) halo, following Ferrer, da Rosa and Will. The paper also solves the linearised Einstein equations spectrally for the spike's own effect on the metric. On stable prograde circular orbits, Kerr capture and binding conditions allow more particles that overtake the companion in azimuth than particles that trail it, and scattering at low relative speed dominates. Above a spin threshold, the net friction therefore adds orbital energy between the innermost stable circular orbit and a few gravitational radii (for spin 0.65, from 3.6M to about 4.9M). For a Milky Way-like halo, mass ratio 1e-5 and a four-year inspiral, the abstract reports about 100 radians of dephasing from friction, plus a redshift correction from the metric response that is comparable in size to, and opposite in sign from, the first-order conservative self-force.

✅ **Strengths**

- The mechanism is isolated cleanly in the Supplemental Material. Pairing opposite azimuthal velocities shows that the sign comes only from the support difference Delta-chi and from the occupation difference, and a constant-occupation benchmark separates the orbital-support effect from the kernel effect (R_1 = 0.61 net loss vs R_W = 2.22 net gain).
- A simple, rigorous sufficient bound (eta < D_0) shows that the energy gain survives pointwise occupation perturbations of up to about 38% at r=4M and 17% at r=4.5M, so the result does not hinge on a fine-tuned distribution function.
- The sign is checked against the scattering cutoff (b_max varied from half to twice the Hill-type value) and against quadrature refinement, with quoted error estimates well below the margins.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Negative friction only operates in a thin band just outside the innermost stable circular orbit (about 3.6M to 4.9M at spin 0.65), and only above a spin threshold.
- The 100-radian dephasing is for the total friction, which is most likely dominated by ordinary drag, and assumes an idealised adiabatically grown dark matter spike.
- The friction is a local Chandrasekhar-style prescription using Schwarzschild cross sections and a chosen impact-parameter cutoff. It does not include a self-consistent wake or feedback from the companion on the spike.
- The concept of 'negative dynamical friction' predates this paper; what is new is how it arises from Kerr orbital support.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Supplemental Material Sec. I (Energy-supply mechanism), which has the paired-velocity argument and the R_1 vs R_W benchmark, and Sec. II (Robustness under occupation perturbations), which has the D_0 margin and the radial extent table. In the main letter, read the inspiral and dephasing figure and the paragraph comparing the redshift correction with the first-order conservative self-force. End Matter C covers the spectral metric-response construction and its boundary conditions (fixed horizon angular velocity).

#### 🕳️ Variational Approach to the Mathisson-Papapetrou-Dixon Equations of Motion for Extended Objects in General Relativity

Scheopner, Lichtner — [arXiv:2611.10794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10794) — gr-qc, hep-th

🌠 **Should-Read** · 🔬 Quality 6.5/10 · 🎯 Relevance 6/10

> 💡 A long, modernised review rebuilds Dixon's exact theory of how a spinning, deformable body moves in curved spacetime from a single action principle, linking the classic equations directly to the effective-field-theory language used in binary calculations.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper reviews the Mathisson-Papapetrou-Dixon theory of extended bodies: momentum and spin are defined as generators of approximate Poincare transformations built from Synge's world function, and the stress-energy and current densities are decomposed into Dixon's reduced multipole moments, which are not constrained by the conservation laws. It goes through the pieces in order: Newtonian gravity, flat-space electromagnetism, linearised gravity, then curved-space electromagnetism and full general relativity. It works with the gauge-invariant pairing of the stress-energy tensor with a metric perturbation throughout, so each reduced moment ends up paired with one 'multipolar Riemann tensor' (Dixon's tensor extension of the metric). The whole system is then packaged into a worldline action with a dynamical mass function, from which the moments follow as derivatives. The authors also give a fixed-point (contraction) argument, with explicit integral bounds, for when the momentum-aligned time slicing and the covariant (Tulczyjew-Dixon) spin supplementary condition can be imposed.

✅ **Strengths**

- Fills a genuine pedagogical gap: Dixon's 1970-74 trilogy is notoriously hard to read, and a self-contained, modern-notation treatment that builds up from Newtonian gravity through flat-space electromagnetism and linearised gravity is useful as a reference.
- Real calculation, not hand-waving: the curved-spacetime gravitational sector is derived explicitly (metric variation of the tangentised metric, Lie-derivative terms that generate force and torque, pairing of the reduced moments with the multipolar Riemann tensors).
- Gives a clean dictionary between Dixon's reduced moments and derivatives of the dynamical mass function with respect to the multipolar Riemann tensors and field-strength extensions, which is exactly what is needed to match effective-field-theory Wilson coefficients to physical multipoles without degeneracies.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- This is a review. The new result (an all-order stress-energy formula in a basis of curvature tensors, one per reduced multipole) also appears in the companion paper arXiv:2611.10783.
- Self-force is not treated. All applications are left to the test-body limit or to other work.
- The 'gap' about when the covariant spin condition can be imposed overlaps older existence and uniqueness work (Beiglbock 1967, Schattner 1979).
- No new physical calculation, waveform or flux: this is formalism and pedagogy only.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Start with 'Overview of the Main Results' in the Introduction and the 'Summary' section near the end (subsections on mass multipole moments, worldline and time-direction specification, and variational results); together they give the whole content compactly. 'Full General Relativity: Final Form of the Action' is where the new all-order pairing of reduced moments with multipolar Riemann tensors is derived. For the spin supplementary condition argument, read 'Selection of the Worldline' and the 'SSC Integral Bounds' appendix. 'Applications and Limitations of the MPD Equations' covers how to close the system with effective-field-theory power counting.

#### ♻️ Next-to-next-to-leading gravitational radiation-reaction corrections to quasi-elliptic and quasi-hyperbolic compact binary orbits

Bini, Blanchet, Faye, Geralico et al. — [arXiv:2611.12093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12093) — gr-qc

🔦 **Worth-Skimming** · 🔬 Quality 6/10 · 🎯 Relevance 5/10

> 💡 Using the recently completed gravitational-wave damping force at two orders beyond leading, the authors work out how that force slowly and rapidly reshapes both bound eccentric orbits and unbound scattering orbits of two compact objects, including the effects of wave echoes and of the recoil kick from the emitted waves.

🔍 **What it does.** The paper feeds the 4.5PN radiation-reaction acceleration in harmonic coordinates (Blanchet, Faye, Seraille, Trestini 2026) into the Lagrange method of variation of constants on top of the 2PN quasi-Keplerian parametrization, for both quasi-elliptic and quasi-hyperbolic orbits. It gives the secular and oscillatory corrections to all orbital elements and to the mean-anomaly and phase variables; most expressions are in the Supplemental Material. Local terms are computed separately in the bound and unbound cases and shown to agree by analytic continuation. The non-local pieces are handled separately: the 4PN tail via Hansen/Bessel Fourier series (elliptic) or a large-eccentricity expansion (hyperbolic), and the 4.5PN recoil term via a multi-scale integration in which a 3.5PN momentum flux produces a 2.5PN-looking, purely oscillatory recoil. The authors show this term is gauge.

✅ **Strengths**

- Completes the NNLO step of a well-defined programme. The input force is fully first-principles and was cross-checked against flux balance and against the post-Minkowskian effective-field-theory result truncated at order G^2.
- Bound and unbound orbits are handled in parallel, and the local results are cross-validated by analytic continuation between them.
- Section 5.2's multi-scale recoil treatment is an actual conceptual point. It removes the secular growing term of the naive Fitchett-style integration, explains why a 3.5PN flux feeds a 2.5PN-order recoil, and shows the leftover constant is pure gauge.
- *…and 2 more in the full report.*

🚧 **Caveats**

- Incremental: the established osculating-element method applied to the authors' own recently derived 4.5PN force.
- Results live almost entirely in the Supplemental Material; the main text shows only leading-order expressions.
- No numerical validation and no estimate of the size of the new effects on gravitational-wave phasing.
- Hyperbolic tail results are only a large-eccentricity expansion; elliptic tail results are infinite Bessel series.
- *…and 1 more in the full report.*

📖 **Where to start.** Introduction (for positioning relative to the 4.5PN force papers and the flux-balance conflict); Section 5.2 (multi-scale recoil and PN order reduction); Section 5.3.1 (Fourier/Hansen treatment of the tail for bound orbits); Conclusions. Skip Section 4 unless you need the explicit formulas, and use the Supplemental Material for the actual results.

1. **Horizon exchange and the universal all-spin response of Kerr** ([2611.05054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05054), Vanhove) — 🔬 quality 6/10 · 🎯 relevance 4/10

   📣 **Claimed.** An exact monodromy 'exchange law' relating the MST near-zone responses of the physical solution and its horizon-exchanged image, valid for integer spin weight at any frequency. Its leading-frequency, all-spin consequences, for every multipole, are the horizon-exchange-odd response, the odd contribution to quadrupolar absorption, and a digamma organisation of the even dynamical response that accounts for the odd- and even-zeta structures seen in Kerr-Compton calculations. The author states explicitly that the exact relation, rather than the individual previously known small-spin coefficients, is the central result.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The main letter text could not be retrieved section by section: the source has no section headings before the Supplement, and the paper was not in local storage. This assessment rests on the abstract and the full Supplement (S1-S8), which contain all of the derivations and checks.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The central 'exact relation' is a 2x2 eigenvector computation that follows from the standard monodromy factorisation M_inf = M_- M_+ (Castro et al.; da Cunha et al.). That is elegant but technically light, and comparable exchange symmetries are already built into the Nekrasov-Shatashvili-function treatment of the same problem.

2. **The Energy-Momentum Tensor of a Compact Object in General Relativity to all Multipole Orders** ([2611.10783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10783), Scheopner, Lichtner) — 🔬 quality 5/10 · 🎯 relevance 5/10

   📣 **Claimed.** A 'novel exact distributional formula' for the energy-momentum tensor of an arbitrary compact object, valid to all multipole orders and written in terms of Dixon's fully reduced multipole moments. It comes with a new named basis of curvature tensors ('multipolar Riemann tensors'), each canonically conjugate to exactly one reduced multipole, which the authors say makes effective-field-theory operators non-degenerate by construction. The formula is said to generalise the quadrupole-order expression of Saketh and Vines (2022) and to improve on eq. 6.36 of Scheopner and Vines (2023), which used non-reduced moments.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** Much of the conceptual content is a reorganisation of Dixon: the reduced moments, their Riemann-like symmetries and the force written via tensor extensions of the metric are all Dixon's (1970-74). Eq. mrtdef is an antisymmetrised Dixon metric extension under a new name.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** There are no worked checks. Within what was read, the paper never explicitly reduces the new formula to the known quadrupole-order energy-momentum tensor or to an octupole example; it only asserts that it generalises Saketh-Vines eq. 2.13.

3. **Deep-Learning-Assisted Bayesian Inference for Extreme-Mass-Ratio Inspirals** ([2611.11922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11922), Song, Xia, Ruan, Chen et al.) — 🔬 quality 4/10 · 🎯 relevance 4/10

   📣 **Claimed.** A complete deep-learning-assisted Bayesian inference pipeline for EMRIs (neural-network localization, then particle swarm optimization, then staged MCMC) that reproduces posteriors from MCMC runs initialized near the truth, without needing the true parameters for initialization, in about 10 hours per six-month signal once the network is trained.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The injected signals contain no noise ('Although the simulated signals contain no added noise...'). Neither the localization nor the sampler is tested on a realistic noise realization, which is exactly where multimodal likelihoods and network miscalibration hurt.

   ⚠️ **Why it fails.** The full pipeline is shown on only two signals. Statements about reliability rest on n=2 with no failure-rate statistics for the swarm-plus-MCMC stage.
