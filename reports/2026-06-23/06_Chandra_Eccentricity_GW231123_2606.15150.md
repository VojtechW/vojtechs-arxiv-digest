# 🎢 Eccentricity in Disguise? Insights from GW231123 and Simulated BBH Mergers

**arXiv:** [2606.15150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15150)
**Authors:** Koustav Chandra, Johann Fernandes, Akshita Mittal, Gregorio Carullo
**Categories:** gr-qc, astro-ph.HE
**Comments:** 13 pages, 9 figures; LIGO DCC P2600297

---

## Summary

The authors inject ~1000 eccentric numerical-relativity waveforms (ICCUB/SXS/RIT catalogs) and recover them with the **quasi-spherical** NRSur7dq4 surrogate, showing that the recovery can mimic near-extremal spin. They then apply full IMR parameter estimation plus a pyRing binary-agnostic ringdown analysis to **GW231123**. The honest punchline: eccentric NR fits to GW231123 are statistically acceptable and favour higher M_f and e_0 ≳ 0.5, but the quasi-spherical NRSur7dq4 still gives the higher maximum likelihood (ln L > 208), so the LVK high-spin interpretation survives.

## What's genuinely new

- A **quantitative map of where eccentricity-vs-spin confusion bites**: only at SNR 21–67 for total mass M_T > 300 M_⊙ is the degeneracy genuinely resolvable.
- Application of a binary-agnostic ringdown framework to GW231123 specifically.

## Strengths

- ~1000 NR injections — a serious statistical effort.
- Honest reporting that the high-spin interpretation still wins on likelihood; this is not a "we overturn LVK" paper.
- The mismatch-vs-eccentricity quantification is the kind of methodology paper that future detector science needs.

## Weaknesses

- The setup is intentionally adversarial (recover eccentric NR with non-eccentric surrogate), so the "confusion" finding is partly tautological — what's novel is the quantitative map of where in (SNR, mass) the confusion is statistically resolvable.
- The interpretation of GW231123 doesn't change; the practical impact is mostly a calibration/awareness contribution.
- No new eccentric IMR model is offered — the paper reinforces the gap rather than closing it.

## Relevance to Vojtěch
