# 💫 Universal Closed Form for Dynamical Love Numbers of Black Holes

**arXiv:** [2606.19281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19281)
**Author:** Mikhail P. Solon (UCLA)
**Categories:** hep-th, gr-qc

---

## Summary

Closed-form, all-orders, all-multipole, all-spin (s = 0, 1, 2) expression for the **scheme-independent dynamical tidal response of a Schwarzschild black hole**. The result is constructed as a leading-log RG solution evaluated on a Newtonian-phase-dressed argument:

log τ = log(R_S / R) + 2[ψ(1 − η) + γ_E],

where the rate is the MST renormalized angular momentum ν^(2), the boundary value is fixed by horizon absorption, and the result factorizes cleanly into horizon × near-zone × far-zone pieces. The Riemann-zeta tower seen earlier in scalar perturbations is reinterpreted as the gravitational analog of a Coulomb phase.

## What's genuinely new

- **Universal closed form** across all multipoles and all spin (s = 0, 1, 2) for the dynamical tidal response, unifying earlier multipole-specific computations.
- **Identification of the rate as MST ν^(2)** in the leading-log RG flow — a structurally satisfying statement linking the MST formalism to the Wilsonian EFT picture.
- **Factorization** horizon × near-zone × far-zone verified against shell EFT to **O(G^15)**.

## Strengths

- Single-author Solon paper — author has consistent track record in this exact area (Love symmetry, PN amplitudes).
- Verification to O(G^15) against an independent shell EFT calculation is a strong cross-check.
- Clean, structurally illuminating reformulation: the "Coulomb phase" reading of the zeta tower in the scalar case generalizes to gravitational perturbations, which clarifies a previously mysterious analytic structure.

## Weaknesses

- **Schwarzschild only.** Kerr extension is left open. The most physically interesting case (rotation-induced dissipative response) is not addressed.
- **Contact terms.** The scheme-dependent contact terms are explicitly excluded; the result is only the scheme-independent piece. For waveform applications, contact-term matching to observation remains the harder problem.
- **No SL(2,R)/Love-symmetry explanation of ν^(2)** — the paper provides the closed form but doesn't yet trace it back to a hidden-symmetry origin. That's open.
- The title is slightly overpromised: "universal" here means universal across (s, ℓ, m) on Schwarzschild, not across spin parameters of the background.
