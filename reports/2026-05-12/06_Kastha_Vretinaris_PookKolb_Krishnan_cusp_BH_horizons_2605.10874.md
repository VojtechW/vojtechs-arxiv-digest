# 🪛 Cusp Formation in Merging Black Hole Horizons

**Authors:** Shilpa Kastha, Stamatis Vretinaris, Daniel Pook-Kolb, Badri Krishnan
**arXiv:** [2605.10874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10874) [gr-qc]
**Date:** 11 May 2026
**Categories:** gr-qc

---

## Summary

Kastha, Vretinaris, Pook-Kolb & Krishnan study how *cusps* form on quasi-local marginally-trapped tubes (MTTs) during binary black-hole head-on collisions and how multipole moments of the dynamical horizon behave at the cusp. The Pook-Kolb–Krishnan line of work (Pook-Kolb, Birnholtz, Krishnan, Schnetter, and others, ~2019-2024) has shown that during a binary merger the separate MTSs of the two pre-merger black holes do not smoothly join into the final remnant horizon; instead, the geometry develops an intermediate cusp structure on the MTT, and the topology change is mediated by that cusp.

This paper takes a head-on, non-spinning binary configuration and performs a detailed numerical analysis of (i) the geometry of cusp formation, (ii) the behavior of the dynamical-horizon mass and the higher mass multipole moments through the cusp, and (iii) a phenomenological model that captures the cusp's effect on these multipoles. The motivation is the longer-term project of using the quasi-local horizon as a complementary probe (vs. the asymptotic GW signal) of the link between initial pre-merger BH parameters and remnant properties — including which quasi-normal modes are excited.

## Strengths

- **Concrete numerical study of a real geometric phenomenon.** Cusp formation on MTTs is now an established feature of merger geometry; this paper adds quantitative multipole-level analysis.
- **Phenomenological model.** A model that captures multipole behavior at the cusp is exactly the kind of thing that can then be tested in less symmetric mergers.
- **Right group.** Pook-Kolb and Krishnan are the people who put cusps-on-MTTs on the map. The work is in their core competence.
- **Connects asymptotic and quasi-local pictures.** The long-term program of using horizons to predict remnant QNM excitation is genuinely interesting, even if not yet realised observationally.

## Weaknesses

- **Head-on, non-spinning only.** This is the toy configuration, far from the equal-mass-but-spinning or generic-eccentric mergers that actually populate astrophysical catalogs. The phenomenological model needs validation on more realistic configurations before it has predictive use.
- **No direct GW-observable bridge.** The paper's program is to connect horizon multipoles to QNM excitation amplitudes, but the abstract does not deliver that connection — it studies horizons in isolation. The downstream comparison to asymptotic ringdown is the bigger prize and is deferred.
- **Quasi-local horizon framework has interpretational ambiguities.** Which choice of foliation, which marginally-trapped surface — these are not unique, and the cusp structure can depend on choices. The paper does not appear to systematically vary these.
- **Incremental within the Pook-Kolb–Krishnan program.** The cusp existence and structure were already established; this paper quantifies multipole behavior at the cusp. Useful, but follow-up rather than breakthrough.
