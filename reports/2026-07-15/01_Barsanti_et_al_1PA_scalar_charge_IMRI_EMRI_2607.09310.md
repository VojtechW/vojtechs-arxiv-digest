# 📜 First post-adiabatic contributions for scalar-charge measurements with IMRI/EMRI

**Citation:** Susanna Barsanti, Ollie Burke, Andrea Maselli, Thomas P. Sotiriou, Andrew Spiers, Niels Warburton, *The significance of first post-adiabatic contributions for scalar charge measurements with intermediate and extreme mass ratio inspirals*, arXiv:[2607.09310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09310) [gr-qc, astro-ph.HE, hep-ph], 10 Jul 2026.

## Executive Summary
First beyond-GR EMRI/IMRI waveform combining a full 1PA gravitational self-force sector (quasi-circular Schwarzschild) with leading-order scalar emission from a scalar-charged secondary, plugged into Bayesian injection–recovery for LISA. The authors quantify how neglecting 1PA in GR biases intrinsic parameters but *not* scalar charge, whereas using pure-GR templates on scalar-charged signals biases everything. A contrarian claim revises downward the detectability of secondary spin at q ≤ 10⁻⁴ compared with earlier Fisher-matrix optimism.

## Key Contributions
- First "1PA-GR + 0PA-scalar" quasi-circular Schwarzschild inspiral pipeline for beyond-GR physics with self-force accuracy.
- Full Bayesian injection–recovery (not just Fisher) mapping how 1PA vs 0PA GR modelling affects scalar-charge inference for both EMRIs and IMRIs.
- Scalar charge d is shown robust to missing 1PA GR corrections across mass ratios, but pure-GR templates fit to scalar-charge signals produce significant parameter bias and *underestimated* uncertainties.
- Secondary spin χ_s and scalar charge d² are effectively uncorrelated in the posterior.
- Contrarian claim: χ_s is **not** measurable for q ≤ 10⁻⁴ even in pure GR — overturning optimistic Fisher-based results.
- Validation that leading-order dipolar PN scalar flux is sufficient — full relativistic scalar fluxes (Spiers et al. 2023) don't matter for quasi-circular, Schwarzschild inspirals.

## Strengths
- Genuinely first-of-kind combination; builds on Wardell–Pound–Warburton 1PA and Spiers–Maselli–Sotiriou 2310.02315, closing a real gap in the beyond-GR EMRI systematics literature.
- Uses actual Bayesian sampling rather than Fisher matrices — corrects a known failure mode (Fisher overestimates measurability of near-degenerate parameters like χ_s).
- Directly refutes over-optimistic Fisher claims about secondary spin measurability.
- Clean logical separation of "which sector needs which order" — actionable guidance for LISA data challenges.

## Weaknesses
- Restricted to **quasi-circular Schwarzschild**. Real astrophysical EMRIs are eccentric and Kerr; the 1PA gravitational sector is only fully known in this slice, so the finding "scalar-charge is robust to 1PA neglect" may not survive spin + eccentricity.
- The scalar sector is still at 0PA (leading dipolar). No self-force in the scalar sector means the "1PA" label is asymmetric between the two sectors; the paper is really a systematics study rather than a full 1PA-beyond-GR calculation.
- Injection–recovery results at IMRI mass ratios (q ~ 10⁻³) stretch the small-mass-ratio expansion; the 1PA waveform may already be inadequate there.
- Secondary-spin conclusion rests on a specific LISA sensitivity setup — a robustness check would help.
