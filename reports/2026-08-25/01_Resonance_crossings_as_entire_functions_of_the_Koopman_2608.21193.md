# 💫 Resonance crossings as entire functions of the Koopman operator

**Citation:** Priscilla Canizares, *Resonance crossings as entire functions of the Koopman operator*, arXiv:[2608.21193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21193) [gr-qc, astro-ph.SR, math.DS], submitted 21 Aug 2026 (v2).

**In one sentence:** A single-author methods paper argues that the long-standing blow-up of averaging schemes when an inspiralling orbit passes through a resonance disappears if you rewrite the averaging step as a well-behaved function of the operator that advances the orbit by one radial cycle, and it checks this on a stirred-up model orbit around a spinning black hole.

## Executive Summary

The paper recasts orbit averaging for extreme mass-ratio inspirals in the language of the Koopman operator: the waveform is treated as a function on phase space, and evolution over one radial period becomes a linear operator U_Lambda whose eigenvalues are exp(i k.Upsilon Lambda). In this language the standard near-identity transformation is the operator (1 - U_Lambda)^{-1}, which is meromorphic and has a pole exactly where an eigenvalue hits unity, i.e. at a resonance; the proposed alternative applies log U_Lambda, evaluated as a finite-window (Magnus) integral, which is entire and therefore finite at a crossing. The first-order coefficient is chi_k = G_k Lambda exp(i delta Lambda/2) sinc(delta Lambda/2), giving the uniform bound |chi_k| <= |G_k| Lambda, saturated at the crossing. The scheme is demonstrated on a Kerr geodesic (a = 0.9M, e = 0.2) driven through the 3:2 resonance by a toy drag force and by a tidal perturbation, recovering the known stationary-phase jump amplitude 2 sqrt(pi) (measured 3.52 +/- 0.09) and its cosine dependence on the resonant phase, plus a planar post-Newtonian off-resonance benchmark and a second-order Magnus coefficient reduced to a physical-space integral.

## Claimed Contribution

That resonance crossings can be handled by choosing an entire rather than a meromorphic function of the one-cycle Koopman operator, so that -- in the author's own summary -- 'no resonance need be located in advance, no second transformation constructed, and no switching criterion invoked', with one generator covering the entire inspiral and a pipeline that 'carries no crossing catalogue and nothing to tune'. Secondary claims: the finite-time Magnus scheme of Blanco (2026) is a special case (first Magnus term) of the operator construction; the structure extends to second order with no detuning in any denominator; and a parity selection rule forbids crossings with odd polar harmonic index for equatorially symmetric driving.

## Strengths

- The operator reframing is genuinely clean and pedagogically valuable: writing the near-identity coefficient as (1 - U_Lambda)^{-1} and the windowed generator as log U_Lambda makes it obvious that the small-denominator problem is a property of the function chosen, not of the perturbative bookkeeping, and gives the exact relation chi_k = w_k (exp(i delta Lambda) - 1) in which the pole cancels analytically.
- The Caveats and Limitations subsection (Sec. 3.5) is unusually honest for a methods paper: it states outright that neither driving is a gravitational self-force, that the comparison with partial averaging is 'structural rather than numerical', that the advantage is 'confined to detunings below the window resolution 2 pi / Lambda', and that the two schemes 'cannot yet be ranked'.
- There is real quantitative work behind the demonstration: fundamental frequencies validated against the sixteen resonant geodesics of Flanagan, Hughes and Ruangsri; the first-order generator matched to Blanco's closed forms to 1e-15 (with an identified typo in a published coefficient, 36 -> 37); the jump amplitude Xi = 3.52 +/- 0.09 against the analytic 2 sqrt(pi) = 3.54, including the predicted zeros at q0 = 3 pi/4 and 7 pi/4.
- The sequence-of-crossings analysis (Sec. 3.4) contains a genuinely nice small result: crossing width scales as |dp/dlambda|^{-1/2} while the gap scales as |dp/dlambda|^{-1}, so resonances separate as the sweep slows, and the Chirikov overlap criterion is comfortably satisfied at realistic rates (22-37 widths of separation).
- The equatorial-parity selection rule (only even k_theta excited by an m-definite, reflection-symmetric driving) is derived, checked against two physically unrelated drivings, and then correctly walked back for the true self-force, where it survives only as a suppression by 22-127 in the published flux tables rather than an exact zero.

## Weaknesses

- The headline numerical claim is close to tautological. 'The finite-window generator attains its analytic bound to better than one part in 1e6' is a numerical verification that sinc(0) = 1. The bound |chi_k| <= |G_k| Lambda follows in one line from |sinc| <= 1 and saturates at delta = 0 by construction; confirming it to 1e-6 tests the quadrature, not the physics.
- There is an unaddressed internal contradiction between the central selling point and the successful demonstration. The paper claims 'nothing to locate' because delta enters only through sinc(delta Lambda/2) at window length Lambda = one radial period. But one radial period does not capture the jump: the crossing lasts sqrt(2 pi / |delta-dot|), i.e. hundreds of cycles, and the paper only recovers Xi by integrating over +/- 2 crossing widths -- a window whose length must be matched to the crossing. And every crossing in Secs. 3 and 3.4 is in fact located, by bisection on delta. The bounded coefficient at Lambda and the correct jump at 2 crossing widths are two different constructions at two different window lengths, and the paper presents them as one method.
- Boundedness of the generator is necessary but nowhere near sufficient. The near-identity divergence is a symptom, not the disease: at a crossing the resonant phase becomes a slow dynamical variable that a fully averaged system simply does not contain. Making the coefficient finite does not by itself make the averaged evolution accurate, and the paper never computes an inspiral trajectory or a waveform phase through a crossing with a quantified error against a resolved reference -- which is precisely what the competing switching scheme of Lynch et al. (2024) does, with a measured O(eps^{4/7}) phase error and a measured two-orders-of-magnitude speed-up.
- No gravitational self-force anywhere. The drivings are an ad hoc modulated drag f_a = -alpha[1 + beta cos 2theta] p_a with alpha = 1e-3, and a tidal perturbation. The sweep rate is prescribed (dp/dlambda = -2e-4), not self-consistent, and is 'derived from a different force than the driving'. The self-force resonant amplitude is not computed at all but read off the nearest corner of a published flux table, without interpolation.
- The off-resonance benchmark is a poor proxy. It is a planar post-Newtonian binary at nu = 1/4 -- an equal-mass system, not an extreme mass ratio -- chosen precisely because it carries no resonance at any order. It yields a modest 1.37-1.42 accuracy gain over orbit averaging on a sampling-matched estimator, and on the more realistic dense-grid waveform estimator the flow map is actually worse than plain orbit averaging unless eps < roughly 2e-6, i.e. for a good fraction of the LISA band.
- The mathematical core (finite-time average of an oscillating exponential gives a sinc, not a pole) is elementary and, by the paper's own account, already present in Blanco (2026) for the first-order dissipative case. Only the operator language, the second-order argument, and the Kerr toy demonstration are added; and the second-order coefficient chi_(2), the one piece of substantive new derivation, is relegated to an appendix and never used in any numerical crossing.
- Writing shows signs of hasty assembly: four stacked \label commands on a single subsection, typos in the conclusions ('obsrvations', 'To benchmarks our approach', 'This division would only be efficient only where'), a missing space in a displayed definition. The rubric's usual correlation between presentation and content density is visible here.
- The remark that the Koopman spectrum 'acquires a continuous part' approaching plunge, so 'there is no phase to define', is asserted without support and is loose: the transition to plunge is a breakdown of the invariant-torus structure of a slowly evolving system, not a spectral statement one can make about a geodesic evolution operator without further work.

## Skeptic's Cross-Examination

The strongest objection is that the paper wins its argument by construction and then declines to fight the fight that matters. Everything the demonstration establishes -- that a finite-window integral of exp(i delta lambda) is bounded by the window length and saturates that bound at delta = 0 -- is a two-line consequence of |sinc| <= 1 and was already used by Blanco. The claim that would justify the paper, that a single uniform generator is cheaper or more accurate than locating crossings and switching to partial averaging, is explicitly deferred to future work, and the one place the method is pushed hard enough to reproduce real resonance physics (the jump amplitude) requires a window matched to the crossing width and a crossing found by bisection, which is exactly the crossing catalogue the abstract promises to abolish.

## Novelty in Context

The literature check confirms the framing but shrinks the claim. The paper's own text concedes that the finite-time Magnus scheme of Blanco (2026) 'is thus a particular case of the operator construction' -- i.e. the concrete computational object, the windowed integral that stays finite at a commensurability, was already published, and the paper's own Sec. 2.2.1 credits Blanco with the observation that these integrals remain finite at resonance. What is added is the operator-theoretic packaging, the all-orders argument, and the Kerr demonstration. Against the principal competitor, Lynch, Witzany, van de Meent and Warburton, 'Fast inspirals and the treatment of orbital resonances' (arXiv:2405.21072, CQG 41, 225002), the positioning is weaker than the abstract suggests: that work delivers self-forced-inspiral-scale trajectories with a measured phase error improved from O(eps^{-1/2}) to O(eps^{4/7}) and a speed-up of at least two orders of magnitude, whereas the present paper explicitly states (Sec. 3.5) that 'the corresponding scaling for the finite window at a crossing has not been measured, so the two schemes cannot yet be ranked' and that the cost-at-matched-accuracy question 'will be studied in a future work'. Related canonical-transformation work in the in-in formalism by Aykroyd, Bourgoin and Le Poncin-Lafitte (arXiv:2409.12204, 2507.18658, 2512.11947) covers the Lie-series side. So the honest statement of novelty is: a reformulation of an already-published finite-window generator in Koopman-operator language, extended to second order, demonstrated on a toy-forced Kerr geodesic -- not a new resonance-crossing capability, and not yet a demonstrated improvement on the existing switching scheme.

## Relevance to Your Research

Directly in your line of work: this paper positions itself against the partial-averaging-plus-switching scheme of Lynch, Witzany, van de Meent and Warburton (2024), of which you are an author, and claims a structural advantage over it. It is worth knowing that this challenge exists and exactly how far it does and does not go. Read Sec. 2.2.1 ('Functions of the one-cycle operator') for the reframing, which is the genuinely worthwhile part and takes ten minutes; Eq. (2.2.2) for the exact relation chi_k = w_k (exp(i delta Lambda) - 1); and then go straight to Sec. 3.5 ('Caveats and limitations'), where the author states plainly that the comparison with your scheme is structural rather than numerical and that the two cannot yet be ranked. Appendix on the reduction of chi_(2) to a physical-space integral is the only substantive new derivation and may be reusable. Skip the boundedness figures; they verify an identity.

**Where to start:** Sec. 2.2.1 (operator reframing, the one durable idea), Eq. (2.2.2) and the surrounding text on the exact chi_k / w_k relation, Sec. 3.4 (the crossing-separation scaling argument), Sec. 3.5 (the caveats, which are the honest summary of the paper's standing), and App. 11 (reduction of the second Magnus coefficient). The boundedness figures and Table I can be skimmed.

## Caveats

- The demonstration uses an ad hoc drag force and a tidal perturbation, never a gravitational self-force; the inspiral sweep rate is prescribed by hand.
- No inspiral trajectory or waveform phase is computed through a crossing with a quantified error against a resolved reference, so the scheme's accuracy scaling at a resonance is unmeasured.
- The comparison with the existing partial-averaging switching scheme is, by the author's own statement, structural rather than numerical; the two 'cannot yet be ranked' and the cost-at-matched-accuracy question is deferred to future work.
- The headline '1 part in 1e6' figure is a numerical confirmation that sinc(0) = 1 -- it validates the quadrature, not a physical prediction.
- The 'no resonance need be located' claim sits uneasily with the paper's own procedure: crossings are found by bisection, and recovering the resonant jump needs a window of two crossing widths rather than the single radial period the bounded coefficient is defined at.
- The off-resonance benchmark is an equal-mass (nu = 1/4) planar post-Newtonian binary with no resonances by symmetry; on a realistic dense-grid waveform estimator the method loses to plain orbit averaging above mass ratio of roughly 2e-6.
- The core finite-window result is credited in the paper itself to Blanco (2026) at first order; the new content is the operator language, the second-order extension, and the Kerr toy demonstration.
