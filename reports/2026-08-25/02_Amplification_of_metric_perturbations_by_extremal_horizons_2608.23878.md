# 🎵 Amplification of metric perturbations by extremal horizons

**Citation:** Samuel E. Gralla, Sepehr Salamat, *Amplification of metric perturbations by extremal horizons*, arXiv:[2608.23878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23878) [gr-qc], submitted 24 Aug 2026.

**In one sentence:** A calculation shows that if matter outside a maximally spinning black hole circulates exactly in step with the horizon, an observer falling in is stretched far more strongly than normal right as they cross, instead of feeling nothing special.

## Executive Summary

The paper studies perturbations of extremal (maximally spinning and/or charged) Kerr-Newman black holes that co-rotate with the horizon, i.e. have frequency exactly equal to the azimuthal number times the horizon angular velocity. For such modes the near-horizon radial behaviour becomes a power law in the distance from the horizon, and for the so-called principal modes the exponent has real part -1/2, which the authors show corresponds to a perturbation that grows towards the horizon rather than being screened. Working with nearly co-rotating modes of detuning k to keep the boundary conditions well posed, they show the retarded Green function grows as k^{-1/2}, so the tidal deformation and the energy kick experienced by an infalling observer formally diverge as exact co-rotation is approached. They then compute numerically, using a spectral eigenvalue method for the coupled gravitational-electromagnetic angular equations, the full spectrum of near-horizon scaling exponents of extremal Kerr-Newman as a function of charge, finding principal (complex) exponents for Q < Q_* = 0.9329 M and infinitely many of them below Q = sqrt(3)/2 M, and use the same exponents to predict late-time Aretakis growth and decay rates on and off the horizon.

## Claimed Contribution

That horizon co-rotating (synchronous) perturbations of extremal black holes are near-horizon self-similar with weights of negative real part -1/2 +/- i*alpha, producing anomalously large (formally infinite) tidal deformation for infalling observers -- an amplification effect that is the mirror image of the black hole Meissner screening and can be viewed as a driven Aretakis instability; the first numerical determination of the coupled gravitoelectromagnetic near-horizon scaling exponent spectrum of the extremal Kerr-Newman spacetime across the full charge range; explicit constructions (resonant eccentric geodesics, a helically symmetric Vlasov distribution, a charged co-rotating orbit for Q/M > sqrt(3)/2) of physical sources that excite these modes; and consequent predictions for the Aretakis decay/growth exponents of Kerr-Newman.

## Strengths

- The central mechanism is stated in a way that can be checked line by line: the co-rotating Teukolsky equation gives power laws x^{h-1+s+im} and x^{-h+s+im}, and for principal modes h = 1/2 + i*delta both branches scale as x^{-1/2+s}, so regularity cannot be used to discard either. The whole conclusion follows from that observation plus the known matched-asymptotic Green function.
- The numerics are substantive rather than decorative: a spectral collocation eigenvalue solve of the coupled Chandrasekhar Kerr-Newman angular system with pole regularity imposed at grid level, validated by convergence tests, by reducing to spin-weighted spheroidal harmonics as Q -> 0, and by an independent construction of the retarded Green matrix whose overlap-region powers are extracted with a Hankel dynamic mode decomposition.
- The paper cross-checks against three independent prior calculations in different limits (the axisymmetric analytic solution of Horowitz-Santos, the Reissner-Nordstrom analytic solution of Porfyriadis, the near-extremal quasinormal mode analysis of Dias-Godazgar-Santos) and translates each into a common invariant-weight language, including an explicit dictionary h = 1/2 + sqrt(lambda_2)/(2(1+a^2)).
- A genuinely pretty structural result: the threshold Q_infinity = sqrt(3)/2 M for infinitely many principal modes coincides with the threshold for the horizon Killing field to become timelike outside the horizon, made precise by h = 1/2 + sqrt(-m^2 lim chi^2/x^2), which explains why the amplification and the existence of co-rotating orbits are controlled by the same number.
- The source-construction problem is taken seriously rather than waved at: since extremal Kerr has no co-rotating timelike geodesics, the authors exhibit a concrete resonant equatorial orbit (e = 0.1, p = 1.68854, Omega_phi + 3*Omega_r = Omega_H) and an exactly helically symmetric collisionless-matter distribution built from action-angle variables.
- Unusually honest self-limitation: the last paragraph of the introduction and the closing of Sec. 7 concede that the sources neglect radiation reaction, are only a local-in-time approximation, and that the linear approximation appears to break down entirely.

## Weaknesses

- The amplitude of the effect is never tied to a source strength. The result is 'epsilon * k^{-1/2}' with epsilon undefined, and for the flagship Kerr example the co-rotating harmonic is the n = 3m one of an e = 0.1 orbit, whose Fourier coefficient is suppressed roughly as e^{|n|} (e^6 ~ 1e-6 for the m = 2 case). Nothing in the paper checks that the k^{-1/2} enhancement beats the intrinsic weakness of the high-order resonant harmonic, so the claimed observability is not quantified at all.
- The divergence is a limit that the paper itself says signals the collapse of the calculation: since the metric perturbation scales the same way as the tidal deformation, 'arbitrarily large deformation just means that linearized theory is breaking down'. The reader has to notice that the headline is therefore a statement about the failure of perturbation theory near exact co-rotation, not an established statement about what an observer physically experiences.
- Exact extremality is essential and never relaxed. For any near-extremal hole of surface gravity kappa the overlap region terminates at x ~ kappa and the enhancement would saturate, but no near-extremal scaling of the effect is derived, which is exactly the estimate needed to know whether anything survives outside the idealisation.
- The Kerr-Newman Aretakis predictions (Re[w] = Re[h], t^{-1} off-horizon decay, v^{3/2} growth) rest on an unverified extrapolation: the paper states outright that the relation between co-rotating conformal weights and late-time decay weights 'has not been checked for the KN spacetime' and appeals to 'underlying mathematical similarity'. These are conjectures presented alongside computed numbers.
- The independent Green-function confirmation for Kerr-Newman is thin: two parameter points (Q/M = 0.5, m = 1 and Q/M = 0.65, m = 2), the psi_{-1} sector only ('we are able to reliably extract powers from just psi_{-1}'), single k = 2e-7, and agreement only to about 1e-3. It confirms the powers of x but does not independently demonstrate the k^{-1/2} amplitude scaling in Kerr-Newman, which is the physical claim.
- The Vlasov source is supported on a delta function in action space at the resonant torus, i.e. an infinitely fine-tuned, measure-zero ensemble that must persist forever; its self-gravity, self-consistency and dissipative drift off resonance are not addressed.
- Kerr-Newman with Q up to 0.93 M is a theorist's laboratory. The paper does not claim astrophysical relevance, but it also does not flag how much of the interesting parameter range (principal modes require Q < Q_*) is physically inaccessible.

## Skeptic's Cross-Examination

Strip away the interpretation and what remains is: at exactly co-rotating frequency the linearised solution has no well-defined regular branch, and the retarded Green function of the detuned problem blows up as the detuning goes to zero. That is a statement that the linear problem is singular in a fine-tuned limit, not a prediction about physics. To turn it into a physical claim one needs (i) a source with a finite, computed co-rotating amplitude, (ii) an estimate of how close to co-rotation a real, radiating, backreacting source can stay and for how long, and (iii) a finite-kappa cutoff. The paper supplies none of the three, and its own strongest example -- an n = 6 harmonic of a mildly eccentric orbit -- is precisely the case where the excitation amplitude is exponentially small. A skeptic would say the k^{-1/2} enhancement and the e^{-|n|} suppression plausibly cancel, and nobody has checked.

## Novelty in Context

The literature check largely supports the paper's positioning but shrinks two of the specific claims. The near-horizon power-law structure of synchronous/co-rotating extremal Kerr modes was already the subject of Richartz, Herdeiro and Berti (1706.01112), and the Kerr conformal weights, the matched-asymptotic Green function used in Sec. 3 (their Eqs. 24, 28, 32, 54 are lifted directly), and the principal/supplementary classification with the 0.74*l threshold all come from Gralla and Zimmerman (1711.00855). Section 3 is therefore reinterpretation of an existing calculation, not a new one; what is new there is the physical reading (tidal deformation and energy kick of an infalling observer, scaling as k^{-1/2}) and the source-excitation constructions. On the Kerr-Newman side, the paper's own text concedes that Q_* = 0.9329 M is 'in agreement with prior calculations' below Eq. (3.41) of Dias, Godazgar, Santos et al. (2109.13949), and that the sqrt(3)/2 large-m threshold 'follows from the WKB analysis' of that same paper. So the critical charges are confirmations, not discoveries; the new numerical content is the full coupled gravitoelectromagnetic exponent spectrum as a function of Q and m, its degeneracy structure and Reissner-Nordstrom branch labelling, and its translation into invariant tensor weights. The framing against Horowitz, Kolanowski, Remmen and Santos (2210.02473, 2303.07358, 2411.07295) -- singular extremal horizons from effective-field-theory corrections or axions -- is fair and is the paper's clearest genuine novelty: obtaining analogous singular behaviour within ordinary Einstein-Maxwell by dropping stationarity/axisymmetry instead of adding new physics. One relevant recent paper is not cited: Horowitz, Kolanowski and Santos, 'Frozen firewall: generic singularity formation on an extremal horizon' (2510.18947), which does the nonlinear evolution question for extremal planar anti-de Sitter holes and finds unbounded horizon curvature growth captured by the linear analysis. That is precisely the gap this paper flags as future work, and its omission makes the concluding discussion look less informed than it should.

## Relevance to Your Research

The technical machinery sits squarely in the reader's territory: Teukolsky Green functions built by matched asymptotic expansions, spin-weighted spheroidal eigenvalue problems, bound Kerr geodesics in action-angle variables, and a resonance condition n*Omega_r + q*Omega_theta + m*(Omega_phi - Omega_H) = 0 that is structurally identical to the transient-resonance conditions studied in extreme-mass-ratio inspiral work, with the horizon frequency replacing the usual commensurability. The Vlasov construction in Appendix A is a neat use of coordinate-time action-angle variables that may be reusable elsewhere. Where the connection stops is astrophysics: exact extremality plus large black hole charge means nothing here transfers to inspiral modelling as it stands, and the interesting open question the paper raises -- how large the amplification actually gets once radiation reaction pushes the orbit off resonance and the hole off extremality -- is exactly the kind of question the reader's own toolbox could answer.

**Where to start:** Section 1 for the framing against the Horowitz et al. singular-extremal-horizon programme; Section 3 for the complete Kerr argument, including Eq. (16) for the overlap-region Green function and the resonant-orbit construction at its end; Section 4.1 (skim the tables, read the Q_* and Q_infinity discussion and Figs. 6-8); Section 5.1 for the honest translation into other people's results; Section 7 for the comparison of driven amplification with the ordinary Aretakis instability, which is the clearest statement of what is new. Appendix A is short and worth reading for the action-angle Vlasov trick. Appendix C can be skipped unless you care about the dynamic-mode-decomposition power extraction.

## Caveats

- The divergence is formal: the paper itself notes the metric perturbation scales like the tidal deformation, so 'infinite deformation' means linear perturbation theory has failed, not that a physical observer is torn apart.
- No amplitude is ever computed. The result is a k^{-1/2} enhancement of an unspecified source strength, and the flagship Kerr example needs a sixth-order eccentric harmonic whose intrinsic weakness is never weighed against the enhancement.
- Exact extremality is required throughout; there is no near-extremal cutoff estimate, and no radiation reaction, so the co-rotating source must be assumed to persist unchanged forever.
- The critical charges 0.9329 M and sqrt(3)/2 M reproduce values already obtained by Dias, Godazgar and Santos (2109.13949); the paper says so, but the abstract reads as if they are new.
- The Kerr-Newman Aretakis decay and growth rates are conjectural extrapolations from Kerr, explicitly unverified, and are presented next to computed numbers.
- The independent Green-function check for Kerr-Newman covers two parameter points in one spin sector at about three-digit accuracy.
