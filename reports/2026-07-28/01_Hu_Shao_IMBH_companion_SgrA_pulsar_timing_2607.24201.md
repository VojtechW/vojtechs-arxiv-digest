# 🕳️ Probing an Intermediate-Mass Black Hole Companion of Sgr A* with Pulsar Timing

**Citation:** Zexin Hu & Lijing Shao, *Probing an Intermediate-Mass Black Hole Companion of Sagittarius A* with Pulsar Timing*, arXiv:[2607.24201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24201) [astro-ph.HE], 27 Jul 2026 (Peking Univ. / KIAA / NAOC).

## Executive Summary
A numerical three-body pulsar-timing model, built from the 1PN ADM Hamiltonian (Newtonian + 1PN + leading spin-orbit + the nonlinear three-body term), showing that a future SKA-discovered pulsar orbiting Sgr A* would be a sharp probe of a hidden intermediate-mass black hole (IMBH) companion. The authors display IMBH-induced timing residuals (Shapiro delay ~1–10 ms with double-period modulation; secular three-body residuals ~150 ms over 5 yr), then Fisher-forecast the IMBH mass precision — while candidly flagging that the idealized Fisher numbers are unphysical — and back this with a Monte-Carlo detectability estimate that yields an m_I exclusion curve occupying parameter space distinct from S-star astrometry and proper-motion bounds.

## Key Contributions
- First application of a full three-body 1PN pulsar-timing model (including the nonlinear 1PN three-body term and spin-orbit coupling) to the detection/exclusion of an IMBH companion of Sgr A*.
- Concrete residual morphology: Shapiro-delay double-period modulation and secular growth that a monolithic-SMBH model cannot absorb.
- A realistic (non-Fisher) detectability map over IMBH orbital phase and orientation, giving a 95% exclusion curve in the IMBH mass–separation plane.
- Complements, rather than duplicates, existing S-star and radio proper-motion constraints on a Sgr A* companion.

## Strengths
- A genuine timing model with numerical orbit integration, not order-of-magnitude arm-waving.
- Unusually honest: repeatedly states the idealized Fisher precision (implying sensitivity to sub-0.1 M_⊙ objects) is unphysical and that the stellar cusp will dominate the real error budget.
- Detectability analysis properly randomizes over the unknown IMBH orbital phase and geometry.
- Squarely in the well-established Galactic-center pulsar-timing program (Wex–Liu–Kramer–Eatough lineage).

## Weaknesses
- The whole premise rests on a suitable close Galactic-center pulsar that does not yet exist; none of the seven known GC pulsars is close enough. Pure SKA-era forecast.
- The crucial background-noise thresholds (10–100 s post-fit residuals) are imported wholesale from a companion paper (Hu 2026), so the headline exclusion curve is only as trustworthy as that reference.
- IMBH-vs-stellar-cusp / individual-S-star degeneracy is acknowledged but not solved — separation rests qualitatively on "double-period modulation," with no Bayesian model-selection treatment.
- 1PN only; 2PN and the ADM→harmonic-gauge transformation are dropped.
