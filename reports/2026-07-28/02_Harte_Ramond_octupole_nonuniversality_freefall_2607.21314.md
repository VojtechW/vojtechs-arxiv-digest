# 🧩 Octupole Moments and the Non-Universality of Free-Fall in General Relativity

**Citation:** Abraham I. Harte & Paul Ramond, *Octupole moments and the non-universality of free-fall in general relativity*, arXiv:[2607.21314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21314) [gr-qc], 23 Jul 2026 (Dublin City Univ. / LPC Caen).

## Executive Summary
Working in Dixon's reduced-multipole / generalized-momentum formalism for extended test bodies (no self-interaction), the authors carry the quadrupolar analyses of Harte (2020) and Harte–Dwyer (2023) up to octupolar order. They prove that in any vacuum spacetime only the *trace-free* octupole affects the motion (reducing 40 → 24 relevant components), and show this trace-irrelevance breaks at hexadecapole order. Two decompositions of the trace-free octupole are given (a 3+1 mass/momentum split and a null-tetrad form). The physical payoff: octupole moments generate torque components that are strictly forbidden at quadrupolar order in Petrov type-D backgrounds (Schwarzschild/Kerr), and momentum moments produce "hidden momentum" in nearly-Newtonian spacetimes.

## Key Contributions
- First octupolar-order treatment of extended-body motion in the Dixon/MPD framework, with an explicit force/torque catalogue.
- Theorem that only the trace-free octupole couples to the dynamics in vacuum, plus identification of the hexadecapole level where this simplification fails.
- Two complementary decompositions (3+1 and null-tetrad with three complex vectors) that make the independent degrees of freedom manifest.
- Demonstration that octupoles in type-D spacetimes produce torques violating the quadrupolar constraint Z_ab N^ab = 0, except where suppressed by Killing symmetries.

## Strengths
- Real, careful tensor derivations (explicit force/torque formulas, a full trace-free-projection derivation in an appendix) — not hand-waving.
- A coherent, honest extension of an established program; the authors flag their own loose ends (a hexadecapole trace exception, an unexplained PN component-count mismatch left for future work, and a Nariai counterexample where octupoles add nothing).
- Connects cleanly to the spin-induced-multipole literature relevant to EMRI secondaries (appendix).

## Weaknesses
- Results are purely kinematic: they enumerate which forces/torques are *possible* under arbitrary moment control; the equations of motion are never solved, so no dynamical or observational prediction follows.
- The multipole series is only asymptotic; physical magnitudes are tiny (hidden momentum is O(1/c²)).
- The suggestive claim that non-radial hidden-momentum effects (∝ 1/r³) could dominate ordinary extended-body forces (∝ 1/r⁴) at large r is intriguing but unquantified.
- Framing ("shape-changing spacecraft," neutron-star EOS) is rhetorical rather than worked out.
