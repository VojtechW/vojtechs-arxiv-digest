# 〰️ Ringdown and echoes from compact objects: Debye series and Debye quasinormal modes

**arxiv:** [2605.15429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15429)
**Authors:** Mohamed Ould El Hadj, Sam R. Dolan
**Categories:** gr-qc, astro-ph.HE
**Collaborator flag:** 🌟 Sam R. Dolan (Tier 2)

## TL;DR
A genuinely clever reorganization of the horizonless-compact-object response in which the Fabry–Pérot-like cavity denominator is expanded as a *Debye series* — successive interior traversals — yielding "Debye QNMs" (D-QNMs) attached to the *individual* propagation channels rather than the resummed system. The standard QNMs are recovered as collective poles after Fabry–Pérot resummation. Applied to constant-density "Schwarzschild stars" at R=6M (neutron-star-like) and R=2.26M (ultracompact, near-Buchdahl), with careful treatment of the sub-threshold branch cut 0<ω<ω_c generated by the interior wavenumber. Convergent at *early* times — unlike the textbook QNM sum.

## Summary
Starting from the frequency-domain Green-function representation for a massless scalar on a static spherically symmetric horizonless body with Schwarzschild exterior, the authors rewrite the response kernel in cavity form and expand the cavity denominator (the global coefficient A_ℓ^(−)) as a geometric Debye series. Each Debye order *p* corresponds to *p* internal round trips. They identify three classes of singularities: (i) curvature-type zeros of c_ℓ^in driving the prompt/ringdown and echo amplitudes; (ii) interface D-QNMs (weakly excited in their windows); (iii) trapped poles clinging to the lips of the sub-threshold cut at 0<ω<ω_c, recovered by a Hadamard finite-part regularization on the upper lip. Numerical reconstructions match the exact waveform for both R/M values, including the *prompt* response — a regime where the ordinary QNM sum is famously divergent.

## Strengths
- The Debye decomposition is genuinely the right organizational principle for echo physics: each Debye order *is* an echo (or a partial trapped-mode build-up), and the formalism makes the optical-length interpretation rigorous instead of hand-wavy.
- The conceptual cleanup of D-QNMs vs. ordinary QNMs — collective resummed resonances vs. per-channel poles — is sharp and useful, and resolves the long-standing puzzle of *why* trapped-mode reconstructions of echoes look so different from the direct interior-traversal picture.
- Careful, non-trivial treatment of the sub-threshold branch cut (principal value at p=1, Hadamard finite part at p≥2) — this is the part most authors hand-wave.
- Convergence at early times is a real, demonstrable advantage over standard QNM-plus-tail reconstructions, where the prompt response is a known weakness.
- The previous Dolan–Ould El Hadj paper [41] developed the Debye S-matrix for these objects; this is the natural and well-motivated time-domain follow-up.

## Weaknesses / Skeptic's Attack
- Yes, this *is* re-importing optics — Debye series are 100+ years old in Mie scattering — but the adaptation is non-trivial (the sub-threshold cut has no straightforward optical analog), and the authors don't oversell novelty.
- The "Schwarzschild star" (Schwarzschild interior, constant density, perfect fluid) is the toy model par excellence. At R=2.26M it's pushed essentially to Buchdahl. Whether the Debye organization survives realistic, non-uniform-density, rotating, dissipative, or *physically motivated* exotic compact objects is left entirely to future work. This is a methodology paper, not a phenomenology paper.
- Only the scalar (s=0) sector is treated. Gravitational perturbations (s=2 RW–Zerilli, never mind Teukolsky for spin) are explicitly relegated to "future work." For GW phenomenology that's the relevant case.
- No connection to actual detectability or LISA/3G template construction. The framework is currently a clarifying lens, not a data-analysis tool.
- The two test radii feel a bit cherry-picked, and an analytic large-p / large-ω asymptotic analysis (which the authors flag as a natural extension via WKB) would have strengthened the paper considerably.

## Relevance to Vojtěch's Research
Adjacent rather than central. The Debye/D-QNM split is most directly relevant to the ringdown/QNM strand of Vojtěch's interests. The methodology is interesting as a complement to standard QNM excitation factor analyses of horizonless objects, and the cut-regularization techniques are reusable in any context where interior wavenumbers introduce square-root branch points (e.g., resonance/trapping analyses for self-force in non-vacuum backgrounds). No direct connection to EMRIs, spinning particles, or self-force; no Kerr.
