# 🎵 Dissipative Tides at First-Post-Newtonian Order in Spinning Compact Binaries

**arXiv:** [2604.21990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21990)
**Authors:** Anand Balivada, Abhishek Hegade K. R., Nicolás Yunes
**Categories:** gr-qc, hep-th
**Quality:** 6.5/10 | **Relevance:** 7/10

---

## Summary

The authors construct the next-to-leading-order (1PN) post-Newtonian description of dissipative *electric-quadrupolar* tides in spinning compact binaries on quasi-circular orbits with spins aligned/anti-aligned with the orbital angular momentum. They write down the centre-of-mass equations of motion, a generalized energy-balance law, and a Fourier-domain phase correction. The headline result: spin-induced tidal dissipation enters the GW phase at **2.5 PN order** with a characteristic **logarithmic frequency dependence**, so it is not degenerate with the constant coalescence phase. In the extreme-mass-ratio limit they recover the known horizon-absorption flux for spinning BHs and identify what they describe as a redshift-related correction that may be missing in some recent worldline-EFT calculations.

## Strengths

- **Honestly states the dependence.** The 2.5 PN entry order with a ln(f) factor is the right answer for spin-induced absorption; the breaking of degeneracy with the coalescence phase is genuinely useful for parameter estimation in the LISA / 3G band.
- **EMRI sanity check.** Reducing to known horizon-absorption fluxes in q→∞ provides a non-trivial internal consistency test, and the agreement is the right kind of cross-validation between the PN tidal formalism and BHPT horizon flux calculations (Tagoshi–Mano–Sasaki–Takasugi for spin-aligned circular Kerr).
- **General linear tidal response.** Working with the most general, low-frequency, rotation-invariant tidal response (rather than fixing to a specific model) makes the result re-usable beyond Kerr — neutron stars, exotic compact objects, etc.
- **Concrete claim about the redshift correction.** Identifying a possibly missing piece in recent WQFT/EFT calculations is the kind of falsifiable, citation-trackable contribution that can be checked against the [Steinhoff–Hinderer–Buonanno–Taracchini 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.01907) and the more recent Plefka-group dissipative-tide work.

## Weaknesses

- **Quasi-circular only.** Eccentricity is a major LISA target for both EMRI and IMRI sources; the absence of an eccentric extension is conspicuous. So is the absence of generic precession.
- **Phase-only forecast.** A 2.5 PN logarithmic phase term is in principle observable, but the paper (from the abstract) does not provide a Fisher-matrix forecast against realistic noise curves and parameter degeneracies. The "not degenerate with the coalescence phase" claim is encouraging but not yet quantitative.
- **Limited discussion of the EFT comparison.** The "redshift correction potentially absent in certain recent worldline-EFT calculations" is the most provocative claim and deserves an explicit table or equation-by-equation comparison; from the abstract one cannot tell whether the discrepancy is at the level of the action, the equations of motion, or the phase.
- **Spin-aligned only.** Generic spin orientations (precessing binaries) are deferred. The whole motivation for studying *spin*-dependent tidal dissipation is that spins are dynamically important; restricting to aligned configurations leaves the most interesting case for follow-up.

## Verdict

**Should-Read.** This is solid, incremental PN work whose value is mostly in the explicit 2.5 PN logarithmic phase term and in the BHPT-limit cross-check. Read primarily to verify the redshift-correction claim against the worldline-EFT literature (Goldberger–Rothstein-style, the Mandal–Mastrolia–Patil–Steinhoff series, recent Plefka WQFT papers).
